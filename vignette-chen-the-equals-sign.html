Vignette Chen The Equals Sign

Vignette

Chen projected the Drake equation onto the screen. N = R* × fp × ne × fl × fi × fc × L.

"What kind of equation is this?"

A student answered immediately. "It estimates the number of communicating civilizations in the galaxy."

"That's what it does. I asked what kind of equation it is."

Silence.

"Compare it to F = ma. What's the difference?"

Another student: "In F equals ma, you can measure all the terms."

"Right. Force, mass, acceleration — independently measurable in a laboratory. The equation constrains. Given any two, the third is determined. Now look at the Drake equation. How many of its seven factors are well-constrained by observation?"

"Two? R-star and fp?"

"Two. Star formation rate and the fraction of stars with planets. Kepler nailed fp — nearly 100 percent of stars have planets. But the other five?" Chen paused. "fl — the fraction of habitable planets where life arises. Estimates range from 1.0 to vanishingly small. fi — the fraction where intelligence evolves. Ernst Mayr pointed out that of approximately fifty billion species in Earth's history, one developed high intelligence. L — the average lifetime of a communicating civilization. Estimated from one hundred years to millions."

She let that settle. "Drake's original estimate: N equals ten. Sagan's: up to one hundred million. Same equation, same factors, ten orders of magnitude apart. The equation constrains nothing."

"So it's useless?" a student asked.

"It's not useless. It's not an equation. It's an agenda. Drake wrote it in late October 1961, preparing for a conference at Green Bank. He needed to organize the discussion. He made a list of questions and arranged them as factors in a product. The equation was a meeting agenda in the typography of physics."

Chen pointed to the equals sign. "This is the concealment. In physics, this sign means: determined. In the Drake equation, it means: organized. The sign is identical. The epistemology is not. You see N equals and you read: there is a calculable answer. There isn't. The format performs the solvability of a question that may not have a calculable answer."

"But it structured an entire field," someone objected.

"Yes. That's the point. The format's power is real. SETI exists because the equation made the question look answerable. Funding, research programs, telescope time — all organized around the seven factors. The format is the contribution and the concealment simultaneously. It made the unknown look like a quantity awaiting better measurement, when the unknown may be a permanent feature of the question."

She advanced to a new slide: Sandberg, Drexler, and Ord, 2018. "They showed that when you replace point estimates with realistic probability distributions and run Monte Carlo simulations, the probability of being alone in the Milky Way ranges from 53 to 99.6 percent. The Fermi paradox — 'Where is everybody?' — dissolves. The paradox was never in the universe. It was in the format. It was in treating guesses as measurements and the product of guesses as a calculation."

Chen turned off the projector. "Jill Tarter called the Drake equation 'a wonderful way to organize our ignorance.' That's precisely right. It organizes ignorance into the format of knowledge. The organization is the contribution. The format is the concealment. They are the same thing."

Chen-Maren fictional universe. The equals sign is the concealment.
