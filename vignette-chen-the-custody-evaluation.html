Vignette Chen The Custody Evaluation

Vignette

Maren said: My sister's evaluation was 87 pages.

Chen set down his pen.

She was trying to protect her daughter. Her ex had been violent — police reports, a restraining order, the ER visit where the nurse documented bruising on Lily's arms. She thought the evaluation would confirm what the records showed.

Chen said: It didn't.

Maren said: The evaluator noted that my sister was "unable to promote a positive relationship between the child and the father." That she displayed "hostility" during the interview when discussing the father. That her allegations of abuse were "not corroborated by the testing." The father's attorney said: alienation. The evaluator agreed it was "consistent with the dynamics."

Chen said: The protection was reclassified.

Maren said: As pathology. The MMPI-2 showed elevated defensiveness. The evaluator wrote: "guarded presentation." Of course she was guarded. Her children's future depended on the answers. Forty-four percent of custody protocols yield no useful data because people respond defensively. The evaluator treated the defensiveness as a finding about my sister rather than a finding about the test.

Chen said: The evaluation cost—

Maren said: Thirty-two thousand dollars. My sister took out a second mortgage. Her ex's company paid his share. She hired the evaluator believing the process would protect her daughter. The process transferred custody to her ex.

Chen said: The research.

Maren said: Over 2,000 court opinions. When fathers claim alienation, courts reject mothers' abuse claims at double the rate. When evaluators are appointed, outcomes for protective mothers worsen further. Parental Alienation Syndrome was rejected by the DSM-5 for lack of scientific evidence. The concept persists under the word "alienation" without the word "syndrome." The name changed. The function didn't.

Chen said: The instrument inverts.

Maren said: Twice. First my sister's behavior: protection becomes evidence of danger. Then Lily's fear: the child is afraid of her father, which the evaluator reads as evidence that my sister turned the child against him. The child's fear of the abuser is the evidence that the mother is alienating. The evaluation transfers the child to the person the child fears.

Chen looked at the paragraph. Thirty-nine lines. Somewhere in those lines was a six-year-old girl whose fear was reclassified as manipulation, whose mother's protection was reclassified as pathology, whose custody was transferred by an 87-page report written in clinical vocabulary by a professional who charged $400 an hour to construct the child's best interest.

Maren said: Lily is twelve now.

Chen waited.

Maren said: She doesn't talk about it. My sister sees her on alternate weekends. The evaluation is in a filing cabinet in a courthouse. Eighty-seven pages. It constituted a family's life and then it was filed away.

Chen did not add this to the paragraph. The paragraph was about measurement. This was about a girl.
