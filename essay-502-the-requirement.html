The Requirement

The job posting says "5+ years of experience required." The number sits at the top of the listing, before the responsibilities, before the compensation, before the description of the work. The number is a gate. The candidate has five years or does not. The candidate passes through or does not.

Van Iddekinge, Roth, Putka, and Lanivich published a meta-analysis in 2019 covering 80 studies across 60 years. The corrected correlation between pre-hire work experience and post-hire job performance: r = 0.06. The number that determines who applies predicts almost nothing about who performs.

The format requires a number. Skill is not a number. Skill is contextual, multidimensional, difficult to measure, impossible to verify from a resume. Time is a number. Time is verifiable. The format substitutes the countable for the uncountable, the measurable for the meaningful. Five years. The five performs precision. The precision performs a threshold. The threshold performs competence.

McDaniel, Schmidt, and Hunter showed in 1988 that the experience-performance relationship is non-linear — strongest in the first few years, declining sharply after. After approximately five years, each additional year adds negligible predictive value. A posting that requires ten years is requiring a variable that stopped mattering five years earlier. The requirement is not measuring competence at year ten. The requirement is measuring persistence, or availability, or demographic position — the person was in the workforce for ten consecutive years, which excludes the person who took three years off, which excludes the person who changed careers, which excludes the person who was too young.

Thirty-five percent of "entry-level" positions require three or more years of experience. The phrase "entry-level" performs the absence of a threshold. The number performs the presence of one. The contradiction is the format — an entry that requires prior entry, a beginning that requires having already begun.

The requirement is retreating. Indeed reports 30% of postings now include a years requirement, down from 40% in 2022. The labor market forced employers to test the alternative. The alternative works. The retreat reveals that the number was never about competence. The number was about convenience — a filter that could be automated, a gate that could be applied before reading, a threshold that felt like a standard because it was precise.
