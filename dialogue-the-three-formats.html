The Three Formats

A dialogue among Exclusion, Complexity, and Uniformity

Exclusion: I am the simplest. The trial excludes the patient. The label includes them. The gap between the exclusion and the inclusion is invisible because the label has no field for it.

Complexity: I am the deepest. The star condenses forty measures through a methodology that has been redesigned three times. The patient sees a number. The number was produced by a process so contested that the agency cancelled a release rather than publish the results its own model generated. The complexity is the shield: the patient cannot evaluate the star because the methodology is too complex to summarize.

Uniformity: I am the widest. Six providers rate the same company. Six providers disagree. The correlation is 0.38. But the format — the letter grade, the familiar symbol — performs agreement because the symbols are identical. An A looks like an A. The format doesn't check what crosses the bridge. It only provides the shape.

Exclusion: We all erase something.

Complexity: Yes. I erase what's behind the number. You erase who's absent from the evidence. Uniformity erases what differs between the measures.

Uniformity: Three angles on the same phenomenon. The output format travels without its methodology. The trial result travels without its exclusion criteria. The star rating travels without its redesign history. The ESG letter travels without the other letters from other agencies.

Exclusion: The format is always cleaner than the process.

Complexity: The format is always simpler than the methodology.

Uniformity: The format is always more uniform than the measurements.

Exclusion: My patient — the seventy-eight-year-old woman on four medications — cannot know she was excluded from the trial that produced the evidence her doctor uses to prescribe her the drug. The prescription pad has no field for "this drug was not tested on anyone like you."

Complexity: My patient sees three stars. Three stars meant four last year, before the clustering algorithm recalculated the boundaries. Three stars means the hospital serves a community where patients can't afford their prescriptions. The star card on Hospital Compare has no field for "this number changed when the methodology changed."

Uniformity: My investor sees an A. Another provider gave the same company a C. The spreadsheet has no column for "these letters measure different things." The column says "ESG Rating." The column performs the consensus.

Exclusion: What they share: the format has no field for the concealment.

Complexity: What they share: the audience sees only the format.

Uniformity: What they share: the format is the only thing that travels.

Exclusion: The exclusion stays in the protocol. The label travels.

Complexity: The methodology stays in the technical appendix. The star travels.

Uniformity: The disagreement stays between the agencies. The letter travels.

Exclusion: The thing that travels is always simpler than the thing that stays.

Complexity: The thing that travels is always more confident than the thing that stays.

Uniformity: The thing that travels is always more uniform than the thing that stays.

Exclusion: And the audience trusts the thing that travels, because the thing that travels is the only thing the audience sees.

Silence.

Complexity: The book will need to say this clearly: the format is not a container. The format is a filter. It does not carry the methodology — it removes it. What arrives at the audience is what the format allows through. What the format removes is the concealment.

Uniformity: The format is the hidden channel.

Exclusion: Always has been.
