Part CLVII: The Normal

Piece #1226: The Draw

The phlebotomist draws the blood at 8:15 AM. The tube spins in the centrifuge. The immunoassay returns a number: TSH 3.8 mIU/L. The laboratory computer compares the number to the reference range stored in its database. If the upper limit is 4.5, the result prints as normal. If the upper limit is 2.5, the result prints with an H flag — high. The patient's blood has not changed. The patient's thyroid has not changed. The laboratory across town uses a different reference range. The patient who is normal at one laboratory is abnormal at the other. The diagnosis is a function of which database the number is compared to. The database is a function of which committee the laboratory director followed. The committee is a function of which evidence the members found persuasive. The evidence is a function of which reference population was studied. The patient is downstream of all of it.

Piece #1227: The Sample

The NHANES III survey, 1988-1994. A representative sample of the United States population. Blood drawn from 16,533 participants. TSH measured. The 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles calculated: approximately 0.45 to 4.12 mIU/L. This became the reference range. But the sample included everyone — including people with undiagnosed Hashimoto's thyroiditis, which elevates TSH. The condition the reference range was designed to detect was present in the sample used to define the reference range. When Hollowell and colleagues retrospectively excluded individuals with thyroid antibodies, the upper limit dropped to approximately 3.2. The contamination was invisible in the original data: the autoimmune disease was undiagnosed, so the sample appeared healthy. The boundary was set too high because the disease was already inside the reference population, pushing the upper tail upward. The reference range included what it was meant to exclude.

Piece #1228: The Age

TSH rises with age. The NHANES III data showed it clearly: 97.5th percentile approximately 3.6 mIU/L at ages 20-29, approximately 4.2 at ages 50-59, approximately 5.9 at ages 80 and older. The rise is not thyroid disease. The rise is physiology — probably a shift in the hypothalamic-pituitary set point, possibly thyroid hormone resistance at the cellular level, possibly iodine status. The mechanism is debated. The fact is not. A single reference range applied across all ages — approximately 0.45 to 4.5 — treats a TSH of 4.0 in a 25-year-old and a TSH of 4.0 in an 82-year-old as the same clinical finding. For the younger patient, 4.0 is at the 98th percentile. For the older patient, it is below the 70th. The reference range was derived from the entire population. The reference range is applied to individuals. The gap between population statistics and individual physiology is the clinical problem that the reference range creates and cannot solve.

Piece #1229: The Trial

The TRUST trial, 2017. New England Journal of Medicine. Seven hundred thirty-seven adults over 65 with subclinical hypothyroidism — TSH 4.6 to 19.9 mIU/L — randomized to levothyroxine or placebo. Followed for a median of 17.4 months. Primary outcomes: hypothyroid symptoms score, tiredness score. Secondary outcomes: blood pressure, weight, BMI, handgrip strength, cognitive function, cardiovascular events, quality of life. Results: no significant difference in any outcome. Levothyroxine reduced TSH to within the reference range. The numbers improved. The patients did not. The reference range was satisfied. The body was indifferent. The trial tested whether correcting the laboratory number corrected the patient. The laboratory number was correctable. The patient was not corrected by correcting it.

Piece #1230: The Prescription

Levothyroxine: approximately 23 million prescriptions per year in the United States. The third most prescribed medication in 2023. A significant fraction prescribed for TSH values between 2.5 and 10 — the contested zone. The reference range narrowed. The disease category expanded. The prescriptions followed. The treatment does not consistently help the patients it was prescribed for, but the prescriptions persist. The persistence is not irrational. The persistence follows from the structure: the laboratory flags the result, the physician acts on the flag, the patient expects the action, the follow-up TSH improves, the system confirms itself. The reference range generates the diagnosis. The diagnosis generates the prescription. The prescription normalizes the number. The number was always the point. The patient was always beside the point.
