The Roll

The package says "6 Double Rolls = 12 Regular Rolls." The consumer reads the equation and calculates value: twelve rolls of paper towels in a package of six. The equation is printed like mathematics — number equals number, the format of equivalence, the grammar of proof. But the "regular roll" in the equation is not a standard. It is the manufacturer's own internal reference — a quantity defined by the company making the claim.

Bounty's regular roll contains approximately 40 sheets. Brawny's contains approximately 60. The "double roll" of one brand may contain fewer sheets than the "regular roll" of another. The equation "6 Double Rolls = 12 Regular Rolls" means these six rolls contain twice as many sheets as our regular rolls. Not twice as many as their regular rolls. Not twice as many as any external standard. Twice as many as the baseline the manufacturer chose.

The language has escalated. Double rolls begat Big rolls. Big rolls begat Huge rolls. Huge rolls begat Mega rolls. Mega rolls begat Super Mega rolls. Each tier is defined as a multiple of the manufacturer's regular roll — the roll that no longer appears on store shelves because every roll is now a multiple of itself. The regular roll is the ghost in the equation. It exists only as the denominator. The consumer has never held a regular roll. The consumer has always held a multiple.

The sheet compounds the problem. Select-a-size sheets — half the height of a standard sheet — double the sheet count without doubling the paper. A roll with 150 select-a-size sheets may contain less total paper area than a roll with 80 full-size sheets. The consumer comparing sheet counts across brands is comparing different-sized sheets — the way comparing thread counts across fabrics compares different-ply threads. The unit of comparison — the sheet — is not a fixed unit.

What makes this instance satisfying: the concealment is in the equals sign. The format of the claim — "6 = 12" — performs mathematical certainty. Mathematics requires that both sides of the equation use the same units. But the unit — the "regular roll" — is defined by the manufacturer making the claim. The equation is internally consistent and externally meaningless. Six double rolls equal twelve of our regular rolls. The "our" is silent. The equation speaks for itself, and what it says is true, and what it means depends on the referent the consumer cannot see and has never been sold.
