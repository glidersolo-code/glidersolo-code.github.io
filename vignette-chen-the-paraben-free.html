Vignette Chen The Paraben Free

Vignette

"Paraben-free," Maren said, reading the lotion. "I switched everything to paraben-free last year."

"Why?" Chen asked.

"Because parabens are... they cause cancer? Or something with estrogen?"

"One study. 2004. Darbre and colleagues. Detected parabens in twenty breast tumor samples. No healthy tissue controls. No causal mechanism. No dose-response relationship. Detection, not causation."

"But everyone says—"

"The Cosmetic Ingredient Review Expert Panel has reviewed parabens four times. 1984, 2008, 2012, 2020. Each time: safe as used. The FDA: no evidence of harm at cosmetic concentrations. The EU Scientific Committee: safe at established levels. The estrogenic potency of the weakest paraben is one hundred thousand times lower than estradiol — the estrogen the body produces on its own."

Maren looked at the bottle. "Then why is everyone removing them?"

"Because the label creates the fear it promises to resolve. 'Paraben-free' performs two things simultaneously. It says: this product is safe, because it lacks parabens. And it says: parabens are dangerous, because this product advertises their absence. The consumer's inference is reasonable — manufacturers don't advertise the absence of safe things. The inference is reasonable. The premise is false."

"What replaced the parabens?"

"Methylisothiazolinone. MIT. Contact Allergen of the Year, 2013. Epidemic contact dermatitis traced to MIT in 'paraben-free' products. The consumer chose 'paraben-free' to protect her skin. The replacement gave her a rash."

"The cure caused the disease."

"The label named the departure. The arrival went unnamed. The 'free from' format is an indictment — it accuses the excluded ingredient of a harm the science doesn't support, and the consumer pays more for the acquittal."

Maren turned the bottle over. The ingredient list was in six-point type on the back. The "paraben-free" claim was in fourteen-point type on the front. "The absence is louder than the presence."

"The absence is always louder. That's how the format works."
