The Roadside

Chen is reading the NHTSA manual when Maren comes in with two shoes — one flat, one heeled.

"Walk a line in these," Maren says, holding up the heeled shoe.

Chen doesn't take it. "That's the point. The test doesn't control for footwear. Or road surface. Or age. Or inner ear conditions. Or anxiety. Or cold. Or fatigue. It tests the ability to perform a difficult physical task under stressful conditions and interprets the difficulty as alcohol impairment."

"And the accuracy?"

"Burns and Moskowitz, 1977. Eighty-three percent in a laboratory with known BAC levels and alcohol as the only variable. Cole and Nowaczyk, 1994: officers watching video of completely sober subjects classified forty-six percent as impaired."

"Nearly half."

"Nearly half of sober people failed. The test is not specific to alcohol. The test is specific to difficulty. The officer is trained to read difficulty as impairment. The training is the specificity. The performance is the instrument. The officer is the reader. The reading has no calibration."

Maren sets the shoes down. "And this happens before the breathalyzer."

"Before the breathalyzer. On the roadside. As probable cause. The performance provides the legal basis for the measurement. The actual measurement — the one that would show the driver is sober — arrives after the arrest. The arrest is based on the performance. The performance has no mechanism for distinguishing alcohol from any of the other things that make walking a line difficult at two in the morning."

"So the body is—"

"The body is the instrument the system doesn't have. The officer reads the body the way a doctor reads a thermometer, except the body isn't calibrated, the conditions aren't controlled, and the reading has no error rate. The performance substitutes for the measurement. The substitution is the test."

The paragraph would have a place for this: the body performing the impairment the system needs to see. But the thirty-nine lines already recognize the shape — the system that reads what it needs to read, in the instrument it chose not to calibrate.
