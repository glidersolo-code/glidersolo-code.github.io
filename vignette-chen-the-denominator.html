The Denominator

"Ninety-five percent effective," Chen reads from the headline.

"Against what baseline?" Maren asks.

"The trial had 8 cases in the vaccine group and 162 in the placebo group. Out of roughly 21,000 per arm."

"So the placebo infection rate was 0.75%. The vaccine rate was 0.04%. The absolute risk reduction is 0.71%."

"And the relative risk reduction is 95%."

"Same data. Same trial. Same 43,548 people. Two arithmetic operations. One produces a number a hundred times larger than the other."

Chen looks at both numbers. "The relative risk reduction divides. The absolute risk reduction subtracts."

"The division strips the baseline. The baseline is the denominator. Without the denominator, 95% could mean preventing 475 cases per thousand or 8 cases per thousand. Both yield 95%. The number can't tell you which."

"Olliaro's table."

"Olliaro, Torreele, and Vaillant, Lancet Microbe, 2021. Pfizer: 95% relative, 0.84% absolute, NNV 119. AstraZeneca: 67% relative, 1.3% absolute, NNV 78. By relative risk, Pfizer wins. By absolute risk, AstraZeneca wins. The rankings invert."

"Because Pfizer's trial had a lower background rate."

"The denominator was smaller. Dividing by a smaller denominator produces a larger ratio. The vaccine that looked most effective by ratio looked most effective because its denominator — the thing the division removed — happened to be the smallest."

Chen is quiet. "Schwartz and Woloshin."

"Show only the relative number: 88% would take the drug. Show the absolute number: 42% would take it. A 46-point swing. Same drug, same trial, same evidence. Different arithmetic shown to the reader."

"The paragraph would say —"

"The paragraph would say the concealment is the division sign. Not the format. The format is a percentage, and percentages are fine. Not the precision. The numbers are precise. The concealment is the mathematical operation itself — the act of dividing by baseline risk, which inflates the result in proportion to how low the baseline was, and removes from the reader's view the very quantity that determines whether the effect matters."

"And Schwartz?"

"Died in 2018. The Drug Facts Box her team designed — two columns, both rates visible, no ratio computed for the reader — was endorsed by the FDA advisory committee. Congress directed the FDA to consider it. It has not become mandatory."

Chen closes the paper. The denominator is the context. The ratio is the number without context. The headline is the ratio. The reader received the headline.
