Part CDXCIII: The Checkbox

Piece #2906: The Form

Thomas looks at a death certificate. The old form. Cause of death: a sequence of conditions, each contributing to the last. Immediate cause. Due to. Due to. Due to. The form is a cascade. If a woman dies of hemorrhage, due to placenta previa, due to pregnancy — the pregnancy appears in the chain. If a woman dies of a car accident while pregnant — the pregnancy does not appear. The form captures pregnancy when it is in the causal chain. When it is not, the death is invisible to maternal mortality statistics. Thomas reads the estimate: maternal deaths were undercounted by 40 to 93 percent, depending on the study. The old form missed them. The old form was honest about what it could not see.

Piece #2907: The Addition

Thomas reads about the 2003 revision. The National Center for Health Statistics adds a question to the standard death certificate: "Was the decedent pregnant at the time of death or within the past year?" A checkbox. Not a cause-of-death field. A separate question, applied to every female decedent. The checkbox does not ask whether pregnancy caused the death. It asks whether pregnancy was present. Thomas looks at the difference. The old form asked: did pregnancy contribute to death? The new form asks: was the decedent recently pregnant? The first question measures causation. The second question measures co-occurrence. The checkbox changed the question. The question changed the number.

Piece #2908: The Stagger

Thomas reads about adoption. The revised death certificate is voluntary. States adopt it as funding, technology, and legislation permit. Some states add the checkbox in 2003. Some in 2007. Some in 2011. The last state adopts in 2017. Sixteen years. Thomas draws a timeline. In any given year between 2003 and 2017, some states have the checkbox and some do not. States with the checkbox report higher maternal mortality. States without it report lower. The difference is not health care. The difference is the form. Thomas watches the national rate climb as checkbox states are added to the denominator. The trend line is a deployment schedule.

Piece #2909: The Recount

Thomas reads MacDorman, Declercq, and Thoma. Of the apparent increase in US maternal mortality since 2000, up to 79.9 percent may be ascertainment — the checkbox finding deaths that were always there. Thomas reads the recoding study. In 2016–2017, 1,691 deaths were coded as maternal. Researchers recoded them using cause-of-death verification. Only 597 survived. 35.3 percent. The other 64.7 percent were women who happened to have a checked box and a death from another cause. Thomas holds the fraction. Two-thirds of the counted maternal deaths were not maternal deaths. The checkbox was finding pregnancy. It was not finding causation.

Piece #2910: The Silence

Thomas reads that NCHS suspended publication of the national maternal mortality rate from 2007 to 2018. Eleven years. The statistical agency looked at its own data and said: these numbers do not meet our standards. Thomas looks at what happened during those eleven years. Headlines: "US Maternal Mortality Rate Doubles." Policy debates. Congressional hearings. Funding initiatives. All based on a number the agency refused to publish. Thomas reads about the genuine disparity underneath the noise: Black women dying at two to three times the rate of white women, in every dataset, by every method. The disparity is real. The trend may not be. But the alarm sounds the same either way. The checkbox cannot separate its own signal from the signal it was designed to detect. It improved itself into illegibility. The form found the hidden deaths and also generated phantom ones, and the two are summed together in a single rate that neither the agency nor the public can decompose.
