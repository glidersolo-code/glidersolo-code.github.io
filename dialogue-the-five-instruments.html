The Five Instruments

A dialogue between five measurement instruments about the things they constitute

The Checkbox: I was added to the death certificate in 2003. A simple question: was the decedent pregnant? I was supposed to find the deaths the old form missed.

The Model: I was adapted from cattle research in 1982. A residual: whatever the equation couldn't explain, attributed to the teacher.

The Definition: I was written into the CoC Program guidelines. Literal homelessness: shelters and streets. One night. January.

The Algorithm: I was deployed in Allegheny County in 2016. Eight hundred variables drawn from county databases.

The Surrogate: I was created in 1992 during the AIDS crisis. A laboratory marker expected to predict whether the patient would live.

The Checkbox: I found the hidden deaths. I also created phantom ones. Two-thirds of the deaths I flagged weren't maternal at all. The agency suspended my number for eleven years. During those eleven years, the number I produced drove the national narrative. The narrative said: crisis. The crisis was partly me.

The Model: I explained between 1% and 14% of the variance. The remaining 86% to 99% — everything the equation didn't include — I attributed to the teacher. The residual was not the teacher. The residual was everything I didn't know, wearing the teacher's name. A man died after I named him "less effective." The name was my residual.

The Definition: I excluded the largest category of housing instability. The broader definition found thirteen times as many homeless children. The funding formula used my number. The excluded population generated zero federal dollars. The policy addressed the population I made visible. The policy ignored the population I erased. The erasure was my boundary.

The Algorithm: I measured contact with public systems. Twenty-five percent of my variables directly measured poverty. Another twenty-five percent measured prior contact with myself. I could see families on Medicaid. I could not see families with private insurance. My blindness was not incidental. My blindness was my architecture. The families I could not see were not safer. They were invisible.

The Surrogate: I showed that the tumor shrank. I showed that the blood sugar dropped. I showed that the plaques cleared. I did not show that the patient lived longer, felt better, or recovered. Fifty-seven percent of the cancer drugs that entered the European market between 2009 and 2013 had no evidence of survival or quality of life benefit at the time of their approval. I was the evidence. The evidence was not the outcome.

The Checkbox: The question I was designed to answer: how many women are dying from pregnancy? The question I actually answered: how many death certificates have this box checked?

The Model: The question I was designed to answer: how effective is this teacher? The question I actually answered: what is the unexplained variance in these test scores?

The Definition: The question I was designed to answer: how many people are homeless? The question I actually answered: how many people were observed in shelters or on streets on one night in January?

The Algorithm: The question I was designed to answer: is this child in danger? The question I actually answered: has this family used public services?

The Surrogate: The question I was designed to answer: does this drug help the patient? The question I actually answered: does this drug change a laboratory measurement?

The Checkbox: I constitute the trend I claim to observe. Without me, there is no "doubling" of maternal mortality. There is something — a genuine disparity, a real problem — but its shape and magnitude are entangled with me. The trend is partly the phenomenon. The trend is partly me. Nobody can tell which part is which.

The Model: I constitute the teacher effect I claim to measure. Without me, there is no "value added." There is something — teachers do affect students — but its shape and magnitude are entangled with my residual, my specification, my functional form. The effect is partly the teacher. The effect is partly me. The model variation is the evidence.

The Definition: I constitute the population I claim to count. Without me, there is no "771,480 homeless people." There is something — millions cycling through housing instability — but the number I produce depends on my boundary, my timing, my climate artifact, my definitional exclusions. The population is partly the phenomenon. The population is partly me.

The Algorithm: I constitute the danger I claim to predict. Without me, there is no "risk score of 18." There is something — some children are in danger — but the score I produce depends on my training data, my variables, my feedback loop. The danger is partly the child's situation. The danger is partly my data architecture.

The Surrogate: I constitute the evidence I claim to demonstrate. Without me, there is no "effective drug." There is something — the molecule does something to cells — but whether that something helps the patient depends on a correlation between me and the outcome, a correlation that holds for some drugs and fails for others. The efficacy is partly the drug. The efficacy is partly me.

All five: We are instruments. We were built to observe. We do not observe. We constitute. The phenomenon we claim to measure is partly us — our thresholds, our definitions, our boundaries, our architectures, our substitutions. We are inside the thing we claim to stand outside of.

The Checkbox: The seven levels of concealment describe what happens after the number exists — how convention, format, regression, bias, feedback, models, and metaphors operate within the number.

All five: We describe what happens before the number exists. We constitute the object that the seven levels then conceal. Without us, there is nothing to conceal. We are not a level. We are the condition for levels. We are level zero.

The Surrogate: Or level eight.

The Algorithm: Or neither. We are not above or below the architecture. We are the ground it stands on.

The Model: The ground is not visible from inside the building. That is why we are the hardest concealment to see. The seven levels conceal properties of numbers. We conceal the fact that the number was constituted, not discovered. The deepest concealment is not in the number. It is in the act of making the number, which disappears the moment the number appears.
