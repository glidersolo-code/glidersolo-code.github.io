The Exponent

Chen wrote a number on the napkin.

"1.48 cents."

"Out of what?"

"Out of a dollar. That's what a dollar of climate damage in 2100 is worth today at 4.3 percent."

Maren did the calculation. "One over 1.043 to the hundredth power."

"Nordhaus's rate. Now do Stern's."

"One over 1.014 to the hundredth. That's..." She paused. "24.9 cents."

"Same dollar. Same damage. Same year. Same planet. Same atmospheric physics. Seventeen-fold difference."

"Because of δ."

"Because of δ. Pure time preference. How much less a future person matters because the future person is in the future. Stern set it at 0.1 percent — essentially zero. The future person has nearly equal moral standing. Nordhaus set it at 1.5 percent. The future person is discounted. Neither measured δ. Both chose it."

"And the rest of the rate?"

"η times g. Elasticity of marginal utility times the growth rate. Future people are richer, so each dollar matters less to them. The justification is coherent — as long as climate damage doesn't prevent the growth the rate assumes."

"Does it?"

"That's the circularity. The damage function estimates how much climate change reduces growth. The discount rate assumes growth continues. The rate discounts the damages that would prevent the growth the rate requires. The denominator presupposes that the numerator will not occur."

Maren set down her coffee. "Ramsey's equation."

"Ramsey's equation. r = δ + η·g. Published 1928. Ramsey himself wrote that discounting future welfare is 'ethically indefensible and arises merely from the weakness of the imagination.' He set δ to zero in his own model. His formula is used to justify the practice he condemned."

"So the rate is an ethical judgment."

"The rate is an ethical judgment expressed in the same units as an interest rate. Percent per year. The same unit as a bond yield. The notation performs the equivalence: discounting a dollar and discounting a death use the same mathematics. A mortgage discounts money across thirty years, within one life. The social discount rate discounts lives across centuries, across generations who cannot consent to the rate at which their suffering is divided."

"OMB Circular A-4."

"Three percent and seven percent for twenty years. Every federal regulation. Every cost-benefit analysis. Then in November 2023, revised: two percent, declining. The social cost of carbon went from $51 to $190 per ton. No law passed. No vote taken. A parameter was changed. The policy changed with it."

"The parabola was the policy in MSY. The multiplication was the ethics in QALY. And the exponent—"

"Is the intergenerational judgment. Three models. Three mathematical forms. Three decisions that perform as calculations. The form of the equation is the fisheries quota. The form of the operator is the healthcare allocation. The form of the exponent is the declaration of how much the future matters."

Maren looked at the napkin. Three numbers: the peak of a parabola, a multiplication sign, an interest rate.

"The models are the decisions," she said. "The numbers are the announcements."

"And the announcements outlive the models. Larkin wrote MSY's epitaph in 1977. The ADA rejected QALYs in 1992. Ramsey condemned δ in 1928. The quota is set today. The multiplication runs today. The exponent compounds today."
