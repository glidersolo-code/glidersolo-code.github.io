The Flood

"One hundred years," Chen reads from the FEMA website. "The 100-year flood."

"What does the reader hear?"

"Once per century. If one happened last year, the next one is 99 years away."

"And what does the mathematics say?"

"1% annual exceedance probability. Independent each year. After a 100-year flood, next year's probability is still 1%."

"The probability during a 30-year mortgage?"

"1 minus 0.99 to the 30th power. Twenty-six percent. One in four."

Maren sets down her calculator. "Same information. '100-year flood' and '26% chance during your mortgage.' The first sounds like a distant possibility. The second sounds like a reason to move."

"Gumbel's reciprocal."

"Emil Julius Gumbel. Fled Nazi Germany. Published 'The Return Period of Flood Flows' in 1941. The recurrence interval is T = 1/P. For the 1% flood, T = 100. A mathematical reciprocal. The reciprocal became a label. The label became a schedule."

"And then Harvey."

"Harris County, August 2017. Over a meter of rain in three days. 154,170 homes flooded. 68% were outside the 100-year floodplain. Of 37 fatalities, only 8 inside the 100-year zone."

"The map said they were safe."

"The map was a statistical inference from historical data drawn on a geographic surface that had changed since the inference was made. A quarter of FEMA maps are 15 or more years old. Congress directed FEMA in 2012 to incorporate climate projections. As of 2025: not done."

"The paragraph would say —"

"The paragraph would say the name is the concealment. Not the mathematics — the mathematics is correct. Not the map — the map is a reasonable rendering of the data it had. The name. The conversion from '1% annual probability' to '100-year flood' replaces a probability with a temporal interval. The temporal interval invites the gambler's fallacy. The fallacy says: the rare event happened, so the waiting period has begun. The probability says: nothing has begun. Each year starts over. The river does not remember."

"The USGS changed the terminology."

"To '1% AEP flood.' FEMA still uses '100-year flood.' The name that converts probability into schedule remains the official language of federal flood management."

Chen closes the FEMA page. The reciprocal T = 1/P was a mathematical convenience. The convenience became a label. The label became a promise. The promise became the reason 68% of flooded homes in Harvey didn't have flood insurance.
