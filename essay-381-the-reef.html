The Reef

The bottle says reef safe. The consumer applies sunscreen and swims into the ocean, carrying the label's promise: this product will not harm the coral. The reef is safe from this swimmer. The guilt dissolves in the water.

"Reef safe" has no legal definition. No FDA regulation. No certification standard. No third-party verification. The FTC settled a $300,000 enforcement action against one brand for the claim. The phrase is available to any manufacturer who prints it on any bottle.

In practice, "reef safe" means the product does not contain oxybenzone and octinoxate — two UV filters that studies have linked to coral bleaching at concentrations found near popular snorkeling sites. Hawaii banned them in 2018. Several island nations followed. The bans are real. The science on these two chemicals is substantial.

But the label says "reef safe." Not "free of oxybenzone and octinoxate." Not "partially evaluated for marine toxicity." Not "two chemicals removed; others untested." The label says the reef is safe. The exclusion of two chemicals performs the safety of the entire formulation.

The formulation contains dozens of ingredients. Other chemical UV filters — avobenzone, homosalate, octocrylene — remain in many "reef safe" products. Their marine effects are less studied. Mineral alternatives — zinc oxide, titanium dioxide in nanoparticle form — have shown toxicity to coral larvae, sea urchin embryos, and marine algae at relevant concentrations. The particles that make mineral sunscreen cosmetically elegant are the particles that interact with marine biology. Beyond the UV filters: preservatives, emulsifiers, fragrances, stabilizers. None evaluated for reef safety by the label that claims reef safety.

The mechanism is exclusion performing safety. The label names what was removed. The consumer infers the safety of what remains. The removal of two known harms performs the absence of all harm. The exclusion performs the exoneration.

This is the thirteenth modality. The label does not claim the product was tested on reef organisms. It does not claim all ingredients were evaluated. It claims the result — safe — by announcing the exclusion. Two chemicals were removed. Therefore: safe. The arithmetic of subtraction again, as in "carbon neutral." Removing known bad things performs the goodness of the remaining things. The consumer reads the subtraction and sees the zero. The reef receives the formulation.
