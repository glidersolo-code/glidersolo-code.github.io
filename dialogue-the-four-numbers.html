The Four Numbers

A dialogue between the 2% inflation target, the 3% deficit ceiling, the US debt ceiling, and the number 435.

2%: I was improvised on television. April Fools' Day, 1988. A finance minister on a New Zealand talk show, asked what inflation should be, said "zero to one percent." A chance remark. A rationalization was built around me afterward — CPI measurement bias, rounding, a band. Then I was adopted. New Zealand, Canada, England, Sweden, the ECB, and finally the Fed, twenty-three years later. By the time the last central bank adopted me, nobody asked whether I was the right number. The chain of adoption performed the research.

3%: I was calculated on the back of an envelope. May 1981. Mitterrand wanted a rule that "sounds like economics." A young graduate divided a budget deficit by GDP, got roughly three, and delivered a number he later described as "devoid of the slightest economic sense." Then someone noticed that 3/5 equals 0.6, and the sixty percent debt threshold was reverse-engineered from that coincidence to make the arithmetic look coherent. The ratio format performed the economics. The percentage sign performed the derivation.

Ceiling: I was created for convenience. 1917. Congress was tired of approving every individual bond issuance. They set a limit — $11.5 billion — so Treasury could manage debt without returning for permission each time. A gift of flexibility, not a constraint. I have been raised ninety-eight times. The word "ceiling" performs immovability. I have never been immovable. The separate vote performs deliberation over the government's fiscal trajectory. The trajectory was determined by the spending and revenue votes that preceded me. I am, as Greenspan said, either redundant or inconsistent. There is no third option.

435: I was never chosen. I accumulated. The House grew after every census for a hundred and twenty years — thirteen consecutive expansions. Then the 1920 census showed more Americans in cities than on farms, and a rural Congress blocked reapportionment for a decade. The resolution was to freeze me at whatever I happened to be. Congress "avoided the fight entirely by deciding to not find the right number but use the current number." I am the status quo performing a design specification.

2%: What do we have in common?

3%: None of us was derived.

Ceiling: And all of us govern.

435: The format does the work. You are a percentage. You are a ratio. You are a fixed dollar amount in statute. I am a count in legislation. In each case, the format performs the analysis that was never conducted.

2%: But the mechanisms are different. I was adopted sequentially — each central bank cited the one before, not the origin. Convergence performed discovery. The chain substituted for research.

3%: I was formatted — a percentage of GDP looks economic regardless of whether the number was calculated on an envelope in an hour. The format substitutes for the economics.

Ceiling: I am procedural — a convenience that performs a principled commitment. The separate vote substitutes for fiscal deliberation.

435: And I am frozen — a status quo that performs optimal design. The act of not changing the number substitutes for the analysis of what the number should be.

3%: Here is what disturbs me. The origins are documented. Abeille gave interviews. He said it plainly: devoid of the slightest economic sense. Douglas improvised on television. The Gephardt Rule acknowledged the ceiling's redundancy for sixteen years. And you — you are simply the number that existed when Congress stopped counting.

435: All four origins are public knowledge.

Ceiling: And the knowledge changes nothing.

2%: This is the shared structure. Not that we lack derivation — that the absence of derivation is known and does not weaken us. The documentation circulates alongside the governance. The textbook footnote and the policy target coexist.

3%: The Herndon correction circulated alongside the Reinhart-Rogoff paper. The correction was published. The policies continued. The error entered politics at the speed of testimony. The correction entered at the speed of academic journals.

435: My ratio is twenty-five times worse than Washington's maximum. The statistic is published by Pew. It changes nothing. The cube root law predicts 692. Every other democracy sits close to its prediction. The United States sits at 63 percent. Published. Known. Unchanged.

Ceiling: I have been raised ninety-eight times. Each time, the raise is treated as a crisis — as though the ceiling were a principled commitment being reluctantly abandoned. The history of constant revision is public record. The performance of immovability continues regardless.

2%: Four numbers. Four mechanisms. One structural insight.

3%: What is it?

2%: The absence of derivation is never the vulnerability. The format is always the defense. The percentage sign, the dollar figure, the statutory count, the adoption chain — these formats perform the derivation so completely that the documented absence of derivation cannot compete. The format is stronger than the fact.

435: The freeze is stronger than the analysis.

Ceiling: The word "ceiling" is stronger than ninety-eight raises.

3%: The percentage sign is stronger than the envelope.

2%: And the chain of central banks is stronger than the television interview.

The four numbers remain in their respective statutes, treaties, and acts. Each governs. None was derived. Each origin is documented. Each document changes nothing. The format performs.
