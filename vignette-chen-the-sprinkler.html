The Sprinkler

Chen stood at the edge of the dry grass, watching the oscillating sprinkler paint a wet rectangle in the center of the yard. Twenty-two feet in each direction. The box had said 3,600 square feet. The wet patch was perhaps 1,300.

"The box describes the sprinkler at 80 PSI," Chen said.

"And the spigot?"

"Forty-two. Through a hundred feet of hose, maybe thirty at the nozzle." Chen watched the arc of water reach its apex and fall short of the flower bed. "The coverage area varies with pressure roughly linearly. Thirty over eighty is thirty-seven percent. Thirty-seven percent of 3,600 is 1,332."

"Which is what the wet grass says."

"Which is what the wet grass says. The box and the lawn agree on the physics. They disagree on the premise. The box assumes 80 PSI. The lawn provides 30."

Maren looked at the box, still sitting on the patio. "Covers up to 3,600 square feet."

"'Up to.' Two words carrying four variables: pressure, hose length, hose diameter, flow rate. The consumer reads the integer. The 'up to' is present but weightless. It cancels in the comparison — both boxes in the aisle say 'up to.' What remains are two areas and the assumption that larger means more."

"The paragraph."

"At zero point eight."

"The paragraph's coverage area — the number of square feet of attention the paragraph claims to water. Every sub-argument, every nuance, every qualifier. Covers up to 3,600 square feet of intellectual territory."

"But the reader's attention is a thirty-PSI spigot through a hundred-foot hose. The pressure at the nozzle — the attention at the moment of reading — is thirty-seven percent of what the paragraph was tested at. The paragraph was tested at eighty PSI. Full, undivided, uninterrupted attention. The laboratory condition."

"And the reader provides the yard condition."

"Always. Distractions are hose friction. Context-switching is pressure loss. The paragraph arrives at the reader's nozzle at thirty PSI and waters 1,300 square feet of the 3,600 it claimed." Chen looked at the dry corners of the yard. "The dry corners are the arguments the reader never received. Present in the paragraph, absent from the reading."

"And the paragraph can't know which corners are dry."

"It can't. The paragraph was designed for 80 PSI. It doesn't know the reader's hose length. It doesn't know the reader's pressure. It covers up to 3,600 square feet. The reader provides the 'up to.'"
