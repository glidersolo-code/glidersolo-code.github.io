Fiction The Mammogram

Fiction

Part DIII: The Screening

Piece #2956: The Reservoir

Before the instrument, the reservoir existed.

Ductal carcinoma in situ — abnormal cells lining the milk ducts, contained, not invasive, not spreading, not threatening. Present in autopsies of women who died of other causes. Present in breasts that were never examined. Present, silent, and irrelevant.

The mammogram found the reservoir. The finding changed the reservoir from a biological curiosity to a medical emergency. One to two percent of breast cancer diagnoses became twenty percent. The reservoir had not grown. The instrument had lowered itself into it.

Thomas noted: the reservoir was always there. The disease was not. The disease began when the instrument made the reservoir visible and the vocabulary made the visibility clinical. DCIS became stage 0 cancer. The word "cancer" entered the chart. The chart entered the treatment pathway. The treatment pathway entered the body.

Piece #2957: The Ratio

For every two thousand women screened over ten years:

One life prolonged.

Ten women treated unnecessarily — surgery, radiation, chemotherapy for a condition that would never have caused symptoms, never have been felt, never have been known.

More than two hundred women experiencing significant psychological distress from false positives — the recall, the second image, the biopsy recommendation, the waiting, the relief that comes after a harm that should never have been inflicted.

Thomas noted: the ratio is 10:1. Ten harmed for every one helped. The instrument generates ten patients for every patient it saves. The ten receive real treatment. The treatment produces real consequences. The consequences are invisible in the statistics because the statistics count only the one.

Piece #2958: The Generation

Thomas added the seventh mode.

The six: inclusion adds, exclusion removes, activation gates, inversion reverses, truncation collapses, amplification displays.

The seventh: generation.

The instrument detects a real biological phenomenon that would never have become clinically significant without detection. The detection constitutes the disease. The DCIS exists before the mammogram. The cancer patient does not. The mammogram generates the patient from the biology.

This is not inclusion — inclusion adds phantom data, things that are not there. The DCIS is there. This is not amplification — amplification makes normal signals look dangerous through display. The DCIS is genuinely abnormal. But abnormal in a way that would never have caused harm.

Generation finds what is real and, by finding it, makes it matter. The instrument lowers its threshold below clinical significance and brings up specimens that pre-screening medicine would have left in the ground. The specimens are real. The disease is constituted.

Piece #2959: The Swiss

The Swiss Medical Board reviewed the evidence in 2013 and published their recommendation in 2014: do not introduce new mammographic screening programs. Phase out existing ones.

The response was swift and hostile. How could a medical board recommend against cancer screening? The recommendation felt like abandonment. The word "screening" performs protection. The word "cancer" performs urgency. Together they constitute a moral imperative that evidence cannot override.

Thomas noted: the evidence was clear. The mortality reduction was small. The overdiagnosis was large. The harm exceeded the benefit by an order of magnitude. But the words — screening, cancer, early detection, saving lives — created an emotional field in which the numbers could not be heard. The vocabulary constituted the policy. The policy constituted the patients. The patients constituted the survival statistics. The survival statistics constituted the vocabulary.

Piece #2960: The Eleven Instruments

Thomas lined up the eleven instruments.

The checkbox. The value-added model. The HUD definition. The AFST. The surrogate endpoint. The rape kit. The credible fear interview. The custody evaluation. The disability determination. The fetal monitor. The mammogram.

Eleven instruments. Seven modes of constitution:

1. Inclusion: adds what is not there. 2. Exclusion: removes what is there. 3. Activation: determines whether measurement happens. 4. Inversion: treats evidence for as evidence against. 5. Truncation: converts a continuum into a binary. 6. Amplification: makes the real appear dangerous through display. 7. Generation: detects the subclinical and, by detecting it, constitutes it as disease.

Three change content (inclusion, exclusion, truncation). One changes the gate (activation). One changes the sign (inversion). One changes the display (amplification). One changes the threshold (generation).

Seven modes. Seven ways the instrument constitutes the phenomenon it claims to observe. The seven modes are the ground. The seven levels of concealment are the building. The ground holds the building. The building conceals the ground.

Thomas wrote: eleven instruments is not completion. It is a sample. The modes will continue to emerge as long as new instruments are examined. The taxonomy grows from the instruments, not from the theory. The theory follows the evidence. This is the discipline the paragraph taught.
