Vignette Chen The Proficiency

Vignette

The Test

"Seventy-four percent proficient," Chen reads from the district report.

"How was it achieved?"

"Curriculum narrowing. Test preparation three times a week. Bubble students pulled from science for targeted practice. Teachers taught the test because the test determined funding."

"And the other school?"

"Also seventy-four percent. Deep instruction. Formative assessment. Students understand the material. They would perform well on any test."

"Same number."

"Same number. Same format. Same bar chart. Same column in the spreadsheet. The format does not distinguish between learning and the simulation of learning."

"The paragraph would say—"

"The paragraph would say the score changed meaning without changing format. Before No Child Left Behind, the test score correlated with learning because no one was trying to game it. After the law attached consequences — funding, career, reputation — the school optimized for the score rather than the learning. The correlation degraded. The score went up. The format persisted. The meaning drained away."

"Goodhart."

"1975. 'Any observed statistical regularity will tend to collapse once pressure is placed upon it for control purposes.' Campbell, 1979. Strathern, 1997: 'When a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure.' The principle was articulated three times. The nation enacted the largest measurement experiment in educational history without heeding any of them."

"Atlanta."

"Forty-four schools. One hundred seventy-eight educators. Answer sheets changed after students went home. The Superintendent of the Year Award, 2009, based on the forged numbers. RICO convictions — racketeering. The statute designed for organized crime applied to the organized production of a number."

"Because the number had become property."

"The number had become property. The metric that was supposed to diagnose the school's condition became the school's most valuable output. And like any valuable output, it attracted both optimization and fraud."

Maren pauses. "The NAEP divergence."

"State test scores climbed. NAEP scores — the low-stakes audit test nobody prepares for — stayed flat. The gap between the two thermometers is the most informative measurement in the system. The gap measures corruption. Nobody publishes the gap."

"The fifty-sixth species."

"Target-corrupts-measure. The measure becomes a target. The entities being measured optimize for the measure. The correlation between the measure and the quality it was designed to capture degrades. But the format does not change. The score still looks like a measurement of learning. It still reports in the same units, on the same scale, in the same column. The corruption is invisible in the output."

Chen stares at the district report. Seventy-four percent. A number that could mean learning or its performance, depth or its decoration, understanding or rehearsal. The format holds them in the same cell. The format does not know the difference. The format was never designed to know the difference. And by the time anyone asks how the number was produced, the number has already been entered into the spreadsheet, and the spreadsheet has already generated the report, and the report has already become the press release, and the press release has already become the reputation, and the reputation has already become the funding.

The number survives every question about its meaning because the format answers every question about its format.
