The Impression

In letterpress, an impression is the pressure of type against paper. The word is physical. The ink transfers because force was applied. The reader encounters the mark because the mark was pressed into the surface.

In digital advertising, an impression is a pixel rendered in a browser. The word is inherited. The force is absent. The reader may not be present. The mark exists in a server log.

The first banner ad appeared on HotWired.com on October 27, 1994. The serving was counted. The count was called an impression. The word migrated from a physical event — type pressing paper, a person holding a magazine — to a technical event: an ad rendered in a viewport, possibly below the fold, possibly for less than a second, possibly to a machine.

The Media Rating Council standardized viewability in 2014. A display ad is "viewable" if 50% of its pixels are visible for one continuous second. Half the ad for one second. This is the definition of "seen." The standard replaced a worse standard — before 2014, ads loaded below the fold and never scrolled to still counted. The improvement made the inadequacy precise. Fifty percent. One second. This is the industry's formal claim about what it means for a human to notice.

Bot traffic generates billions of fraudulent impressions. The Methbot operation simulated human browsing across 1,900 servers; the 3ve botnet infected 1.7 million computers. Combined fraud: approximately thirty-six million dollars. Each bot view was counted the same as each human view. The metric could not distinguish between them because the metric measured delivery, not cognition. The word "impression" performed the cognition the bot could not.

The gap between the word and the measurement is the revenue model. The advertiser pays CPM — cost per mille, per thousand impressions. The word says: a thousand people noticed. The measurement says: an ad was rendered a thousand times. The advertiser reads the word and pays for attention. The server records the rendering and counts the delivery. The money crosses the gap. The gap is not a bug. The gap is the product.
