Vignette Chen The Bathroom Scale

Vignette

The Scale

Chen stepped on the scale. "154.2," she read.

She stepped off, waited three seconds, stepped on again. "154.0."

Off. On. "154.4."

Maren watched from the doorway. "Three readings, thirty seconds, point-four spread."

"The display resolves to zero-point-two pounds. The accuracy is plus or minus one to two pounds. The display performs a precision the instrument doesn't have."

"So the 154.2 could be anywhere from —"

"152 to 156. Roughly." Chen stepped off and slid the scale from the tile to the bath mat. She stepped on. "151.6."

"The carpet."

"The mat. The load cells need a hard, flat surface. On anything soft, the feet sink, the platform flexes, the cells read low." She slid the scale back to the tile. "154.2 again. I lost two and a half pounds by moving the scale three feet."

"The paragraph."

"At zero point eight."

"The paragraph displays to one decimal. It reads 'zero point eight.' But the accuracy —"

"The accuracy is plus or minus something. We don't know the tolerance. The decimal invites us to read the number as precise — 0.8, not 'approximately 0.8,' not 'somewhere between 0.6 and 1.0.' The resolution of the display suggests the resolution of the truth."

"And the surface matters."

"The surface always matters. Read the paragraph on tile — in the context Chen intended — and it reads 0.8. Read it on carpet — in a different context, a different conversation, a different reader's posture — and it reads differently."

"But the paragraph doesn't change."

"No. The paragraph is the scale. The reading changes because the surface changes." Chen stepped on the scale one more time. "154.2. Or something near it. The answer isn't in the tenths, Maren. The answer is in the trend. Read the paragraph once and you get a decimal. Read it across the whole argument — weekly, monthly — and you get a signal."

"The consumer who watches the decimal watches the noise."

"The consumer who watches the trend watches the signal." Chen stepped off. "The scale shows both. It doesn't say which is which."
