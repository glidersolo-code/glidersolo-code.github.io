Part CDXXXVII: The Statement

Piece #2626: The Document

Thomas reads an Environmental Impact Statement. The document is 661 pages long. It took 4.5 years to prepare. It cost $2 million.

The document identifies habitat fragmentation. The document identifies watershed contamination. The document identifies species displacement. The document evaluates alternatives, including no action. The document is thorough. The document is comprehensive. The document demonstrates that the agency considered the environment.

Thomas turns to the Record of Decision. The agency chose to build the highway. The forest will be fragmented. The watershed will be contaminated. The species will be displaced. The document said so. The agency said so. The agency chose to proceed. The document changed the legality of the decision. The document did not change the decision.

Piece #2627: The Law

Thomas reads the National Environmental Policy Act. Nixon signed it on January 1, 1970 — his first official act of the decade. The law requires federal agencies to prepare a "detailed statement" on the environmental impact of major actions.

Thomas reads the case law. Robertson v. Methow Valley Citizens Council, 1989. Justice Stevens, writing for a unanimous court: NEPA "simply prescribes the necessary process for preventing uninformed — rather than unwise — agency action."

Thomas reads the sentence again. Uninformed, not unwise. The law prevents not-knowing. The law does not prevent not-caring. The agency must know the harm. The agency need not prevent the harm. The knowledge is the compliance. The compliance is the knowledge.

Strycker's Bay, 1980: once the agency has considered the environmental consequences, the court cannot interject itself. The consideration IS the legal act. The decision that follows lies beyond the law's reach. The document performs the consideration. The consideration performs the compliance. The compliance need not perform the protection.

Piece #2628: The Process

Thomas learns the numbers. Approximately 500 Environmental Impact Statements are prepared each year — less than 1% of all federal actions requiring NEPA analysis. The other 99% are resolved through categorical exclusions — approximately 100,000 per year — or Environmental Assessments that produce a Finding of No Significant Impact.

Thomas considers the mitigated FONSI. The agency proposes an action. The agency prepares an Environmental Assessment. The assessment finds significant impacts. The agency promises mitigation measures. The measures bring the impacts below the threshold of significance. The agency issues a Finding of No Significant Impact. The full EIS is avoided. The agency monitors its own compliance with its own promises.

The mitigation may or may not occur. The document says it will occur. The document is the compliance.

Piece #2629: The Reform

Thomas reads the Fiscal Responsibility Act of 2023 — the first statutory amendment to NEPA since its enactment 53 years earlier. The amendment imposes page limits: 150 pages, 300 for extraordinary complexity. Time limits: 2 years for an EIS, 1 year for an EA.

Thomas thinks about what was reformed. The document was too long. The document was too slow. The reform made the document shorter and faster. The reform did not address the gap between the document and protection. The reform did not make the document binding. The reform did not require the agency to choose the least harmful option. The reform made the performance more efficient. The performance remains the same performance: documentation performs consideration.

The document is shorter now. The document is faster now. The document still does not require the agency to stop.

Piece #2630: The Gap

Thomas thinks about the gap between knowing and stopping.

The law requires the agency to know. The agency knows. The 661 pages demonstrate the knowing. The agency publishes the knowing. The public reads the knowing. The court reviews the knowing. If the knowing is adequate — if the document is thorough, if the alternatives were evaluated, if the public had opportunity to comment — the knowing is complete. The compliance is achieved. The project may proceed.

The forest may be fragmented. The watershed may be contaminated. The species may be displaced. The document said so. The document is on file. The document demonstrates that the agency was informed. The agency was not uninformed. Whether the agency was unwise is not a question the law asks.

The gap between knowing and stopping is not a flaw. The gap is the design. The law was written to produce informed decisions, not wise ones. The distinction is the mechanism. The document performs the consideration. The consideration performs the compliance. The compliance authorizes the harm the document identified. The document is the permission.
