Fiction The Size

Fiction

Part CLXXXIV: The Size

Piece #1361: The Referent

A size 8 dress in 1958 had a 24-inch waist. A size 8 dress today has a waist closer to 30 inches. The number is identical. The body it names has changed by six inches over sixty years. The label performs stability while the referent drifts. A woman who wore a size 12 in 1968 would wear a size 6 or 8 today without having changed. The number floats. The float is systematic and one-directional: always toward the smaller label, never away from it. A measurement that moves in only one direction is not a measurement. It is a negotiation.

Thomas's PHQ-9 score has its own referent drift. The cutpoints were validated against a population in the late 1990s. The population's symptom burden, help-seeking behavior, and baseline functioning were the referent. The cutpoints have not been revalidated as the population changed. A score of 10 in 2024 may describe a different severity than a score of 10 in 1999. The label is identical. The referent has moved.

Piece #1362: The Asymmetry

Men's clothing uses actual measurement. A 34-inch waist is 34 inches. A 32-inch inseam is 32 inches. The number is the thing it claims to measure — verifiable with a tape, comparable across brands, unchanged since the garment was made. Women's clothing uses an abstract numerical sequence with no fixed referent. The number names a position in a hierarchy the manufacturer sets. Two garments labeled size 10 can differ by four inches in every dimension. The label does not describe the garment. It describes the brand's intention toward the customer.

One system gives the customer information. The other gives the customer a feeling. The systems exist side by side in the same stores, on the same racks, bought by the same people who navigate between them daily and rarely remark on the difference. The asymmetry is not noticed because it is not new. It has always been this way. The always makes it feel like a property of clothing rather than a decision about customers.

Piece #1363: The Invention

Size 0 did not exist before vanity sizing required it. The original sequence ran from 8 to 18, then extended downward to 4 as the drift continued, then to 2, then to 0. The number reached zero and the drift did not stop, so size 00 was invented — a label that has passed through zero and emerged on the other side, a negative that cannot be named as negative. The sequence has no floor. It can always add another zero. The invention of new small sizes is not a response to new small bodies. It is a response to the sequence exhausting its supply of small numbers.

Thomas has watched the scales extend in both directions. The PHQ-9 runs from 0 to 27. Early in his treatment, his scores clustered near 20. As he improved, the question became where he was on the scale, what the numbers below 10 meant in terms of his actual days. He learned that the clinician's interest in the score changed at the cutpoints — 20, 15, 10. The numbers between cutpoints were less interesting than the numbers at them. He was a position in a sequence. He learned to navigate the sequence. He did not always know whether he was navigating toward health or toward a favorable label.

Piece #1364: The Tag

The actual dimensions of a garment — chest, waist, hip, inseam — are printed inside, in smaller type, near the collar or along the seam, facing inward. The abstract size is printed on the exterior tag, in large numbers, facing outward. What is shown to the world is the number with no referent. What is hidden in the seam is the number that describes the cloth. The architecture of the label performs a priority: the feeling is the main information, the measurement is incidental. You buy the feeling. The measurement is there if you need it, available but not prominent, accurate but not the point.

Thomas's discharge summary listed his GAD-7 score, his PHQ-9 score, his session count, his medication dosage. It did not list the dimensions those numbers failed to capture — the three weeks he could not leave the apartment, the specific texture of that difficulty, the way the difficulty changed over the course of treatment. The summary showed the label. The actual measurement was inside the seam, facing inward.

Piece #1365: The Fitting Room

In the fitting room, the number on the tag determines how the moment feels. Smaller than expected: a small pleasure, a sense of the body as favorable. Larger than expected: something to account for, a discrepancy to explain. The body has not changed between garments. The number has. The number is managing the transaction, not reporting the measurement. Two garments from two brands fit identically; one is a 10 and one is a 14. The one labeled 10 is purchased. The body is the same body in both. What changed is the label.

One hundred and eighty-four Parts. Thomas left his last appointment carrying a number. The number was lower than the previous month's. He felt the shape of that — the small pleasure, the sense of location at a favorable position in the sequence. He did not know, and was not told, that the sequence's position had been quietly moved, that the cutpoints were conventions, that the number described his place in the instrument's hierarchy and not his place in his own life. He carried the number home. It fit. He wore it for a week. Then the week ended, and the number was a label sewn into a collar at the back of the neck, facing inward, the actual dimensions of the cloth hidden in the seam, available to anyone who looked.
