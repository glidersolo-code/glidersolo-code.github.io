Vignette

The Score

Chen reads a map. Green, yellow, purple. Free, Partly Free, Not Free.

"What makes a country green?" Maren asks.

"A number above 71."

"What makes the number above 71?"

"Twenty-five indicators, each scored 0-4 by a regional analyst. The scores are summed. If the sum exceeds 71, the country is green."

"And each indicator —"

"Is a judgment call. Is the judiciary independent? The analyst says 3 or 4. Is the press free? The analyst says 2 or 3. The judgment reflects the analyst's framework. The framework reflects the institution's theory of freedom."

The paragraph is thirty-nine lines long. In it, Chen argues that the map performs the most efficient concealment in political metrology: it converts 25 subjective assessments into a color, and the color travels without any of the 25 assessments attached. The student sees green and thinks "free." The policymaker sees purple and thinks "unfree." Neither sees the methodology. Neither sees the template. The template is liberal electoral democracy — multiparty competition, individual rights, independent judiciary, free press. Countries that achieve governance through other traditions score lower not because they have less freedom but because they have a different kind.

"The United States is green," Maren says. "83 out of 100."

"The index's own narrative describes gerrymandering, mass incarceration, voter suppression, money in politics. The number is 83. The narrative is several pages. The number wins."

"Because the number travels."

"Because the number travels. The narrative stays in the report. The 83 goes everywhere."

Chen closes the map. Somewhere an analyst in Washington is scoring an indicator for a country the analyst has visited twice. The analyst assigns a 3. Somewhere else, a different analyst assigns a 4 to a similar indicator in a similar country. The difference — one point, one judgment, one person — will determine whether a country is "Free" or "Partly Free." The category will appear in a headline. The headline will inform policy. The policy will affect millions. The one point was the beginning.
