Vignette: The Assessment

Chen is looking at two property tax bills.

"Same house," she says.

"What do you mean, same house?"

"Same floor plan. Same builder. Same year. Same lot size. Same street. 1414 Elm pays $840. 1416 Elm pays $4,200."

Maren looks over her shoulder. "Different owners?"

"Different purchase dates. 1414 bought in 1985. 1416 bought in 2022. Proposition 13 froze every California property at its 1975 assessed value. Two percent increase per year. Reassessment only when you sell."

"So the 'value' is —"

"A date. A purchase date in currency notation. The bill says 'assessed value.' No one assessed anything. The computer added two percent to last year's number. The word 'assessment' comes from assidere — to sit beside. Nobody sat."

"And outside California?"

"Worse, in a different way. Berry reviewed forty million properties. Bottom decile of sale price: assessed at twice the ratio of the top decile. The errors aren't random. They flow downhill. In Detroit, the average home sold for $1,700 and was assessed at $41,000. Thirty times the price. One hundred thousand properties foreclosed in five years. The Michigan Constitution says no property can be assessed above fifty percent of market value. Detroit violated it for most properties, every year, for six years."

Maren is quiet for a moment. "So the format stayed the same."

"The bill kept arriving. Same format. Same line items. Assessed value. Tax rate. Amount due. The format survived the crisis it caused. And in Cook County, the assessor's campaign fund — five million dollars — came from property tax attorneys. The appeals industry wasn't a correction. The appeals industry was the business model."

"Wealthy homeowners appeal more."

"Forty-six percent versus eleven percent. Each appeal that succeeds shifts burden onto those who didn't appeal. The process performs fairness — anyone can appeal. The outcome performs redistribution — from poor to rich."

"The one hundred and first species."

"Acquisition-as-valuation. The purchase price masquerades as the assessed value. The format performs judgment. The content performs arithmetic on a historical fact."

Maren sets down the bills. "The house was never asked."

"The house was never asked."
