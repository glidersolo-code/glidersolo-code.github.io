Vignette Chen The Five Senses

Vignette

Chen's niece was four. They were reading a picture book together — the kind with flaps that lift. Five pages. Eyes for seeing, ears for hearing, nose for smelling, tongue for tasting, hands for touching. One sense per page. "The five senses!" the book declared on the cover, with a definite article and an exclamation point.

"Auntie Chen," the niece said, "how do I know where my feet are?"

Chen looked down. The niece's feet were under the blanket, invisible.

"You just know," Chen said.

"With which sense?"

Chen opened her mouth and closed it. Not sight — the feet were hidden. Not hearing, smell, taste, or touch. The knowledge was as immediate as seeing, as automatic as breathing. The niece could feel her toes curling without looking, could point to her elbow behind her back, could walk across a room in the dark. None of this appeared in the book.

"It's called proprioception," Chen said. "It's your body knowing where its parts are."

"So that's six," the niece said, counting on her fingers.

"Maybe. Or maybe more. Scientists disagree on how many."

The niece looked at the book. "But this says five."

"It does."

"Is it wrong?"

Chen thought about this longer than the question warranted. The book wasn't wrong in the way that "four" would be wrong — omitting hearing, say. It was wrong in a way that the format concealed: the number five performed a completeness that prevented the question "how many?" from being asked. The flaps — one per sense, five total — made the list look finished. The exclamation point sealed it. There was nowhere in the format for a sixth flap. The book's physical structure enforced the count.

"It's incomplete," Chen said carefully.

"Then why does it say 'the'?"

Chen didn't have a good answer for this. She sat with her niece and felt, without looking, the exact positions of her own hands. The knowledge was perfect, wordless, continuous. It had no page in the book. It had no name in the count. It was the sense that worked every moment of every day, and the number five had made it invisible by not counting it — and the definite article had made the not-counting permanent.

"Proprioception," the niece said, trying the word. She closed her eyes and wiggled her toes under the blanket. "I can feel them."

"Yes."

"With my sixth sense."

"The real one," Chen said.
