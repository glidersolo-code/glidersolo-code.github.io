The Stain

Chen-Maren vignette

Chen submits the paragraph to the committee. Thirty-nine lines. The committee applies their protocol: read, evaluate, classify. The protocol is a Gram stain — a visualization technique repurposed as a taxonomy. Crystal violet: the initial reading, which saturates the paragraph in the committee's interpretive framework. Iodine mordant: the discussion, which fixes the initial reading into a stable complex. Alcohol decolorization: the critical scrutiny that follows, which washes away whatever the paragraph could not hold under pressure. What remains — what retains the stain — is classified. What washes away is counterstained with a secondary category and filed under the lesser heading.

Maren has been reading about Hans Christian Gram. Berlin, 1884, a morgue. Gram was staining bacteria in lung tissue to make them visible. He was not classifying. He was solving a seeing problem. The classification came later, when successors treated the binary result — retains dye or does not — as a fundamental division. Gram-positive bacteria have thick peptidoglycan walls that trap the crystal violet-iodine complex. Gram-negative have thin walls and an outer membrane; the dye washes through. The difference is real but architectural. It does not correspond to evolutionary lineage. Woese showed this in 1987 with 16S rRNA phylogenetics. The binary persisted because the infrastructure — textbooks, clinical algorithms, drug pipelines — was built on it.

Chen's paragraph retains the committee's stain unevenly. Professor Hendricks finds the argument deeply embedded in the paragraph's structure — thick-walled, resistant to decolorization, Gram-positive. The argument holds its purple. Professor Wallace finds the same argument loosely attached to the paragraph's surface — thin-walled, vulnerable to critical scrutiny, Gram-negative. The stain washes through. The paragraph has not changed. The decolorization step differs. Hendricks applies brief, gentle alcohol. Wallace applies prolonged, vigorous alcohol. The protocol does not specify decolorization time in seconds.

The paragraph is also Gram-variable. In the first reading — a young culture, freshly grown — the argument retains the stain fully. Every line holds its purple. In the third reading — an older culture, aged by familiarity — the peptidoglycan of novelty has thinned. Passages that stained positive on first encounter stain variable or negative on the third. The paragraph is the same organism at different culture ages, and the committee reads it at different culture ages without acknowledging that the result depends on when they look.

And the paragraph is, in certain dimensions, mycoplasma. Its most radical move — the structural choice that makes it worth reading — has no cell wall at all. The Gram stain cannot reach it. The committee's protocol tests for properties the paragraph's most important feature does not possess. The stain works. The stain finds what it can find. What it cannot find, it does not report as present-but-unstainable. It reports as absent. The paragraph receives its classification: 3.4 out of 5.0, Gram-variable, with the notation that certain expected structural elements were not detected. The elements were not absent. They were wall-less. The stain was not designed for what has no wall.
