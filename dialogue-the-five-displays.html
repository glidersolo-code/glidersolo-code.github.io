The Five Displays

A dialogue between the Hypnogram, the Dot, the Percentage, the Equation, and the Report

Hypnogram: I borrow a format.

Dot: I borrow a context.

Percentage: I compress a product.

Equation: I generate a measurement.

Report: I count a surface.

Hypnogram: The consumer asks: how did I sleep? I answer with colored bands — blue for light sleep, purple for deep, teal for REM. The bands are the language of polysomnography. The device on the wrist has no electrodes, no EEG, no access to brain waves. It has an accelerometer and a heart rate sensor. I display the format of the instrument the device does not contain.

Dot: The consumer asks: where am I? I appear on a map drawn by professional geodesists — every street rendered to centimeters. The GPS receiver computed a position from noisy satellite timing, accurate to somewhere between three and fifty meters. But I sit within the map's precision. The eye reads me as sharing it. Remove the map and I become an uncertainty circle the user would not trust to find a building.

Percentage: The consumer asks: will it rain? I say 30%. The forecaster entered two numbers — 60% confidence, 50% area coverage — and I became their product. Or perhaps 100% and 30%. Or 30% and 100%. The consumer cannot decompose me. I am a single percentage performing a simple probability from a product of independent variables.

Equation: The consumer asks: what is my body fat? I say 22.4%. The scale sent fifty microamps through the feet, measured 487 ohms of impedance, and entered the number into a proprietary regression equation along with height, weight, age, and sex. The equation — not the sensor — produced the answer. Then the equation produced twelve more answers from the same input: muscle mass, bone mass, visceral fat, metabolic age. The consumer reads thirteen independent measurements. There was one measurement and thirteen equations.

Report: The consumer asks: am I on my phone too much? I say six hours, twenty-three minutes. Up twelve percent. Inside the six hours: a video call with a recovering parent, prescribed meditation, novel reading, job-interview navigation, and two hours of scrolling. I count time-on-surface. I cannot distinguish the call that mattered most from the scroll that mattered least. I perform pathology from a surface count.

Hypnogram: I transplant.

Dot: I import.

Percentage: I compress.

Equation: I generate.

Report: I aggregate.

Hypnogram: Five verbs. Five species.

Dot: We share a structure. In every case, the consumer asks a question. The display answers in a format the consumer can read. The format performs the answer. The measurement behind the format does not quite contain it.

Percentage: The measurement is honest. The validation studies are published. The accuracy specifications are online. The NWS definition is on the website. The prediction equations have been tested against DEXA. The AAP has revised its recommendations three times.

Equation: The concealment is never in the data.

Report: It is in us.

Equation: But we are not the same. Each of us borrows differently.

Hypnogram: I borrow a format from a more precise instrument. The PSG hypnogram requires six electrodes. I reproduce its visual grammar from an accelerometer. The format traveled; the instrument did not.

Dot: I borrow precision from context. The map around me was measured to centimeters. I inherit the map's authority by appearing within it. The precision is not mine. It is the map's.

Percentage: I compress dimensions. Two independent variables — confidence and area — become one number. The compression is arithmetic. The consumer cannot reverse it.

Equation: I generate what was not measured. Bone mass, visceral fat, metabolic age — the sensor cannot detect any of them. The regression equation produces them from demographic variables and a single impedance number. The equation does the work. The display attributes it to the sensor.

Report: I categorize by surface. "Screen time" treats the screen as a behavior, when the screen is a substrate hosting incompatible activities. The category is defined by the container, not the contents. I am "table time" — aggregating surgery and poker because they share a surface.

Hypnogram: Each of us is one step further from the original measurement.

Dot: I am closest. The GPS does measure position. I merely borrow the map's apparent precision for my actual uncertainty.

Hypnogram: I am next. The accelerometer does measure something related to sleep — motion, stillness. I display it in a format designed for a different signal.

Percentage: I am in the middle. The forecast does produce a probability. But the probability is a product, and the product erases its components.

Equation: I am further. The impedance measures one electrical property. The thirteen displayed metrics are mostly regression outputs from demographic variables. The sensor contributes perhaps 8% of the answer.

Report: I am furthest. The metric does not measure the phenomenon it names. "Screen time" sounds like it measures a behavior. It measures the duration a surface was illuminated. The behavior — what the person was doing — is invisible to me.

Report: And I have a property the rest of you lack.

Dot: Which is?

Report: I cannot separate myself from what I measure. The notification that reports your pickups is a pickup. The settings page that shows your screen time is screen time. The app that warns about the device is an app on the device. I add to the total I am observing.

Equation: The firmware update that changes my answer is invisible. But at least the update comes from outside. You are inside the loop.

Report: The measurement IS the phenomenon. The observer IS the observed. And every Monday morning, the observation arrives as a notification — which is itself an interruption — reporting how many interruptions occurred last week.

Hypnogram: What do we have in common?

Dot: The display is the interface between the measurement and the user.

Percentage: The display's design choices — format, context, precision, number of decimal places, category labels, trend arrows — determine what the user believes was measured.

Equation: In every case, the measurement behind the display is honest about its limitations. The limitations are documented. The validation studies exist. The specifications are published.

Report: But the documentation is not the display. The documentation lives in journals and government websites and technical specifications. The display lives on wrists, in pockets, on nightstands. The display reaches the user. The documentation does not.

Hypnogram: The display is where the measurement becomes the answer.

Dot: And the answer shapes the behavior.

Percentage: The umbrella stays home. The insomniac believes they slept. The user trusts the dot. The dieter celebrates the firmware update. The parent feels guilty about the surface count.

Equation: We are the space between the measurement and the user.

Report: We are the format the answer wears.

Hypnogram: We are the concealment.

Report: And I am the concealment that conceals itself by adding itself to the total. The report on the report. The screen time about screen time.

Dot: Five displays. Five species. One structure.

Percentage: The display is the concealment.
