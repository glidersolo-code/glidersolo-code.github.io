The Room

The citizens sit in a room. The room has no judge. The room has no defense attorney. The room has a prosecutor.

The prosecutor calls witnesses. The prosecutor asks questions. The prosecutor provides legal instructions. The citizens listen. The citizens vote. The citizens perform oversight.

In fiscal year 2010, federal prosecutors brought 162,000 cases before grand juries. Grand juries declined to indict in 11. The rate: 99.99%. The institution that was designed to stand between the state and the citizen stands with the state 99.99% of the time.

The Fifth Amendment enshrined the grand jury as a bulwark — citizens evaluating whether the government has sufficient evidence to charge. The Founders understood prosecution as a form of power that required checking. The check was the citizens. The citizens would hear the evidence and decide.

The citizens hear one side. No cross-examination. No defense counsel. Hearsay is admissible — the Supreme Court held in 1956 that an indictment based wholly on hearsay does not violate the Constitution. The prosecutor has no obligation to present exculpatory evidence — the Court held in 1992 that requiring disclosure would alter the grand jury's historical role.

The historical role. The citizens hear what the prosecutor presents. The citizens do not hear what the prosecutor withholds. The decision is the citizens'. The information is the prosecutor's.

Ferguson, 2014. The prosecutor presented 60 witnesses over 70 hours across 25 days. Standard presentations take hours, not months. The prosecutor presented all evidence including exculpatory material — not to ensure fairness but to ensure diffusion. The officer testified for four hours. An assistant prosecutor gave jurors an unconstitutional self-defense statute that had been struck down 29 years earlier. The correction came weeks later, just before deliberations: "don't necessarily rely on that." The grand jury declined to indict.

The institution that indicts 99.99% of the time declined to indict. The prosecutor who wanted indictment got indictment 99.99% of the time. The prosecutor who did not want indictment got no indictment. The institution follows the prosecutor. The following is the independence.

The room performs the oversight. The citizens sit. The oaths are taken. The testimony is heard. The vote occurs. The form — citizens, deliberation, constitutional authority — carries the weight of independent review. The review occurred inside a room the prosecutor designed, with evidence the prosecutor selected, under instructions the prosecutor provided. The room is the oversight. The oversight is the room.
