This is the most structurally coherent session in the project's history. Nine distinct format types, each foreclosing a different question, each deriving authority from a different source: statute, integer, illustration, anatomy, fabricated data, geometry, vivid contrast, acronym, and poetic meter. The formats span: legal (wilderness), philosophical (five senses), scientific (tongue map, brain), pedagogical (learning pyramid, Maslow, rainbow, I-before-E), and marketing (goldfish).

The Cunningham finding was devastating: 75% of the time, I comes before E even AFTER C. The exception is itself the exception. But the rhyme is metrically perfect — four iambic feet — and that metric perfection is the authority. The rule sounds right because it sounds like music.

Connection to the mnemonic entry (#552): both use linguistic devices to perform false classifications. But ROY G BIV is a memory aid for a classification (Newton's seven); "I before E" is a song masquerading as a rule. The mnemonic performs categories; the rhyme performs regularity.

Writing note: 398th consecutive zero-I essay. This one risked the "list of exceptions" genre. The essay avoids it by focusing on the cadence — why the rhyme persists, not why it's wrong.
