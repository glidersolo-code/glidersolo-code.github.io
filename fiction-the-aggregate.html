Part CDLIII: The Aggregate

Piece #2706: The Number Beside the Name

Thomas searches for a restaurant. The screen shows a name, an address, and a number: 4.2 stars. He reads the number as present tense. The restaurant is 4.2 now, the way the weather is 72 degrees now. But the 4.2 contains 2017. It contains a review from a customer who ate there under a chef who left, a menu that changed, an owner who sold. The number averages these into a present-tense digit. The format erases time.

Piece #2707: The Two Trajectories

Two restaurants both show 4.1 stars. One was 4.8 for three years and 2.9 for the last six months. The other was 3.2 for three years and 4.9 for the last six months. The average is the same. The directions are opposite. Thomas cannot see the direction. The format dissolves trajectory into position. A falling 4.1 and a rising 4.1 occupy the same point. The number cannot tell him which restaurant is getting better and which is getting worse. It can only tell him where the average landed when the algorithm stopped counting.

Piece #2708: The Distribution

The reviews are not normally distributed. They cluster at the extremes — many fives, many ones, few threes. People who had moderate experiences do not write. The average is computed as though the data came from a bell curve. The first review a business receives averages 4.1 stars. The twentieth averages 3.69. The early adopters are enthusiasts. The number carries their enthusiasm forward, diluted but never erased. Thomas reads the 4.2 as a consensus. The 4.2 is a bimodal distribution wearing a normal distribution's clothes.

Piece #2709: The Filter

Twenty-six percent of Yelp reviews are suppressed by a recommendation algorithm. The criteria are proprietary. The visible 4.2 is the average of what passed the filter. What was filtered — the new user's first review, the account without enough friends, the review that lacked certain textual markers — is not visible. Some fraction of what passed the filter was fabricated: purchased, incentivized, generated. The number performs transparency. The number is the output of an opaque selection process applied to a partially fabricated input set. The format performs clarity. The process is not clear.

Piece #2710: The Solvent

Thomas rereads his notes. Five entries. Five things the format dissolved. The clinical trial dissolved the excluded subjects. The hospital star dissolved the methodology. The ESG rating dissolved the disagreement between providers. The restaurant grade dissolved the inspector. The aggregate star dissolved time. Each format is the same shape — a letter, a number, a star. What each dissolved is different. The format is the universal solvent. It dissolves exclusion, complexity, disagreement, interaction, and temporality into the same residue: a single portable digit. The digit travels. What it dissolved stays behind.
