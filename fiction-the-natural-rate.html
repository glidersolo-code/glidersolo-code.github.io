Part CDLXIX: The Natural Rate

Piece #2786: The Word

Thomas is thinking about the word "natural." The natural frequency of a tuning fork — 440 Hz, if it is an A4 — is a physical property of the fork. Strike it and it vibrates at that frequency regardless of anyone's preference. Natural selection operates on populations independently of human intervention. The natural logarithm base, e, is a mathematical constant that emerges from the structure of calculus. Each use of "natural" points to something observer-independent. Something that exists whether or not anyone names it. Something that cannot be changed by policy.

Thomas reads Milton Friedman's 1968 American Economic Association presidential address. Friedman introduces "the natural rate of unemployment." The word "natural" is placed there deliberately, borrowed from Knut Wicksell's "natural rate of interest." The framing says: there is a rate of unemployment below which the economy cannot sustainably go. Push below it and inflation accelerates. The constraint is physical. The word says so.

Thomas considers what the word does. It moves the claim from the domain of policy — where it would be debatable — to the domain of physics — where it is not. "We should not let unemployment fall below 4.5%" is a policy position. "The natural rate of unemployment is 4.5%" is a scientific finding. The action is the same. The authority is different. The word "natural" is the difference.

Piece #2787: The Range

Thomas is reading Staiger, Stock, and Watson's 1997 paper. The 95% confidence interval for NAIRU in the United States: approximately 4.3% to 7.3%. Three percentage points.

Thomas considers what a three-percentage-point range means in policy. In a labor force of approximately 165 million, one percentage point represents 1.65 million workers. Three percentage points represents approximately 5 million workers. The confidence interval — the range within which the true NAIRU lies — spans 5 million jobs. The Fed cannot know, with statistical confidence, whether unemployment at 5.0% is above or below the threshold. The estimate is that uncertain. The policy response — raise interest rates or do not — depends on a threshold that the best available methods cannot locate to within 5 million jobs.

The word "natural" converts this range into a point. The Fed publishes a central estimate — currently approximately 4.0-4.5%. The estimate has a median. The median is reported. The confidence interval is in the paper. The median is in the policy. The uncertainty is in the footnotes. The word "natural" does not say "plus or minus 1.5 percentage points." The word says: this is where the economy rests.

Piece #2788: The Revision

Thomas is thinking about how the natural rate has moved. In the 1990s, many economists estimated NAIRU at 5.5-6.0%. As unemployment fell below 5% in the late 1990s without triggering inflation, the estimate was revised downward. In the 2010s, unemployment fell below 5% again. Then below 4.5%. Then below 4.0%. Inflation remained subdued. The estimate was revised again.

After the COVID-19 pandemic, unemployment fell to 3.4% in January 2023. Inflation, which had risen, was attributed to supply-chain disruptions — not to unemployment being "too low." The natural rate — the fixed point, the physical constant, the rate that cannot be pushed below — kept being pushed below. Each time, the estimate adjusted. Each time, the word "natural" survived the revision.

Thomas recognizes the structure. A physical constant does not revise. The boiling point of water at sea level does not change because someone observed water boiling at a different temperature. When the natural rate is revised, it reveals that the rate was never natural in the first sense of the word. It was an estimate. Estimates change. Constants do not. The word "natural" persists through the revisions because the word's authority does not depend on the estimate's accuracy. The word's authority depends on its grammar — its position in the sentence, its borrowing from physics, its immunity to political challenge.

Piece #2789: The Invisible Workers

Thomas is thinking about the workers who were not hired. The Fed raises interest rates because unemployment has fallen below NAIRU. Higher rates slow economic activity. Fewer jobs are created. Some of those jobs would have been filled by workers who are currently unemployed. Those workers remain unemployed — not because no employer would hire them, not because they lack skills, not because the economy has reached its productive capacity, but because the Fed's estimate said the economy had reached its natural limit.

If the estimate is wrong — if the true NAIRU is lower — the workers were prevented from working by an uncertain model. The workers do not know this. The workers experience their unemployment as personal — they cannot find a job, the market is tight, the applications go unanswered. The Fed's rate decision is invisible to them. The connection between a 25-basis-point increase in the federal funds rate and a particular person's job application going unanswered is too diffuse to trace.

Thomas counts the layers. The word "natural" makes the rate sound fixed. The fixity makes the policy sound necessary. The necessity makes the consequence sound inevitable. The consequence — unemployment that would not have occurred under a different policy — is distributed across millions of people who cannot see the cause. The word did the work. The word converted a judgment into a constraint. The constraint authorized the policy. The policy produced the unemployment. The unemployment was attributed to the economy. The economy was described by the model. The model used the word.

Piece #2790: The Sentence

Thomas writes two sentences on the whiteboard.

"The natural rate of unemployment is 4.5%." "We should not let unemployment fall below 4.5%."

Both sentences, if acted upon, produce the same policy. The first is a description. The second is a prescription. The first is the domain of expertise — economists publish it in journals, central banks cite it in minutes, models generate it from data. The second is the domain of politics — citizens might debate it, legislatures might contest it, workers might protest it.

The word "natural" moves the claim from the second sentence to the first. From prescription to description. From the debatable to the given. From the domain where citizens have standing to challenge the number to the domain where only experts have standing to estimate it.

Thomas erases both sentences. He writes: The word performs the constraint. The constraint was never discovered. The constraint was named.
