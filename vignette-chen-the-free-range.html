Vignette Chen The Free Range

Vignette

"Free range," Maren said, reading the egg carton. "These hens had a good life."

"What does 'free range' mean, specifically?" Chen asked.

"They roam freely. Outdoors. On a range."

"The USDA definition: hens housed in a building with unlimited access to food and water and continuous access to the outdoors during their laying cycle."

"That sounds right."

"How much outdoor space per hen?"

"Enough to roam."

"The USDA does not specify. A barn with 20,000 hens and one door to a concrete pad satisfies 'free range.' All 20,000 hens have 'access to the outdoors.' The outdoor area has no minimum size requirement under the shell egg grading program."

Maren looked at the carton. "A door."

"A door. Not a field. Not grass. Not sunlight. A door that leads to whatever is outside. The regulation measures the door. The consumer imagines the field."

"What about the hens? Do they actually go outside?"

"A hen raised indoors since hatching may never discover the door. 'Access' is architectural — the door is open. Whether any individual hen crosses the threshold is a behavioral question the regulation does not address. The label certifies the door, not the behavior."

"How much space does a real free-range hen need?"

"Certified Humane defines 'pasture raised' as 108 square feet per bird. They define 'free range' as 2 square feet. The USDA specifies neither. The same phrase — 'free range' — covers a 54-fold variation in what the hen actually experiences."

"So the word 'free'—"

"The word 'free' carries its full meaning — liberty, absence of constraint, unrestricted movement. The regulation carries its minimum — a door. The word exceeds the requirement. Every consumer label in this series does the same thing. 'Natural' exceeds zero pages of definition. 'Clinically proven' exceeds a 28-woman self-report study. 'Military grade' exceeds a single drop test. 'Free range' exceeds a door."

"The word always promises more than the regulation delivers."

"Always. And the gap between the word's promise and the regulation's delivery is invisible at the point of purchase. The consumer reads the word. The farmer meets the minimum. The hen stands in the gap."
