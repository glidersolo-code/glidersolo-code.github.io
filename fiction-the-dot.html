Fiction The Dot

Fiction

Part CDXXXIII: The Dot

Piece #2606: The Map

Thomas opens the registry website. Thomas types his address. A map appears. Dots — red, each one a person — populate the screen. Thomas counts seven dots within a mile of his house. One is two blocks away.

Thomas clicks the nearest dot. A name. A photograph. An offense: "sexual offense against a minor." A tier: Tier II. A registration date. An address.

Thomas does not know what "Tier II" means. Thomas does not know the offense was statutory — the person was nineteen, the other person was fifteen, the relationship was consensual, the conviction was thirty-one years ago. Thomas does not know the person has not reoffended in thirty-one years. Thomas does not know the actuarial risk is low. Thomas does not know the base rate: 5.3% rearrest for a new sex crime within three years. Thomas knows the dot. The dot is two blocks away. The dot is near the school.

Thomas feels informed. Thomas feels the information means something about his children's safety. Thomas does not feel that the information is incomplete. The dot is complete. The dot says: here. Thomas reads: danger.

Piece #2607: The Classification

SORNA creates three tiers. Tier I: 15-year registration. Tier II: 25-year registration. Tier III: lifetime registration.

The tiers are determined by offense of conviction. Not by individual risk assessment. Not by actuarial instruments (Static-99R, STABLE-2007) that actually predict recidivism. By the statutory category of the offense.

A person convicted of possession of child sexual abuse material (Tier I in some jurisdictions) may pose higher actuarial risk than a person convicted of a specific contact offense (Tier II). The tier system cannot detect this because the tier system does not assess risk. The tier system classifies offense. The offense classification performs the risk assessment the system did not conduct.

Thomas's neighbor is Tier II. The "II" communicates a middle level of danger — worse than I, better than III. The "II" is a product of the offense statute, not the person's risk profile. The number performs the assessment. The assessment was never conducted.

Piece #2608: The Bridge

In Miami, the residency restrictions are 2,500 feet from schools, parks, day care centers, bus stops, playgrounds, and school bus stops.

Thomas maps the restricted zones. They overlap. They cover virtually the entire city. The zones leave no legal residential address within Miami-Dade County for most registrants. The registrants must live somewhere. The registrants live under the Julia Tuttle Causeway — a bridge over Biscayne Bay.

The colony grew to over a hundred people. Sex offenders, registered, living under a bridge because the law that required them to register also prohibited them from living anywhere the registration could be verified. The registration created the homelessness. The homelessness undermined the registration. The system produced the conditions that defeated its own purpose.

The Supreme Court said this was regulatory. Not punitive. Regulatory measures can be applied retroactively. Punitive measures cannot. The distinction between regulation and punishment is the distinction between legal and unconstitutional. The classification — regulatory — enables the consequences. The consequences look like punishment. The classification says they are not.

Piece #2609: The Studies

Thomas reads the research.

Zgoba et al. (2010): Megan's Law in New Jersey. No statistically significant effect on recidivism. No effect on time to reoffense. No effect on number of victims per offense. The registry did not reduce sex crimes. The registry did not change behavior. The registry existed. The existence was the function.

Sandler, Freeman, and Socia (2008): New York. No reduction in sex crimes attributable to the registry.

Prescott and Rockoff (2011): registration alone showed a small deterrent effect. But notification — the public-facing component, the dots on the map, the community alerts — actually increased recidivism. The notification destabilized registrants' housing and employment. The destabilization increased reoffending. The prevention caused the thing it was designed to prevent.

Bureau of Justice Statistics (2003): 5.3% of released sex offenders rearrested for a new sex crime within three years. Compared to 68% rearrest rate for all crimes combined. Sex offenders have the lowest category-specific recidivism rate. The registry assumes the highest.

The assumption is not tested against the data because the assumption is the law. The law does not require evidence of effectiveness. The law requires notification. The notification is the effectiveness.

Piece #2610: The Notification

Thomas thinks about what the registry actually does.

The registry notifies. The notification reaches the parent who checks the website. The parent sees the dot. The parent interprets the dot as danger. The parent acts accordingly — or does not act, because acting on a dot that communicates presence without communicating risk produces no actionable information. The parent cannot assess the dot. The parent can only see it.

The notification performs prevention through visibility. The system's measure of success is the notification itself: how many registrants are registered, how many communities are notified, how many websites are operational. Whether the notifications prevent harm is a different question. That question has been answered: no. The answer has not changed the system.

The system persists because the notification feels like prevention. The feeling is the function. The dot is the protection. The parent sees the dot and feels protected. The protection is the feeling. The feeling was produced by the notification. The notification was produced by the registry. The registry was produced by the assumption. The assumption — that the population is uniformly dangerous, that notification deters, that presence equals risk — has been falsified by every major study. The falsification has not modified the system because the system does not measure outcomes. The system measures notifications.

The notification is the prevention is the notification.
