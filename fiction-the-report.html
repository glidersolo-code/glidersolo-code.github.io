Part CCXCVIII: The Report

Piece #1931: The Chain

Thomas considers the chain that produces the number.

A woman is assaulted. She decides not to call the police. No report. No record. No count. No number.

The crime occurred. The chain did not engage.

Another woman calls. The officer responds. Writes the report. Files it. The report enters the system. One count.

A third woman calls. The officer listens. Decides the complaint does not meet the threshold. No report. No count.

A fourth calls. The report is filed. The department reviews it. Marks it unfounded. The count subtracts. The one becomes zero.

Each link in the chain can break. Each break subtracts one from the number. The number that survives is the number that survived. It is not the number that occurred.

Thomas asks: what does a number measure when it counts only the occasions the system engaged?

Piece #1932: The Dark

Thomas reads Biderman and Reiss, 1967. "On Exploring the 'Dark Figure' of Crime."

Dark. Not absent. Not imaginary. Dark — present but unilluminated. The metaphor says: the crime is there. The light of the reporting system does not reach it.

Fifty-eight percent of violent crime. Seventy-nine percent of sexual assault. Sixty-eight percent of property crime. Dark. Present. Uncounted.

The National Crime Victimization Survey was built in 1972 to measure what the UCR could not. A survey that asks victims directly. The answer: more crime than the number says. Much more. Most of it.

Thomas thinks: the dark figure is not an error in the system. The dark figure is the system working as designed. The system was designed to count what it records. It records what survives the chain. The chain filters. The filter is the function.

The crime rate counts the light. Most of the room is dark.

Piece #1933: The Definition

Thomas examines eighty-six years.

From 1927 to 2013, the UCR defined rape as "carnal knowledge of a female forcibly and against her will." Eighty-six years. The definition excluded male victims. Excluded non-vaginal penetration. Excluded penetration by objects. Excluded non-forcible rape. Excluded rape by women.

For eighty-six years, the national rape statistic measured a subset and called it the total. The definition was the filter. The number appeared on the FBI's annual report under the heading "rape." Not "rapes matching our 1927 definition of rape." Not "rapes of females involving vaginal penetration by force." Just: rape.

The definition was changed on January 1, 2013. A single bureaucratic revision made visible what had been structurally invisible for nearly a century. The number changed because the definition changed. The crime had always been there.

Thomas holds two truths simultaneously: the definition served a real administrative purpose, and the definition erased real victims from the count that claimed to count them.

Piece #1934: The Incentive

Thomas watches CompStat.

Weekly meetings. Precinct commanders. Projected numbers. Red means up. Green means down. The room rewards green.

The incentive is to show fewer crimes. Not fewer victims. Fewer counts. The difference between fewer victims and fewer counts is the space where downgrading lives. An assault becomes a trespass. A robbery becomes a larceny. A rape becomes an unfounded complaint.

In New Orleans, rape victims seeking hospital treatment increased in the same period that reported rapes decreased. The event rose. The statistic fell. The gap between the hospital and the police report is the gap CompStat creates.

Thomas knows: measurement systems that reward their own decline will decline — not because the thing measured decreased, but because the measurement adjusted. Goodhart's Law applied to crime: when the crime rate becomes the target, it ceases to be a good measure of crime. The number improves. The neighborhood does not.

Piece #1935: The Fraction

Thomas holds the number.

312 per 100,000. Published by the FBI. Calculated to several digits. Presented as: how much crime there is.

It measures: how much crime survived the chain, passed through the filters, met the definition, was not unfounded, was not suppressed by the hierarchy rule, was submitted by a participating agency, and was divided by a residential population that may not represent the people actually at risk.

The fraction. Presented as the total.

Thomas thinks about what the number performs. The precision — per 100,000, three significant figures — performs completeness. The source — the FBI — performs authority. The format — a rate, comparable across jurisdictions — performs commensurability. The label — "crime rate" — performs measurement.

None of these performances are lies. The number is a rate. The FBI does publish it. The calculation is precise. But the precision describes the arithmetic, not the coverage. The completeness describes the format, not the data. The authority describes the publisher, not the phenomenon.

The number stands for the whole. Most of the whole is not in the number. The fraction presents itself as the census. The report presents itself as the world.
