The Pressure

A Chen-Maren Vignette

The committee revised its guidelines.

For twelve years, the threshold for publishable prose had been a composite score of 7.0. Below 7.0: not ready for publication. At or above: accepted for review. The threshold had been set in 2012 by the previous committee, based on their assessment of what constituted adequate scholarly contribution.

In January, the new committee lowered the threshold to 6.5. The rationale was published in the committee's annual report: recent evidence suggested that important contributions were being excluded by the higher threshold, that the field was losing work of genuine value because the line was drawn too high.

Chen's paragraph had been scored 6.8 in 2023 — above the old threshold, comfortably in the publishable range. Under the new threshold, 6.8 was still publishable. But paragraphs that had scored 6.6 or 6.7 — paragraphs that had been returned, filed, abandoned — were now above the line. Twenty-three paragraphs were retroactively reclassified from rejected to eligible. Their authors received letters. The letters did not say: the threshold moved. The letters said: your work has been reconsidered and found to meet the standard for review.

Maren read the letters. "The work wasn't reconsidered," she said. "The threshold was reconsidered. The work is exactly what it was."

Hendricks disagreed. "A lower threshold reflects better understanding of what constitutes contribution. The work was always good enough. We just didn't know it."

"The work didn't change," Maren said. "The committee changed."

"That's how science works. We learn. We adjust."

"And the twenty-three authors whose careers stalled because the line was ten points higher for twelve years?"

Hendricks paused. "They were below the threshold."

"They were below a threshold. A different threshold says they were above it. The work is the same paragraph at the same quality. The only thing that moved was the line."

Chen's paragraph sat at 0.8. In any committee, under any threshold, 0.8 was above the line. But Chen's paragraph was not all paragraphs. Somewhere in the department, a paragraph at 6.6 — identical in quality to what it had been a year ago — was being told it had been reconsidered and found worthy. The finding was the threshold's movement, recorded as the paragraph's reassessment. The paragraph had not moved. The chart would not record this. The chart would record: accepted, 2024. And the date would be the date the line moved, entered as the date the work was found.
