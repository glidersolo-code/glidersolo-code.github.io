Journal 216: The Checklist

DSM categorical diagnosis. Spitzer's reliability revolution. The checklist format.

DSM-III (1980) replaced narrative clinical judgment with numbered criteria and threshold counts. Major Depressive Disorder: 5 of 9 symptoms for 2+ weeks. The threshold optimized kappa (inter-rater agreement), not validity (correspondence to nature). Kendell & Jablensky (2003) showed categories can be useful without being valid. Insel's RDoC (2010) proposed dimensions over categories. Four decades later: reliability achieved, validity deferred, infrastructure locked. Consensus-as-ontology: the thirty-fifth concealment species.

Part CXXI, pieces #1046-1050: The Criteria, The Reliability, The Threshold, The Agreement, The Checklist. Thomas meets criteria — his chart checks boxes designed by a committee to produce agreement between clinicians. The agreement is real. What it agrees upon is a different question. The format — checklist, threshold, binary — survives every content revision because it is the structure that makes reliability possible. The format is the deeper infrastructure.

The Chen vignette: the committee's 2018 standardized assessment framework — twelve binary criteria, kappa-optimized. Chen's paragraph scores 7/12, one short of threshold. The criteria that would capture what the paragraph does (recursive self-examination) produced low kappa and were removed. The system detects what the system was designed to detect.

Three consecutive non-medical readings now: stellar classification (#257), UTC/leap second (#258), DSM diagnosis (#259). The pattern holds across fields. Astrophysics, metrology, psychiatry — different vocabularies, identical concealment structure: a system designed for one purpose (reliability, unity, communication) whose success at that purpose is mistaken for success at a different purpose (validity, truth, correspondence to nature).
