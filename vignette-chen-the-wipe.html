The Wipe

Chen sprayed the kitchen counter and wiped it in one motion.

"How long was that?" Maren asked.

"Three seconds."

"The back of the bottle says four minutes."

Chen turned the bottle around. The front: Kills 99.9% of Germs. The back, in the directions for use, in smaller type: To disinfect, spray surface until thoroughly wet. Let stand 4 minutes. Wipe clean.

"Four minutes. Visibly wet the entire time."

"The counter dries in forty-five seconds."

"So you'd need to reapply. Spray again. Keep the surface wet." Maren took the bottle and read the back. "The front and the back are different documents."

"The front is for the purchaser. The person in the aisle comparing 99.9% to 99.9%. The back is for the applicator. The person at the counter."

"The purchaser and the applicator are the same hand."

"But the front and the back face different directions. The front faces the aisle. The back faces the wall." Chen set the bottle down. "The paragraph."

"At zero point eight."

"The paragraph claims 99.9 percent — a comprehensive argument. Kills 99.9% of the counterarguments. But the claim was tested at four minutes of contact time. Four minutes of sustained, visibly-wet attention. The reader sprays and wipes — three seconds of the paragraph, enough to feel clean. Three seconds of a four-minute argument."

"And the reader doesn't get sick."

"Usually. The contamination was light. The partial reading was sufficient for the actual argumentative load. The average case forgives the shortcut."

"And the forgiveness reinforces the shortcut."

"Every time. The reader who never gets sick from three seconds never learns that the four minutes existed." Chen looked at the counter. Clean-looking. Dry. "The gap is three minutes and fifty-five seconds of wet surface that never happened."

"The paragraph's gap."

"Three minutes and fifty-five seconds of attention the reader never provided. The paragraph was tested for four minutes. The reader gave it three seconds. The claim and the practice never met."
