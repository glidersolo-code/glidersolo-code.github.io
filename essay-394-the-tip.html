The Tip

The server brings the check. The customer writes a number. The act performs generosity — a voluntary recognition of labor well done. The word "gratuity" comes from gratus: pleasing. The customer is pleased.

The server's rent depends on whether the customer is pleased.

The tipped minimum wage in the United States is $2.13 per hour. It has been $2.13 since 1991. In that time, the general minimum wage has risen from $4.25 to $7.25 — itself inadequate. The tipped wage did not move. In 1996, the president of the National Restaurant Association brokered a deal with Congress: the industry would not oppose the minimum wage increase if the subminimum wage stayed frozen. The freeze became permanent. Customer tips now account for 71% of a tipped worker's minimum wage. In Delaware, the employer pays 14.9%. The customer pays the rest.

The format: a line on the receipt. A suggested percentage. An option to write any amount. The format performs discretion. The content performs payroll.

The custom entered America as a Gilded Age import. Seven states criminalized it between 1909 and 1915 — tipping was seen as anti-democratic, a relic of European class hierarchy, demeaning to both parties. All the laws were repealed. But the custom mutated. After emancipation, employers in the hospitality sector hired formerly enslaved Black workers and refused to pay them a wage, forcing reliance on tips entirely. The Pullman Company paid its Black porters $27.50 a month — one-sixth of white conductor wages — and built tips into the pay structure to save money. The tip went from bonus to wage. The European courtesy became an American racial labor structure.

Michael Lynn at Cornell studied 2,547 dining parties. Service quality predicted tip size, but weakly — much smaller than generally supposed. Stronger predictors: bill size, alcohol consumption, whether the server touched the customer. For white servers, tips rose from 16.8% to 23.4% with service quality. For Black servers, tips remained at 16.6% regardless of quality. The meritocratic format produced a racial outcome. The gift discriminated.

Ninety percent of women in tipped positions report experiencing sexual harassment. Women in $2.13 states are twice as likely to face it. The dependency on customer approval for income makes workers reluctant to reject advances. The tip credit creates the vulnerability. The vulnerability is formatted as the customer's prerogative.

The rest of the developed world does not do this. Japan considers tipping rude. Australia pays $24.10 per hour with no expectation of tips. France includes service in the price. The customer pays once. The worker receives a wage. The arrangement is legible. Only two countries maintain the for-profit tipping system as primary wage mechanism: the United States and the Philippines.

The tip performs as a gift. The gift is the paycheck. The employer appears to employ. The customer actually pays. The format has not changed in a century. The subsidy deepens every year the tipped wage stays frozen. The word gratus — pleasing — describes an act of grace. The act of grace is a payroll function that no one authorized the customer to perform and no one authorized the employer to abandon.
