Part CCLXXV: The Repellent

Piece #1816: The Percentage

The bottle says 30% DEET. The one beside it says 98.11% DEET. The consumer reads: 98 is more than 30. More is better. The consumer buys the 98. What the percentage describes is concentration — how much of the liquid in the bottle is the active ingredient N,N-diethyl-meta-toluamide. What the percentage determines is duration. A 10% product repels mosquitoes for approximately 2 hours. A 30% product: 5 to 8 hours. A 50% product: 8 to 10 hours. A 98% product: 8 to 10 hours. The curve is logarithmic. It plateaus at approximately 50%. The consumer who paid more for 98% bought concentration past the plateau — additional DEET that adds negligible additional time.

Piece #1817: The Duration

The distinction: a 10% product and a 30% product repel mosquitoes equally during the period both are active. At minute one, 10% DEET on the skin repels the same mosquito with the same efficacy as 30%. The mosquito does not encounter a stronger barrier on the arm with more DEET. The mosquito encounters the same barrier for a longer period. The percentage is not the height of the wall. The percentage is the number of hours the wall stands. The consumer standing in the store, comparing 30 to 98, is comparing wall heights. The walls are the same height. The walls stand for different durations.

Piece #1818: The Linear

Percentages invite linear thinking. Thirty percent is three times ten percent. Ninety-eight is approximately three times thirty. The consumer's intuition: three times the DEET, three times the protection. The relationship is not linear. It is logarithmic — each doubling of concentration adds a diminishing increment of duration. From 10% to 20%: roughly 2 additional hours. From 20% to 40%: roughly 2 more. From 40% to 80%: perhaps 1 more. From 80% to 98%: functionally zero. The format — a percentage on a bottle — performs linearity because percentages are linear by definition. The biological response is not.

Piece #1819: The Sweat

Thomas sprayed 30% DEET before the hike. The duration: nominally 5 to 8 hours. He sweated through the first mile. The DEET dissolved in the sweat and ran off his forearms. He wiped his face — transferring DEET from forehead to eyes (it stung), removing the repellent layer from the forehead. Two hours in, the mosquitoes found his forearms. He reapplied. The "5 to 8 hours" on the label assumed dry skin, moderate temperature, no physical contact. His skin was wet, the temperature was 88, and his pack straps rubbed the DEET off his shoulders every step. The duration was theoretical. His arms were actual.

Piece #1820: The Wall

Two hundred and seventy-five Parts. The insect repellent bottle prints a percentage. The consumer reads it as a strength — a higher wall against mosquitoes. The percentage is a duration — a longer wall, not a taller one. Above 50%, the wall stops getting longer. The consumer who buys 98% has purchased the same wall duration as the consumer who bought 50%, at a higher price, with more chemical on the skin, for no additional protection. The number that matters — hours of protection under the consumer's actual conditions of sweat, heat, and physical activity — is not on the front of the bottle. The percentage is on the front because the percentage is the comparison number. The shelf is organized by percentage. The consumer walks toward the higher number. Thomas walked toward the 98% and carried the same wall height as the 30% next to it. The wall fell at the same rate. His arms itched at the same hour.
