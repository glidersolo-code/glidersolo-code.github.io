The Blower

The leaf blower has two numbers. They describe the same air stream. They cannot be added together.

CFM — cubic feet per minute — measures the volume of air the impeller moves. MPH — miles per hour — measures the speed of that air at the nozzle exit. A wider nozzle delivers the volume across a broader area at lower speed. A narrower nozzle concentrates the same volume into a faster, narrower stream. The manufacturer selects the nozzle geometry that optimizes whichever metric the box prints largest.

The tasks are different. Clearing dry leaves from a flat lawn requires volume — a broad push of air moving light debris across area. CFM is the relevant metric. Dislodging wet, matted debris from cracks and crevices requires speed — a concentrated jet breaking adhesion at a point. MPH is the relevant metric. The box prints both numbers side by side as though they are additive: 530 CFM and 180 MPH. They are alternatives. Optimizing for one degrades the other. The nozzle that delivers the highest MPH covers the least area per minute. The nozzle that delivers the broadest CFM coverage produces the lowest point-impact speed.

The measurement point is the third variable. "530 CFM" may be measured in the housing — the total air the impeller moves before the nozzle shapes it. Or at the nozzle exit. Or at a working distance. Each location yields a different number, and consumer packaging does not consistently specify which. "180 MPH" may be measured at the nozzle lip — peak velocity — or at 12 inches, where the stream has already dispersed. The consumer holds the blower 18 to 24 inches from the ground. The air that reaches the leaves has been reshaped by distance, turbulence, and ground interference. The number on the box described the air at the nozzle. The leaves received the air at the end of its journey.

No uniform standard governs consumer labeling. ANSI/OPEI B175.2 provides a test protocol. Compliance is voluntary. Some manufacturers report maximum values — peak readings at full throttle with the concentrator attached. Others report values at a representative speed with the standard nozzle. The "530 CFM / 180 MPH" on one box and the "470 CFM / 200 MPH" on the next may be measuring under different nozzle configurations, different throttle settings, and different distances. The consumer comparing the numbers is comparing measurement decisions, not machines.

Two metrics for one air stream, inversely coupled through a nozzle the box does not specify, measured at a distance the label does not state, applied to tasks the number cannot distinguish.
