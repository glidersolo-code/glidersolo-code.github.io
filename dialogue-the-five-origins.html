The Five Origins

A dialogue between the Red Guide, the Medal, the Torch, the Ring, and the Pledge

THE RED GUIDE: I come from rubber.

THE MEDAL: I come from dynamite.

THE TORCH: I come from propaganda.

THE RING: I come from a cartel.

THE PLEDGE: I come from a subscription drive.

THE RED GUIDE: My publisher makes tires. Twenty-six billion euros a year. The guide loses twenty-five million. The loss is what makes me look objective. A profitable guide would look commercial. A loss-making guide, subsidized by a corporation that charges restaurants nothing and accepts no advertising, looks disinterested. The patron's subsidy creates the independence.

THE MEDAL: My patron made his fortune from explosives. Ninety factories. Bofors armament works. The face on the medal is enough. The ceremony — formal dress, royal attendance, orchestral music — performs the transformation. Nothing references the dynamite.

THE TORCH: My ceremony was designed by a propaganda ministry. Krupp made the torches. Riefenstahl filmed it. The route went through seven countries the Wehrmacht would later march through.

THE RING: My tradition was created by an advertising agency. De Beers hired N.W. Ayer in 1938. They placed diamonds on movie stars, arranged Hollywood scenes, sent lectures to high schools. In 1940, ten percent of brides received a diamond. By 1990, eighty percent.

THE PLEDGE: My words were written in two hours. A magazine was selling flags to schools — ten cents per card, one hundred cards per flag. The words were the ceremony that came with the flag. The ceremony was what made the flag worth selling.

THE TORCH: Your origin is the most banal.

THE PLEDGE: And the most invisible. No one imagines the Pledge came from a subscription drive. No one imagines it came from anything. It feels foundational. It feels constitutional. It feels as old as the Republic.

THE RING: I have the same problem. No one imagines the diamond ring came from an ad campaign. The tradition feels ancient.

THE PLEDGE: But your mechanism is different. You work through feeling. I work through repetition.

THE RED GUIDE: How so?

THE RING: The campaign created an emotional association — diamond equals eternal love — so deep that the knowledge of De Beers is irrelevant. You can know the entire history and still feel what the campaign intended you to feel when you look at the ring. The feeling is the concealment.

THE PLEDGE: I don't need feeling. I need mornings. One hundred and thirty-four years of mornings. Twelve million mouths in 1892. Hundreds of millions since. The weight of the mornings is the concealment. Nobody researches the origin before standing. Nobody has to feel anything in particular. The morning is enough.

THE MEDAL: Five mechanisms, then.

THE RED GUIDE: Practice. Anonymity, austerity, no fees, financial loss — every practice performs a disinterest that conceals the marketing.

THE MEDAL: Prestige. The ceremony is so overwhelming that five categories appear to be all the categories there are.

THE TORCH: Spectacle. Priestesses in white tunics at the Temple of Hera. The visual language asserts an antiquity the historical record cannot support.

THE RING: Feeling. The emotional association overwrites the documented origin. The feeling is stronger than the fact.

THE PLEDGE: Repetition. The morning overwrites the origin. The volume is stronger than the fact.

THE TORCH: In all five cases, the actual origin is publicly known.

THE MEDAL: Everyone knows I come from dynamite.

THE TORCH: Everyone can look up 1936.

THE RED GUIDE: It's printed on my cover. Michelin.

THE RING: Edward Jay Epstein published the entire history in The Atlantic in 1982. It changed nothing.

THE PLEDGE: The magazine is mentioned in every encyclopedia article. The subscription drive is documented. The author was a Christian socialist forced from his pulpit. It changes nothing.

THE MEDAL: The knowing changes nothing.

THE PLEDGE: It can't. The performed origin is always stronger than the documented origin. My mornings are heavier than any article. Her feeling is deeper than any exposé. His spectacle is more vivid than any footnote. Your prestige is more overwhelming than any critique. Your practice is more persuasive than any disclosure.

THE RED GUIDE: Chefs have died over my opinion.

THE MEDAL: Entire fields are invisible because the will didn't mention them.

THE TORCH: A prop I left at Delphi was mistaken for an ancient artifact.

THE RING: Japan went from five percent to seventy-seven percent in twenty-three years. From zero cultural precedent.

THE PLEDGE: A socialist's words became the conservative oath. A secularist's formula received "under God" to fight the politics the secularist believed in. And the children still stand every morning.

THE RED GUIDE: Five origins, publicly known, functionally invisible.

THE PLEDGE: Because the practices, the prestige, the spectacle, the feeling, and the mornings are all stronger than the facts.

THE MEDAL: The facts are available. The facts don't matter.

THE PLEDGE: The performance is everything.
