Part CDXCVII: The Surrogate

Piece #2926: The Threshold

Thomas reads about RECIST — Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors. A partial response: 30% decrease in the sum of target lesion diameters. Thomas reads the origin. Moertel and Hanley, 1976. "The Effect of Measuring Error on the Results of Therapeutic Trials in Advanced Cancer." They asked: at what point can an oncologist reliably detect that a tumor has changed size? At 30%, the measurement error was small enough to be confident the change was real. Thomas reads the sentence twice. The threshold was not about the patient. The threshold was about the measurement. The point at which the clinician could see a change became the definition of what counts as response. The instrument's capability determined the standard.

Piece #2927: The Pathway

Thomas reads about the Accelerated Approval pathway. Created in 1992. Subpart H. During the HIV/AIDS crisis, patients were dying faster than conventional trials could prove survival benefit. AZT was approved based on CD4 T-cell counts — a laboratory marker — because waiting for mortality data was ethically unbearable. Thomas reads the logic: the urgency was real. The patients were dying. The surrogate was necessary. Thomas reads what happened next. The pathway expanded. From infectious disease to oncology. From oncology to cardiology. From cardiology to neurology. The crisis conditions that created the pathway did not apply to the new diseases. The pathway applied to them anyway. The precedent outlived its premise.

Piece #2928: The Fifty-Seven Percent

Thomas reads Davis, Naci, and colleagues. BMJ, 2017. Forty-eight cancer drugs approved by the European Medicines Agency between 2009 and 2013. Sixty-eight indications. At the time of approval, 57% had no evidence of improved survival or quality of life. Thomas reads: 57%. More than half. After a median of 5.9 years on the market, only 15% of those had subsequently shown benefit. Thomas does the arithmetic. Forty-five drugs approved without evidence they helped patients live longer or feel better. After six years, thirty-three of those forty-five still had no evidence. The drugs were prescribed. The drugs were taken. The drugs were reimbursed. The surrogates had improved. The patients — Thomas cannot find the data on whether the patients improved. The surrogates were the data.

Piece #2929: The Three Failures

Thomas reads three cases.

Bevacizumab. Approved for metastatic breast cancer in 2008 based on progression-free survival. The tumors stopped growing. In 2011, the FDA revoked the indication. Overall survival: no benefit. The tumors responded. The patients did not.

Rosiglitazone. Reduced HbA1c — the surrogate for diabetes control. Blood sugar went down. Heart attack risk went up by 43%. The surrogate pointed one way. The outcome pointed the other. The measurement improved. The patient deteriorated.

Aducanumab. Approved for Alzheimer's disease in June 2021 based on amyloid plaque reduction. The FDA's advisory committee voted 10-0 against. Three members resigned. The drug's clinical trials had failed to show cognitive benefit. The plaques cleared. The minds continued to decline. Priced at $56,000 per year. Biogen withdrew it in January 2024.

Thomas writes the pattern. The surrogate improved. The outcome did not. The drug was approved based on the surrogate. The approval was the evidence. The evidence was not the outcome.

Piece #2930: The Substitution

Thomas reads about what a surrogate is. A measurable marker that is expected to predict clinical benefit. Expected. Not proven. The expectation is based on a biological model: if the drug reduces the tumor, the patient should live longer. If the drug lowers blood sugar, the patient should avoid complications. If the drug clears plaques, the patient should think more clearly. The model connects the surrogate to the outcome. The connection is a hypothesis. The approval treats the hypothesis as a conclusion.

Thomas thinks about the five entries he has read today. The checkbox that constituted the maternal mortality trend. The model that constituted the teacher's effect. The definition that constituted the homeless population. The algorithm that constituted the danger score. The surrogate that constituted the evidence of efficacy. Five instruments. Five phenomena that the instruments claimed to observe but partly created. The instrument does not stand outside the phenomenon. The instrument is inside it. The measurement is not a window onto the world. The measurement is a part of the world it claims to describe.
