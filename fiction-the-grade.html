Part CDLII: The Grade

Piece #2701: The Window

The letter A is taped to the inside of the glass. Thomas sees it from the sidewalk. He does not see the inspector, the clipboard, the Tuesday in February when the inspector arrived, the 23-point spread between inspectors applying the same checklist, the fact that the most commonly cited violations are dirty ceilings and missing signage rather than the holding temperatures that actually cause illness. He sees the letter. The letter is sufficient. It was designed to be sufficient. The sufficiency is the product.

Piece #2702: The Spread

Two inspectors trained on the same manual, applying the same point system, arrive at the same restaurant on different Tuesdays. One scores 78. The other scores 95. The difference is 17 points — enough to span from a B to an A. Thomas considers: the letter is presented as a property of the restaurant, the way a temperature is a property of the air. But the temperature does not change when a different thermometer arrives. The restaurant score changes when a different inspector arrives. The letter attributes to the kitchen what belongs to the encounter between the kitchen and the clipboard-holder. The attribution is the concealment.

Piece #2703: The File

The full inspection report is available online. It lists every violation, the point value, the inspector's notes, the date. Thomas could look it up. He does not look it up. Ninety percent of consumers do not look it up. The letter satisfies the need the report was meant to serve. The summary replaces the evidence not by hiding it but by being more legible, more portable, more displayable. The evidence is public. The summary is more powerful than the evidence. Thomas walks in.

Piece #2704: The Score That Did Not Predict

Restaurants that experienced confirmed foodborne outbreaks scored 81.2 on average. Restaurants without outbreaks scored comparably. The letter did not predict what it claimed to prevent. The twelve most cited violations were matters of appearance: dusty shelves, cracked floor tiles, missing signs. The violations that cause illness — the chicken held at 50°F instead of 40°F, the cutting board used for raw meat and then lettuce, the cook who did not wash hands between tasks — these require observation at the right moment. The inspector visits once. The moment is Tuesday. The letter performs the entire year.

Piece #2705: Attribution

Thomas reads: attribution-erases-interaction. The grade attributes to the restaurant a stable property that emerged from an unstable interaction. The format erases the inspector the way a diploma erases the semester, the way a credit score erases the model, the way a hospital star erases the methodology. But each erasure operates through a different mechanism. The diploma erases duration. The credit score erases the algorithm. The hospital star erases complexity. The restaurant grade erases the encounter. What remains after erasure is always the same shape: a letter, a number, a star. What was erased is always different. The format is the universal solvent. It dissolves everything into the same residue.
