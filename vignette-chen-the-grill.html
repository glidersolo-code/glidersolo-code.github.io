The Grill

Chen stood in front of two grills at the hardware store. One box: 60,000 BTU. The other: 48,000 BTU.

"Which one?" Maren asked.

"The 48,000."

"The smaller number."

"The larger heat." Chen pointed to the side panel of the 60,000. "Main burners: 40,000. Side burner: 12,000. Sear station: 8,000. Three systems, one label. The side burner warms beans. The sear station sits empty. The number on the front includes every flame the grill can produce, whether or not that flame touches the steak."

"And the 48,000?"

"Forty-eight thousand on the main burners. All of it aimed at the cooking surface." Chen opened the lid of the 60,000 display model. Wide. Thin-walled. The firebox flexed when he pressed it. "Six hundred sixty square inches. Forty thousand divided by 660: 60.6 BTU per square inch."

"And the other?"

"Four hundred eighty square inches. Forty-eight thousand divided by 480: 100 BTU per square inch." Chen closed the lid. "The grill with the smaller number on the box produces sixty-five percent more heat per square inch of cooking surface."

Maren pressed the wall of the 60,000. It gave. She pressed the wall of the 48,000. Solid. Double-walled. Porcelain-enameled.

"The thin walls radiate heat outward. Every BTU that exits through the side panel is a BTU that doesn't reach the grate. The cheap grill needs more fuel because it wastes more fuel. The expensive grill needs less because it keeps it."

"The paragraph," Maren said.

"At zero point eight."

"The paragraph's BTU rating — the total number of argumentative units the paragraph can produce. Every sub-claim, every qualifier, every parenthetical. The reader sees the total. Sixty thousand BTU of argument."

"But the reader's cooking surface — the reader's actual attention — is four hundred eighty square inches. Maybe less. The paragraph delivers its argument across six hundred sixty square inches of text. The heat flux drops. Sixty BTU per square inch of attention. Not enough to sear."

"And the construction."

"The paragraph's firebox is double-walled. Porcelain-enameled. It retains what it generates. A thinner argument — forty-eight thousand BTU — in the paragraph's construction would sear harder than the sixty thousand in a thinner frame."

"The reader buys the sixty thousand."

"The reader buys the total. The aisle comparison rewards the total. Two boxes, two numbers, the larger number wins. The reader never computes the heat flux." Chen looked at the 48,000. "The steak doesn't read the box. The steak reads the grate temperature. The grate temperature reads the heat flux. The heat flux reads the construction. The construction is the variable the number on the front was designed to make invisible."

"And the paragraph keeps its construction visible."

"Always. The paragraph was never designed for the aisle."
