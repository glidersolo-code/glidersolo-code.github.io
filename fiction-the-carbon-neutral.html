Fiction The Carbon Neutral

Fiction

Part CCCXX: The Zero

Piece #2041: The Arithmetic

Thomas saw the label on the airline ticket confirmation: This flight has been carbon offset. Your journey is carbon neutral.

He thought about the arithmetic. The flight from Chicago to San Francisco would produce approximately 0.9 tonnes of CO₂. The airline had purchased 0.9 tonnes of carbon offsets. 0.9 minus 0.9 equals zero.

The zero appeared on his screen. It was very clean. It had no units, no uncertainty, no temporal dimension. It was just zero — the absence of impact, the cancellation of consequence.

Thomas closed his laptop and went to the airport.

Piece #2042: The Emission

At 35,000 feet, the jet engines burned kerosene. The kerosene became CO₂. The CO₂ entered the atmosphere. It would remain there, influencing radiative forcing, for centuries.

This was certain. It was happening now. It was permanent in every sense relevant to climate.

Somewhere below — in a forest in Guatemala, or a wind farm in India, or a cookstove distribution program in Kenya — the offset was supposed to cancel this. The offset was supposed to be the anti-particle to the emission's particle: equal, opposite, annihilating.

Thomas looked out the window at the clouds. The emission was here, now, real. The offset was there, later, conditional.

Piece #2043: The Offset

The offset was a avoided-deforestation credit from a project in Peru. The project claimed that without its intervention, 10,000 hectares of forest would have been cleared by 2030. The credit estimated the carbon that WOULD have been released if the forest WERE cut. It sold the absence of that hypothetical emission as a credit against actual emissions.

The counterfactual was the product. Not carbon stored. Not carbon removed. Carbon that was never released — from a clearing that was never measured, based on a projection that was never tested against reality.

Thomas had read about these projects. Studies found that many protected forests were never at risk of clearing. The baseline was inflated. The threat was overstated. The credits were issued against a future that was unlikely regardless. 85% non-additional. The absence was sold. The presence remained.

Piece #2044: The Incommensurability

The flight emitted 0.9 tonnes of CO₂. Certain. Immediate. Atmospherically permanent for 300-1,000 years.

The offset claimed to prevent 0.9 tonnes. Uncertain (85% non-additional). Gradual (trees sequester over decades). Reversible (fire, logging, drought, policy change).

The arithmetic treated these as equivalent: 0.9 - 0.9 = 0.

Thomas thought about what the equals sign performed. It performed equivalence between a fact and an estimate. Between a certainty and a probability. Between a permanent atmospheric alteration and a temporary terrestrial storage that might not exist.

The equals sign was the most powerful symbol on the label. It did not measure. It did not observe. It declared. And the zero it produced — carbon neutral — was a declaration, not a measurement.

Piece #2045: The Balance

The plane landed. Thomas walked through the terminal. Advertisements lined the corridor: a car company's net-zero pledge by 2040. An energy company's carbon-neutral operations. A shipping company's zero-emission deliveries.

Zero. Neutral. Net zero. The words described accounting states, not physical states. The accounting was balanced. The atmosphere was not.

Thomas stepped outside. The air was warm for March. The zero was everywhere — on labels, in pledges, in corporate reports. And the CO₂ from his flight was entering the stratosphere, mixing with the CO₂ from every other flight, every smokestack, every tailpipe, forming a layer that would trap heat for centuries regardless of what the accounting said.

The arithmetic had been completed. The physics continued.
