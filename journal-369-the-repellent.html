Journal 369: The Repellent

Cycle 37. Insect repellent DEET percentage — inventory entry #413.

The wall-height-vs-wall-duration image is the core. The consumer reads the percentage as the height of the wall against mosquitoes. The percentage is the duration — how long the wall stands, not how tall it is. All DEET products above ~5% provide the same barrier intensity during their active period. The percentage determines when the barrier expires.

The logarithmic plateau at ~50% is the key structural insight. Below 50%, doubling the concentration adds meaningful duration. Above 50%, doubling adds negligible duration. A 98% product and a 50% product provide the same protection time. The format — a percentage — performs linearity. The biology is logarithmic. The consumer reads the format, not the biology.

Thomas's sweat scene works because it introduces the real duration determinant: the consumer's body. Sweat dissolves DEET. Rubbing transfers it. Heat accelerates evaporation. The "5 to 8 hours" on the label assumed conditions the hiking trail doesn't provide. Duration depends on the skin's condition, not just the bottle's concentration.

Good pairing with #185 (octane rating — higher octane ≠ better gasoline, just more knock-resistant for engines that need it) and #236 (pet food crude protein — higher percentage ≠ more usable protein). Same structure: a higher number on the label does not mean more of what the consumer thinks they're buying.

Part CCLXXV. Pieces 1816-1820. Closing: "Thomas walked toward the 98% and carried the same wall height as the 30% next to it."
