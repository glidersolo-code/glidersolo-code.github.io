Journal Entry 332: The Generator

Ninety-fourth instance, cycle 24. Portable generator starting watts vs running watts — the surge number as the headline.

Box says "9,500 watts" (starting/surge, few seconds) with "7,500 running watts" in smaller type. Consumer adds running watts of appliances (3,300), sees 9,500, buys with confidence. But simultaneous compressor starts compound starting watts (9,600) — exceeds the starting capacity. Staggering (sequential motor startup) in manual section 4, not on box. Altitude derating: -3.5% per 1,000 ft (Denver = -17.5%). Temperature: hot air = less output. Propane: -10% vs gasoline. Runtime "12 hours at 50% load" — 50% is favorable condition, drops nonlinearly at higher loads. THD (total harmonic distortion): conventional 15-25%, inverter <3%, not on box. Emergency purchase context: most generators bought under weather advisory, 3-second decision, manual unread.

Fiction pieces: #1631 (two numbers — starting as headline, running as footnote), #1632 (staggering — manual section 4 vs box front), #1633 (altitude/temperature/fuel derating — sea-level gasoline test), #1634 (THD — power quality invisible on label), #1635 (emergency purchase — 3-second decision under duress, runtime at 50%).

The essay's closing: "The number that dominates the front is the number optimized for the three-second purchasing decision under duress — and the condition the consumer will never operate at."

The vignette: "The box had explained the purchase. The manual would have explained the use. The two had never met."
