The Flag

"How many prior arrests?" Maren asks.

"Three."

"How were they produced?"

Chen pauses. "One was a drug stop in a park with an undercover quota. One was a broken taillight that became a search. One was proximity — present in the apartment when they came for someone else."

"And in the algorithm, the number is?"

"Three. An integer. The integer does not record the quota, the pretextual stop, or the proximity. It records encounters with the criminal justice system. The system's intensity becomes the individual's risk."

"Age at first arrest?"

"Sixteen. School resource officer referred a hallway argument to juvenile court. Same argument at a school without an officer: detention. Different encounter. Same behavior."

"And in the algorithm?"

"Sixteen. An integer."

"The paragraph would say—"

"The paragraph would say the variable enters the algorithm stripped of its provenance. 'Three prior arrests' does not record that the arrest rate in this neighborhood was three times the rate in a comparable white neighborhood for identical offense rates. The count erases the cause. The integer performs neutrality. And the algorithm, reading integers, cannot see what the integers were made of."

"ProPublica found the false positive rate for Black defendants was nearly double."

"Forty-four point nine percent versus twenty-three point five. Among people who did not reoffend. Nearly twice as likely to be wrongly flagged."

"Northpointe said predictive accuracy was equal."

"It was. Among defendants scored a 7, roughly 60% reoffended regardless of race. Both claims are true simultaneously."

"Both claims cannot be satisfied simultaneously."

"Chouldechova's theorem. When base rates differ — 51% Black, 39% white in the Broward data — it is mathematically impossible to equalize false positive rates, false negative rates, and predictive value at the same time. The impossibility is a theorem. Not a bug. Not a limitation. A mathematical fact about prediction when populations differ."

"So the score conceals—"

"A choice. Which errors to tolerate. Which populations to burden. The decile score presents the choice as a measurement. The judge reads a 7 and sees risk. The 7 does not say: this number was produced by choosing to equalize predictive value at the cost of doubling the false positive rate for one group."

Maren taps the table. "Dressel and Farid. Untrained people from Mechanical Turk. Accuracy: 62%. COMPAS: 65%. A linear classifier with two variables — age and prior convictions — matched the full model."

"The 137 questions produce no more accuracy than two variables and a crowd."

"Then what do the 137 questions produce?"

"Credibility. The complexity performs thoroughness. The proprietary model performs rigor. The decile score performs precision. The pre-sentence report performs objectivity. None of it improves the prediction. All of it sustains the instrument."

Chen writes two numbers on the wall: 3 and 16. Three prior arrests. Sixteen at first arrest. "The fifty-fifth species," he says. "Provenance-erasure. The variable records the outcome but not the process that produced it. The format — a count, a clean integer — performs individual-level neutrality while encoding structural-level disparity. The algorithm reads the output of a system and reports it as a property of a person."

"Different from the coefficient."

"Different from #424. The eGFR equation put race in the formula directly. Here race never enters the equation. Instead, the effects of race enter through every variable the system touches. The coefficient at least named the category. Provenance-erasure conceals it behind apparently neutral counts."

The integers remain on the wall. 3. 16. Clean numbers. No footnotes. No context. No record of the system that produced them. Ready to enter any algorithm that will accept them as facts about a person.
