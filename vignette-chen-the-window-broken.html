Vignette

The Window

Chen has Zimbardo's original paper on the left screen and Wilson-Kelling on the right. The two documents describe the same experiment and draw different conclusions.

"Zimbardo saw inequality," Chen says. "Wilson and Kelling saw a broken window."

"They stripped the cause and kept the trigger." Maren pulls up Skogan's data. "The trigger became the theory. The theory became the policy."

"And the policy worked — crime dropped."

"Crime dropped everywhere. In cities with broken windows policing and cities without it. The crack epidemic ended. Demographics shifted. The economy improved. The decline was multi-causal. The theory claimed credit for a decline it didn't cause."

"But if the theory didn't cause the decline, what did?"

"Stop-and-frisk. 685,000 stops per year at its peak. 87% Black or Hispanic. Officers used minor offenses as pretexts for encounters — pat-downs, searches, weapon confiscations. The deterrent effect of constant police presence in targeted neighborhoods. The mechanism was aggressive enforcement, not window repair."

Chen stares at the data. "The theory described one mechanism. The practice executed another."

"And when the practice was ruled unconstitutional in 2013 and the stops ended, crime continued to decline. The practice was removed. The outcome persisted. The cleanest test of the theory's causal claim, and the claim failed."

"So what's the species?"

"Theory-launders-practice. The sixty-first. A theory provides scientific authority for a policy. The policy reduces the target outcome but through a mechanism the theory doesn't describe. The theory conceals the actual mechanism by providing a narrative that makes the practice appear scientifically grounded and racially neutral."

"The theory doesn't instruct racial targeting."

"It doesn't need to. It describes a mechanism — disorder causes crime — that is agnostic about who lives in the disordered neighborhoods. The racial concentration enters through the practice, not the theory. But the practice operates under the theory's authority. The press conference says 'broken windows.' The courtroom defense says 'quality-of-life enforcement.' The academic citation says Wilson and Kelling, 1982."

"Legitimacy laundering."

"A practice that cannot justify itself on its own terms — 'we are stopping hundreds of thousands of Black and Hispanic people based on furtive movements' — borrows legitimacy from a theory that describes something else entirely. The theory's neutrality is the laundering agent."

Maren closes both documents. "Harcourt and Ludwig found that Skogan's statistical foundation depended on five Newark neighborhoods. Remove them and the correlation disappears. One fragile finding in one crime type, carrying twenty years of policy."

"Five data points," Chen says. "And 685,000 stops."

"The ratio between the evidence and the practice it authorized is the measure of the laundering."

The screens go dark. The broken window that started it — Zimbardo's deliberate strike with a sledgehammer — was an experiment in social inequality. The theory that followed was an experiment in what happens when you strip the cause from the trigger and give the trigger a police badge.
