The Shift

Chen is grading papers at 2 PM and realizes she has been productive for about three hours today. She has been at her desk for six. The other three hours were: checking email, attending a meeting about scheduling, walking to the department kitchen twice, reading a thread about departmental politics, and staring at a paragraph she had already read. The three productive hours contained all of the thinking. The six total hours contained all of the being-at-work.

She thinks about the number. Eight hours. The standard. Where did it come from?

She knows: Robert Owen, 1817, a negotiating position. Ford, 1914, assembly-line data. FLSA, 1938, national law. The calibration was for factory work — physical, repetitive, measurable. The body bolting doors to frames has a fatigue curve that bends at eight hours. Her body grading papers has a fatigue curve that bends at three.

Maren would say: The eight-hour day didn't just measure the workday. It built the world. School hours are set to match it. Your daughter's school starts at 8 AM because factories started at 8 AM. Rush hour exists because everyone leaves at the same time. Lunch is at noon because the shift break was at noon. You eat dinner at six because the second shift started at six and the first shift ended. The whole day is organized around a number that was designed for a type of work you don't do.

Chen looks at the clock. 2:07 PM. She has been productive for three hours and present for six. She will stay until five because the department expects it, and the school picks up her daughter at 3:15, and her husband's shift ends at 4, and the grocery store closes at 9 (it used to close at 6, before retail extended to serve workers who couldn't shop during factory hours), and the whole scaffolding of the day depends on her being in this chair until five.

She is not productive from 2 to 5. She is not expected to be productive from 2 to 5. She is expected to be present from 2 to 5. The presence is the point. The presence is the shift. The shift was designed for a factory she has never entered, in a century she was not born into, and it holds her in this chair as firmly as it held the man bolting doors.

She grades another paper. It is 2:11.
