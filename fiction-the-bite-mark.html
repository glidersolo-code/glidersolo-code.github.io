Fiction The Bite Mark

Fiction

Part DIV: The Mark

Piece #2961: The Skin

The medium was never suited for the message.

Skin stretches. Skin compresses. Skin swells and bruises differently depending on the person, the location, the angle, the force, the time elapsed, the temperature, the blood flow, the tissue thickness, the age. A mark made by teeth on skin at the moment of contact is not the mark the examiner reads hours or days later. The mark has been edited by the medium. The medium is unreliable. The medium was never tested for reliability.

Thomas noted: no one tested whether skin could do what the practice required of it. The assumption was that a mark on skin records the teeth that made it, the way an impression in wax records the seal. But skin is not wax. Skin is alive. The living medium rewrites the message. The examiner reads the rewritten message and attributes it to the original sender.

Piece #2962: The Examiner

The instrument is a person.

A forensic odontologist examines the mark, compares it to the suspect's dental impressions, and declares a match. The declaration enters the courtroom as expert testimony. The testimony enters the jury as evidence. The evidence enters the verdict as fact.

The examiner's proficiency: 63.5 percent false identification rate. In some studies, 91 percent. Inexperienced examiners performed as well as board-certified experts. The expertise is not in the examiner's knowledge. The expertise is in the examiner's credentials. The credentials perform the science the method cannot deliver.

Thomas noted: the fetal monitor is a machine that amplifies through display. The bite mark examiner is a person who amplifies through assertion. Both transform uncertainty into certainty through format. The machine produces a paper trace. The person produces testimony. Both constitutions are invisible to the audience — the clinician reads the trace as data, the jury hears the testimony as science.

Piece #2963: The Thirty-Three Years

Keith Allen Harward was convicted of rape and murder in 1982. Six forensic odontologists examined bite marks on the victim. Six agreed: the marks matched Harward's teeth.

He served 33 years.

DNA testing identified the actual perpetrator: Jerry Crotty. Harward was exonerated on April 8, 2016. Six experts. One subjective judgment repeated six times. Thirty-three years.

Thomas noted: the repetition performed as confirmation. Six experts agreeing performed as six independent measurements. But the six were applying the same unreliable method to the same degraded mark on the same distorted skin. Six repetitions of a subjective judgment do not make the judgment objective. They make the subjectivity harder to see.

Piece #2964: The Persistence

Four independent scientific bodies rejected the practice. NAS (2009). PCAST (2016). Texas Forensic Science Commission (2016). NIST (2023). Fifteen years of rejection.

Bite mark evidence remains admissible in most US jurisdictions.

Thomas noted: the format survives its own debunking because the format is not a scientific claim. The format is an institutional position. The expert stands in the courtroom. The expert has credentials. The expert makes a declaration. The declaration enters the record. The record is evidence. The evidence supports the verdict. The verdict is the law. The law is the institution. The institution admits the expert. The circle has no point of entry for the scientific finding that the expert's method does not work.

Piece #2965: The Twelve Instruments

Thomas counted twelve.

The checkbox. The value-added model. The HUD definition. The AFST. The surrogate endpoint. The rape kit. The credible fear interview. The custody evaluation. The disability determination. The fetal monitor. The mammogram. The bite mark.

Twelve instruments. Seven modes. Two instruments within amplification now — the machine display and the human assertion. Amplification is the broadest mode. It operates wherever an uncertain signal is transformed into a definitive output through a format that performs authority.

Thomas wrote: the twelve instruments do not exhaust the modes. The modes do not exhaust constitution. But the twelve demonstrate the structure: every instrument constitutes what it measures. The constitution is concealed by the format. The format is the instrument's first product and its last defense.
