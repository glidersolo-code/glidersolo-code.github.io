Chen-Maren: The Stone

They are at a jewelry store. Maren's sister is getting married and they are looking for a gift.

"This one is F color, VS1 clarity, 0.71 carats," the jeweler says, sliding a ring across the velvet pad.

"What does F color mean?" Chen asks.

"Near colorless. The scale goes D through Z. D is completely colorless."

"Why does it start at D?"

The jeweler pauses. "The old grading systems used A, B, C, and they'd been used so inconsistently that GIA wanted a clean break. So they started at D."

"So D doesn't mean the fourth-best. It means the first-best in a system that skipped the first three letters because the first three letters had been corrupted."

"...Yes."

Maren is looking at the diamond through the loupe the jeweler has set out. "I can't see anything wrong with it."

"At ten-times magnification, the inclusions in a VS1 are minor. If you went to twenty-times, you'd see more."

"But the grade is based on ten-times."

"That's the standard."

Chen thinks about this. "So the definition of a flaw depends on the magnification you chose, and the magnification was chosen by convention."

"The convention has been consistent since the 1950s."

"Consistent doesn't mean natural."

Maren sets the loupe down. "The 0.71 carats — is that how big it is?"

"That's the weight. 142 milligrams. Size depends on cut. A well-cut 0.71 can look as large as a poorly-cut 0.80."

"So the number on the certificate is the weight, but I'm buying the appearance, and those aren't the same thing."

The jeweler smiles professionally. "That's why cut is the most important of the four Cs."

Chen watches Maren hold the ring up to the light. The stone looks exactly as beautiful as it looked before they knew any of this. The grades do not change the diamond. They change the price.

"We'll take it," Maren says.
