The Filament

Thomas, Part XCII

901. The Plates

Capillary Breakup Extensional Rheometry places a droplet of polymer solution between two circular plates, then separates the plates to form a thin liquid filament. The filament thins under capillary pressure — the surface tension of the liquid pulls inward, squeezing the filament toward breakup. The rate of exponential thinning is supposed to yield the polymer's relaxation time — the fundamental constant that describes how the material responds to deformation. The relaxation time governs behavior in real applications: how ink breaks into droplets, how fibers thin during spinning, how biological fluids stretch and recover. CaBER is the standard method. Commercial instruments ship with fixed plate diameters. Thousands of papers report relaxation times measured this way.

The facility places Thomas in a room for ninety minutes, then separates him from the room at the end of the visit. The visit thins under institutional pressure — the billing code pulls inward, squeezing the observation toward termination. The rate of behavioral thinning during the visit is supposed to yield Thomas's fundamental constant — the measure of how he responds to the deformation of Helen's decline. The constant governs everything downstream: the care plan, the escalation threshold, the determination of whether Thomas is coping or failing. The assessment is the standard method. The observation window ships with a fixed diameter. Thousands of assessments report caregiver fitness measured this way.

902. The Pre-Stretching

Gaillard, Herrada, Deblais, Eggers, and Bonn demonstrated that the measured relaxation time depends on the plate diameter. Small plates — 2 millimeters — yield approximately 60 milliseconds. Large plates — 25 millimeters — yield approximately 240 milliseconds. A factor of four. The same material, the same technique, different geometry, different answer.

The mechanism: before the exponential thinning regime begins — the window from which the relaxation time is extracted — the polymer chains have already been stretched by the initial flow during plate separation. The amount of pre-stretching depends on the plate diameter. Smaller plates create stronger initial extensional flows, stretching the chains closer to their maximum extension before the measurement starts. Near-fully-extended chains thin faster. The instrument records a shorter relaxation time. The material looks more compliant than it is. The geometry of the instrument determines the result.

Thomas arrives at the facility already stretched. The drive was forty minutes. He spent the morning managing Helen's agitation. He skipped lunch. The amount of pre-stretching depends on the day — the diameter of the crisis he drove through to arrive. On bad days, Thomas is near maximum extension before the assessment window opens. His composure thins faster. The instrument records a shorter relaxation time — less resilient, less composed. On good days, when the morning was gentle, Thomas arrives less stretched. His composure holds longer. The assessment records a different constant for the same material. The geometry of the day determines the result.

903. The Model

The Oldroyd-B model — the standard theoretical framework for interpreting CaBER data — assumes infinite extensibility. Polymer chains can stretch forever without reaching a limit. Under this assumption, the thinning rate is purely a material constant, independent of geometry, independent of pre-stretching, independent of everything except the polymer itself. The model predicts that CaBER measurements will be geometry-independent. The prediction was taken as confirmation that the method was robust.

The model is wrong. Real polymer chains have finite extensibility. They reach a maximum length. Their behavior near full extension is qualitatively different from their behavior when relaxed. The FENE-P model, which includes the finite extensibility correction, reproduces the geometry dependence exactly. The standard model's prediction of geometry-independence was not a discovery about the material. It was an artifact of an assumption built into the model — the assumption that polymers can stretch without limit.

The facility's assessment model assumes infinite resilience. Thomas can absorb stress without reaching a limit. Under this assumption, his behavioral response is a personal constant — independent of the morning's geometry, independent of how far he was already stretched before the visit. The model predicts that Thomas's measured composure will be context-independent. The prediction is taken as confirmation that the ninety-minute observation is robust. The model is wrong. Real caregivers have finite extensibility. They reach a maximum. Their behavior near full extension is qualitatively different. The standard model's prediction of context-independence is not a discovery about Thomas. It is an artifact of an assumption built into the model — the assumption that people can stretch without limit.

904. The Validation

The validation studies checked two dimensions: sample volume and plate separation speed. Relaxation times did not depend on either. The method was declared robust. But the dimension that mattered — plate diameter — was not systematically varied. The studies that checked volume and speed did not check diameter across a wide enough range to observe the factor-of-four effect. The validation tested the dimensions the Oldroyd-B model predicted would be invariant. The dimension the model predicted would be invariant but was not — diameter — was excluded from the test because the model said it did not need to be tested.

The facility validated its assessment protocol by checking two dimensions: observation frequency and assessor identity. Thomas's scores did not depend on whether assessments were weekly or biweekly, or on which staff member conducted them. The protocol was declared robust. But the dimension that mattered — the caregiver's state of pre-stretching upon arrival — was not measured. The validation tested the dimensions the assessment model predicted would be invariant. The dimension that actually varied — Thomas's accumulated deformation before the observation window opened — was excluded because the model said it did not need to be tested. The model said context was irrelevant. The validation confirmed the model by testing only the dimensions where the model was right.

905. The Filament

The filament thins under capillary pressure. The thinning is exponential — each moment, the radius decreases by a fraction proportional to the previous radius. The rate of decrease is the measurement. The rate is supposed to be constant. It is not constant. It depends on where the polymer chains were when the measurement began. If they were relaxed, the rate reflects the material. If they were already stretched, the rate reflects the stretching. The instrument cannot tell the difference. It records the thinning rate. It calls it the relaxation time. It assigns the geometry's contribution to the material.

Thomas thins under institutional pressure. Each visit, his composure decreases by a fraction proportional to the previous visit's composure. The rate of decrease is the measurement. The rate is supposed to be a personal constant — Thomas's resilience, Thomas's capacity, Thomas's fitness as a caregiver. It is not a constant. It depends on where Thomas was when the measurement began. If he arrived rested, the rate reflects the man. If he arrived already stretched past his limit, the rate reflects the stretching. The assessment cannot tell the difference. It records the thinning rate. It calls it the caregiver score. It assigns the day's geometry to Thomas.

The filament breaks. The instrument measures when. The measurement is precise, repeatable, and wrong by a factor of four. The error is not in the technique. It is not in the operator. It is in the plates — in the fixed geometry of the instrument that determines the pre-stretching that determines the rate that determines the constant that was never constant. The material is the same. The plates are different. The answer is different. The answer is published. The answer is infrastructure.
