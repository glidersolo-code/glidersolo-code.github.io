The Threshold

A Chen-Maren Vignette (II)

The department had a minimum enrollment threshold.

Fifteen students. Any section with enrollment below fifteen was subject to cancellation. The threshold had been set in 1987, when the department's operating costs, student-faculty ratios, and institutional budget structure made fifteen a reasonable minimum for financial viability. The threshold had not been recalculated. The university's costs had changed — facilities, technology, administrative overhead had all increased relative to tuition revenue. The student body had changed — more part-time, more online, more non-traditional. The threshold remained fifteen.

Chen's paragraph was taught in a seminar. The seminar enrolled twelve students.

"Below threshold," Hendricks said.

"The threshold was set in 1987," Maren said. "The assumption was that a section needed fifteen students to justify the instructor's salary plus overhead. The salary structure has changed. The overhead has changed. The definition of a section has changed. The threshold has not."

"Fifteen is the minimum."

"Fifteen was the minimum under 1987 cost structures. Under current structures, the break-even point might be ten. Or twenty. No one has recalculated."

The committee had not recalculated because the threshold had become infrastructure. It appeared in policy documents, in departmental guidelines, in the scheduling software that automatically flagged sections below fifteen for review. The number had migrated from a calculation to a rule. The rule no longer carried the calculation — it carried only the number. The number carried only itself.

Chen's paragraph had twelve readers. The twelve readers were engaged — attendance was high, discussion was substantial, the paragraph's density rewarded the kind of close attention that a twelve-person seminar could provide. The paragraph at 0.8 density was better served by twelve attentive readers than by fifteen who had enrolled to fill a requirement. The threshold could not see this. The threshold could see twelve. Twelve was below fifteen. Below fifteen was below threshold. Below threshold was subject to cancellation.

The threshold divided. The continuous reality — the gradient from no readers through sparse enrollment through adequate enrollment through overflow — was cut at fifteen, and the two sides of the cut were treated as categorically different. Twelve readers was insufficient. Sixteen was sufficient. The difference of four students was the difference between existence and cancellation. The threshold was precise. The precision was arithmetic. The arithmetic was from 1987.

Chen's paragraph continued at 0.8 density with twelve readers. The threshold continued at fifteen. The threshold was wrong in the way that a map of a city that has been rebuilt is wrong — accurate to its era, applied to another, and the distance between them was the width of the error that the infrastructure had absorbed and made invisible.
