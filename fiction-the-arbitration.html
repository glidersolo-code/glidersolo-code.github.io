Fiction The Arbitration

Fiction

Part CCCXLI: The Clause

Piece #2146: The Agreement

Thomas reads a contract. Page fourteen. Section 9.3: Dispute Resolution. "Any dispute arising out of or relating to this Agreement shall be resolved by binding arbitration administered by the American Arbitration Association."

"Agreement," the contract says. Thomas signed it. His employer drafted it. He signed it on his first day. He did not negotiate Section 9.3. He did not read Section 9.3. He needed the job.

Sixty million workers signed something similar. Not because sixty million workers chose arbitration. Because sixty million employers required it.

The word is "agreement." The word performs mutuality. Two parties, choosing together. One party wrote the clause. The other party needed employment.

Piece #2147: The Waiver

Thomas reads further. "The parties agree to waive the right to a trial by jury. The parties agree to waive the right to participate in a class action."

"Waive." The word performs volition. Thomas waived his rights. He chose to give them up. He exercised his freedom to relinquish his freedom.

In 2011 the Supreme Court ruled — five to four — that the Federal Arbitration Act makes these waivers enforceable. A statute written in 1925 for shipping merchants resolving cargo disputes now governs a fast-food worker who was told to sign or not start.

The class action waiver is the functional core. Without it, a company that overcharges thirty dollars from eighty million customers faces a $2.4 billion lawsuit. With the waiver, each customer faces an individual arbitration over thirty dollars. The legal costs exceed the claim. The rational action is to do nothing.

The waiver doesn't change the venue. It eliminates the claim.

Piece #2148: The Neutral

Thomas asks who the arbitrator will be. A neutral third party, the clause says. Selected by mutual agreement.

The arbitrator depends on the employer for future appointments. The employer appears in dozens of arbitrations per year. Thomas will appear once. The arbitrator who rules against the employer is less likely to be selected again. The arbitrator who rules for the employer builds a practice.

Employees win 21.4% of the time in mandatory arbitration. In federal court: 36.4%. In state court: 57%.

Against repeat employers — those who appear multiple times — employees win 16.9%.

The selection mechanism performs neutrality. The incentive structure performs loyalty. The arbitrator is neutral the way a hotel concierge is neutral: employed by the establishment, serving the guest.

Piece #2149: The Scroll

Thomas downloads an app. The terms of service are 47,000 words. He scrolls to the bottom. He clicks "I Agree."

He has agreed to arbitration. He has waived his right to sue. He has waived his right to join a class action. He has agreed that disputes will be resolved in Delaware. He has agreed to a sixty-day notice requirement.

He has agreed to all of this in the time it takes to scroll past a page he did not read.

Seventy-five percent of consumers whose agreements contained arbitration clauses were unaware the clauses existed. The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau found this. Not unaware of the details. Unaware of the existence.

The click performs consent. The scroll performs review. The format of the agreement — "I have read and agree to the Terms of Service" — performs a reader. No one read.

Piece #2150: The Courthouse

Thomas has a dispute. He was underpaid. He knows others were underpaid too. He wants to go to court.

The clause says no. The clause he signed on his first day. The clause he did not read. The clause his employer wrote. The clause that says "agreement."

The courthouse is still there. The courthouse was not demolished. The courthouse door was not locked. The clause simply says: not for you. The public forum, the jury, the precedent, the discovery, the appeal — all still exist. For other people. The clause is a private gate on a public road.

The Federal Arbitration Act was passed in 1925. Two shipping companies. A cargo dispute. Merchants of comparable bargaining power choosing a specialized forum.

Thomas is not a shipping company. Thomas needed a job. Thomas clicked "I Agree."

The word was "agreement." The word did all the work.
