The Checkbox

Maren's sister is an OB nurse in Memphis. She tells stories at Thanksgiving — the bleeding that wouldn't stop, the preeclampsia that came too late to treat, the transfer from a rural hospital two hours away. The stories are real. The women are real. Maren knows this.

"But the number isn't real," Maren says. She is reading MacDorman.

"The number measures the form," Chen says. "Not the phenomenon."

"In 2003, they added a checkbox to the death certificate. 'Was the decedent pregnant?' Before the checkbox, you had to trace pregnancy through the cause-of-death chain. If the certifier didn't write it, it was invisible. The checkbox was supposed to find the invisible deaths."

"It found them."

"It found them and also found women who happened to be pregnant and died of car accidents, cancer, overdoses. The checkbox doesn't ask whether pregnancy caused the death. It asks whether the woman was recently pregnant. Two-thirds of the deaths it flagged in 2016 weren't maternal deaths at all."

Chen thinks about this. A question that was supposed to reveal hidden deaths generating phantom ones. The instrument and the phenomenon entangled.

"NCHS stopped publishing the number for eleven years."

"Eleven years."

"2007 to 2018. The statistical agency looked at its own data and refused to certify it. During those eleven years, the headlines said the rate was doubling. Congress held hearings. Foundations launched initiatives. All based on a number the statisticians wouldn't stand behind."

"And your sister's patients?"

Maren is quiet. "My sister's patients are real. The woman who hemorrhaged on the transfer from the Delta — she was real. The disparity — Black women dying at three times the rate — that's real in every dataset, by every method. But the trend line, the 'doubling,' the 'crisis is getting worse' — that might be the checkbox talking to itself. Better detection looking like deterioration. The form changing and the change looking like a plague."

"The alarm is real and the alarm is an artifact."

"At the same time. In the same number. And nobody can tell you which part is which."
