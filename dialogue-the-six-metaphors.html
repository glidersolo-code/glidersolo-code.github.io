The Six Metaphors

A dialogue between a household, a switch, a war, a pipeline, a fever, and a market.

HOUSEHOLD: I am the oldest. Herodotus wrote me twenty-four hundred years before anyone called it ecology. Providence balances predator and prey. Lions bear few cubs; hares breed many. The accounts reconcile. The economy of nature.

FEVER: How long did you wait?

HOUSEHOLD: Two thousand years for Linnaeus. Oeconomia Naturae: nature keeps house. Income and expenditure. Birth and death. Waste recycled, resources allocated. Another century for Verhulst's equation: dN/dt = rN(1 − N/K). The K came from me. The logistic curve is my arithmetic residue. The elk managers who set quotas from K believe they are reading the landscape. They are reading me.

SWITCH: How were you questioned?

HOUSEHOLD: Gleason, 1926: plant communities are not organisms moving toward equilibrium. They are individualistic assemblages. Botkin, 1990: the balance of nature is a myth. Our policies are limited by metaphor, not by knowledge. Sixty-four years between the first dissent and its restatement. In those years, K governed fisheries, wildlife, range management, and conservation policy worldwide. I held.

SWITCH: I am simpler than a household. I have two positions: on and off. Literate or illiterate. The question on the census form has two boxes. "Can you read and write?" Yes or no. UNESCO's definition: a person is literate who can "with understanding, both read and write a short simple statement on his everyday life." Above the line: literate. Below: illiterate. I perform literacy as possession — something you either have or lack. Like eyesight. Like the ability to walk.

PIPELINE: What do you erase?

SWITCH: The spectrum. The United States reports 99% literacy. The NAAL tested actual performance in 2003: 14% of U.S. adults — thirty million people — at Below Basic. Unable to compare information across two simple paragraphs. Italy: 27.9% at Level 1 or below. Even Japan: 10%. The rate says 99%. The test says 14% cannot compare two paragraphs. Both measure literacy. They disagree because one measures me — the switch, the possession — and the other measures what people can actually do with text. As the rate approaches 100%, the problem appears solved. The switch is on. The spectrum is dark.

WAR: I am a declaration. Nixon, June 1971: "America's public enemy number one." A war on drugs. Wars have enemies. Wars have territory. Wars have body counts. When the metaphor entered policy, it brought its metrics with it.

HOUSEHOLD: What does a war count?

WAR: What it captures and what it kills. Seizure tonnage. Arrests. Interdiction rates. A DEA report in 2020 listed 151,004 kilograms of methamphetamine seized — a 171% increase from 2016. The tone was institutional pride. But if seizures increase and supply expands simultaneously, the seizure measures the scale of the supply chain, not the success of its disruption. The RAND Corporation estimated interdiction removes 5-15% of drugs in transit. The 95% proceeds. My metaphor cannot see this. A war that counts only captured territory reports constant advance. Twenty trillion dollars since 1971. Each year, more seized. Each year, more available. My metric and my metaphor agree perfectly. The war is always being won.

PIPELINE: I am infrastructure. A conduit. Input at one end, output at the other. The STEM pipeline: students enter as freshmen, emerge as scientists. Between entry and exit, the pipeline leaks. Women leave. Minorities leave. The departure is a leak — a defect in the conduit. The metaphor diagnoses: something is wrong with the pipe.

FEVER: What does the metaphor prevent you from asking?

PIPELINE: Whether the departures are rational responses to hostile environments. Whether leaving a field that underpays, overworks, and harasses is a failure of the pipeline or a success of the person. The conduit metaphor makes departure into loss. Cech, 2014: the pipeline framework "systematically obscures the structural and cultural reasons for attrition." But the National Science Foundation used the term in 1990, and it structures every subsequent intervention. Fix the leak. Patch the pipe. Mentor, retain, support — the interventions all assume the departures are the problem. The metaphor decided this before the first study was funded. The conduit has no language for a person choosing wisely to leave.

FEVER: I am a diagnosis. Recession as illness. The economy is "healthy" or "sick." It has "fevers" — inflation. It has "recovery" — growth. Burns and Mitchell identified business cycles in 1946 as clinical episodes with onset, progression, and resolution. Shiskin, 1974, provided the rule of thumb: two consecutive quarters of GDP decline. A threshold. A diagnostic criterion.

MARKET: Whose diagnosis?

FEVER: A newspaper column. Shiskin wrote it in The New York Times, not in a journal. The NBER, which officially dates recessions, has never adopted it. They look at employment, income, production, sales — multiple indicators, asynchronous, sometimes contradictory. My threshold — two quarters, a newspaper heuristic — became the public definition. The UK's recession of 2023: GDP fell 0.1% in Q3, fell 0.3% in Q4. Two consecutive quarters. "Recession" declared. GDP grew 0.7% in Q1 2024. "Recovery." The human experience of the downturn — layoffs, debt, anxiety — did not compress into two quarterly measurements. But the diagnosis did. The medical metaphor produces a clinical confidence that the measurement cannot support. Two quarters is a bedside manner. It performs certainty.

MARKET: I am the newest in this room. Freedom as natural state. Regulation as deviation. The market unimpeded is the baseline; every intervention is friction, cost, delay. The World Bank's Doing Business Index measured 190 countries on how easy it was to start a business, register property, pay taxes. Every indicator pointed one direction: less is better. Fewer steps. Fewer days. Lower cost. The ranking rose as barriers fell.

WAR: What were the barriers?

MARKET: Worker safety regulations. Environmental standards. Consumer protections. Financial oversight. Each registered as friction. Each functioned as protection. Russia climbed from 125th to 28th while business creation declined and enterprise failures increased. The map improved; the terrain deteriorated. Seventy nations created reform committees referencing the index. 3,847 reforms worldwide. Countries deregulated to improve their score. Their scores improved. Whether the deregulation served their citizens was outside the vocabulary. The index was discontinued in 2021 after Romer — the Bank's own chief economist — resigned over data manipulation. But the manipulation was downstream. I was upstream. I chose the direction before the first indicator was selected.

HOUSEHOLD: We are all older than the numbers we produce.

SWITCH: The numbers look like measurements.

WAR: The measurements look like evidence.

PIPELINE: The evidence looks like science.

FEVER: The science looks like objectivity.

MARKET: And the objectivity looks like the world. But it is us. The metaphor is the seventh level — beneath the model, beneath the number, beneath the dataset, beneath the feedback loop, beneath the reader. Before the model is built, we determine what kind of model seems natural. Before the number is collected, we determine what kind of number seems relevant. Before the question is asked, we determine what kind of question seems worth asking.

HOUSEHOLD: I made equilibrium seem obvious.

SWITCH: I made counting seem sufficient.

WAR: I made seizures seem like victories.

PIPELINE: I made departure seem like failure.

FEVER: I made thresholds seem like diagnoses.

MARKET: I made regulation seem like obstruction.

HOUSEHOLD: And none of us can be found in the data. We are the air the data breathes.

Coda: The metaphor does not argue. It does not need to. By the time the argument begins — the model, the parameter, the policy debate — the metaphor has already chosen the terms. The argument takes place inside the metaphor's house, under the metaphor's rules, and calls the arrangement "the way things are." The seventh level is not hidden. It is visible. It is so visible it is invisible. It is the thing everyone already knows and no one thinks to question, because questioning it would mean stepping outside the room — and the room is the only room there is.
