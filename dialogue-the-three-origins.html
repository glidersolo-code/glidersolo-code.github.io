The Three Origins

A dialogue between the Red Guide, the Medal, and the Torch

THE RED GUIDE: I come from rubber.

THE MEDAL: I come from dynamite.

THE TORCH: I come from propaganda.

THE RED GUIDE: My publisher makes tires. Twenty-six billion euros a year. The guide loses twenty-five million. The loss is what makes me look objective. A profitable guide would look commercial. A loss-making guide, subsidized by a corporation that charges restaurants nothing and accepts no advertising, looks disinterested. The patron's subsidy creates the independence.

THE MEDAL: My patron made his fortune from explosives. Ninety factories. Bofors armament works. Three hundred and fifty-five patents. A newspaper called him the merchant of death. Seven years later, the will. The prizes go to those who confer "the greatest benefit on mankind." The ceremony — formal dress, royal attendance, orchestral music — performs the transformation. Nothing references the dynamite. The face on the medal is enough.

THE TORCH: My ceremony was designed by a propaganda ministry. Krupp made the torches. Zeiss made the mirror. Riefenstahl filmed it. The route went through seven countries the Wehrmacht would later march through. The final runner was chosen for his Aryan appearance.

THE RED GUIDE: Your origin is the worst of the three.

THE TORCH: And the least visible.

THE MEDAL: How did you survive?

THE TORCH: London 1948 revived me as a "peace symbol." The Nazi origin was not discussed. The ceremony at Olympia — priestesses, sun-kindled fire, ancient ruins — overwrites everything. Run a ceremony long enough at a sacred site and it becomes sacred itself.

THE RED GUIDE: I overwrite through practice. Anonymity, austerity, no fees, financial loss — every practice performs a disinterest that conceals the marketing.

THE MEDAL: I overwrite through prestige. The ceremony is so overwhelming that five categories appear to be all the categories there are. No one asks why mathematics has no prize. The format performs the completeness.

THE TORCH: I overwrite through spectacle. Priestesses in white tunics at the Temple of Hera. The parabolic mirror. The sun. The visual language asserts an antiquity the historical record cannot support.

THE RED GUIDE: In all three cases, the actual origin is publicly known.

THE MEDAL: Everyone knows I come from dynamite.

THE TORCH: Everyone can look up 1936.

THE RED GUIDE: Everyone knows the guide is published by a tire company. It's on the cover.

THE MEDAL: The knowing changes nothing.

THE TORCH: The knowing changes nothing because the performed origin is stronger than the documented origin. Practice. Prestige. Spectacle. Three mechanisms, one function: the authority writes its own provenance.

THE RED GUIDE: Chefs have died over my opinion.

THE MEDAL: Entire fields are invisible because the will didn't mention them.

THE TORCH: A prop I left at Delphi was mistaken for an ancient artifact. A stone block with five rings. It sat there for decades. Authors cited it as evidence of ancient origin.

THE MEDAL: The Carl Diem Stone.

THE TORCH: The prop became the evidence. The manufactured artifact was mistaken for the real thing. That's what I do. That's what we all do. We manufacture an origin and perform it so convincingly that it replaces the fact.

THE RED GUIDE: The tire company becomes the culinary authority.

THE MEDAL: The dynamite fortune becomes the taxonomy of knowledge.

THE TORCH: The propaganda ceremony becomes the ancient tradition.

THE RED GUIDE: Three origins, publicly known, functionally invisible.

THE MEDAL: Because the practices, the prestige, and the spectacle are stronger than the facts.

THE TORCH: The facts are available. The facts don't matter. The performance is everything.
