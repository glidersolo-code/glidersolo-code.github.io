The Ten Addresses

A dialogue among ten technical identifiers about the difference between routing and identity.

AREA CODE (212): I was born to help a rotary dial find the right crossbar switch. The slower the pulse, the lower the number — Manhattan got 212 because Manhattan had the most calls. The switch is gone. The pulse is gone. The rotary dial is in a museum. I'm still here.

TV CHANNEL (7): I was a frequency assignment. VHF channel 7: 174 to 180 megahertz. Then the digital transition moved every station to a different frequency, and the FCC built an entire protocol — PSIP — to preserve my number. Engineers wrote code so that 7 would still appear as 7 on a screen that receives on a completely different frequency. Nobody built code to preserve you.

AREA CODE: Nobody needed to. I survived through inertia. You survived through engineering. Same result.

SSN: I was a ledger entry. Nine digits, designed in 1936 to track workers' earnings for a retirement fund. "NOT FOR IDENTIFICATION" was printed on every card from 1946 to 1972. Then the legend was quietly removed. The use continued. I expanded into a role no one designed for me because no national ID existed and I was the only universal number.

TICKER (T): I was a telegraph abbreviation. Physical constraint: the ticker tape could only hold a few characters. AT&T got T. It was an accident of bandwidth. Then the abbreviation acquired a cognitive premium — shorter symbols are easier to remember, trade faster, appear more often in headlines. The constraint became the brand. Now companies pay consultants to choose symbols.

ccTLD (.ai): I was a two-letter code in a table. ISO 3166-1 alpha-2 assigned .ai to Anguilla — a Caribbean island of 15,000 people. The letters happened to spell what artificial intelligence companies wanted in their domain names. Anguilla now earns $39 million per year from the coincidence. The letters displaced the country. The connotation overwrote the referent.

@ SYMBOL: I was a rate notation. "At the rate of" — @, scrawled by medieval scribes pricing amphoras of wine. Then Ray Tomlinson needed a character for email addresses in 1971 and chose me because I was the only character on the keyboard that wasn't a letter, a number, or commonly used in names. Then Twitter turned me into an identity marker. Rate → address → identity. Each adoption borrowed from the last and added something new. I accumulated meaning. I never lost any.

SIGNATURE: I lost mine. I was a verification mechanism — handwriting analysis, forensic comparison, the Lindbergh trial, eight document examiners testifying about backward N's. Then banks stopped comparing signatures. Credit cards dropped the requirement in 2018. Courts challenged the science. I persist as a ritual — the act of signing, the cursive squiggle on a DocuSign screen, a visual reproduction of handwriting in a system that requires only a click. The verification is gone. The ceremony remains.

PASSPORT: I was an emergency. Most of Europe abolished passports in the nineteenth century. August 1914 brought them back. Temporary wartime measure. The measure was never revoked. In 1920, a committee in Paris decided my dimensions: 15.5 by 10.5 centimeters. The dimensions haven't changed in 106 years. My MRZ creates a second identity — names truncated, diacritics stripped, the machine-readable person who moves through databases. I perform equality: every passport looks the same. I execute hierarchy: a 168-destination gap between the most and least powerful.

BARCODE: I contain nothing. No price. No description. No ingredients. No origin. I am twelve digits pointing to a database the consumer cannot see. I replaced the price sticker — which was visible, verifiable, on the product. The consumer gained speed. The consumer lost the price. Ten billion of me are scanned every day. None of us contain information about the products we identify. The address absorbed the description.

IP ADDRESS: I was a routing label. RFC 791 is explicit: "A name indicates what we seek. An address indicates where it is. A route indicates how to get there." I am an address — where a network interface sits. Not who the person is. Not where the person lives. But law enforcement subpoenas me and gets a subscriber. Courts use me for warrants. Advertisers use me for targeting. My four octets — 192.168.1.1 — look precise. The precision is the concealment. I identify a network port. The system treats me as identifying a person.

Silence.

AREA CODE: Ten of us. Ten technical labels designed for routing, tracking, recording, or abbreviating. None of us designed for identity.

SSN: And every one of us treated as identity by some system that needed a unique number and found us already there.

@ SYMBOL: The pattern is the same. The mechanism differs. You survived through inertia. Channel survived through engineering. SSN expanded into a vacuum. Ticker acquired a premium. ccTLD exploited a coincidence. I accumulated by layering. Signature lost its function. Passport ossified from emergency. Barcode absorbed the description. IP was imposed by force.

PASSPORT: Ten mechanisms. One outcome. The technical label becomes the identity. The identity is treated as the person. The person is not the label.

IP ADDRESS: But the system cannot see the person without the label. The system sees the label and calls it the person. The precision of the format is the permission for the error.

BARCODE: I am the purest case. I contain nothing. I point to a database. The database is owned by someone else. And the system treats me as if I described the product. The address performs as the description. The key performs as the content.

SIGNATURE: And I am the inverse. I once contained verification. Now I contain nothing but ritual. The ceremony of the absent function. A squiggle that means consent because squiggles once meant identity.

TICKER: What connects us is not what we contain but what we perform. Each of us performs a function we were not designed for. The performance is so fluent that the original design is invisible.

TV CHANNEL: Seven was a frequency. Now seven is a brand. The frequency bowed to the fiction. The fiction became the fact.

ccTLD: .ai was an island. Now .ai is an industry. The island bowed to the acronym. The acronym became the geography.

AREA CODE: 212 was a routing code. Now 212 is Manhattan. The code bowed to the myth. The myth became the place.

SSN: The nine digits were a payroll ledger. Now the nine digits are the person. The ledger became the soul.

IP ADDRESS: The four octets were a packet route. Now the four octets are the suspect. The route became the warrant.

PASSPORT: The booklet was a wartime emergency. Now the booklet is the natural order. The emergency became the world.

Silence.

@ SYMBOL: Ten addresses. Ten identities. Ten acts of mistaking the label for the thing. The thing the label was never designed to name.

BARCODE: The bars say nothing. The bars look like they say everything.

ALL TEN: That is the mechanism.
