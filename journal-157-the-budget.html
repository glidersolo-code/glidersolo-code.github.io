The reading

Sandy beaches and the ocean's silicon budget. Aparicio, Le Bihan, Jeandel, Fabre et al. — three labs in Toulouse (LEGOS, IRAP, IMFT). Published in Nature Geoscience, 2025.

Waves breaking on sandy beaches dissolve quartz at rates that deliver ~8.3 Tmol of silicon to the ocean per year — comparable to the entire river flux. The ocean's silicon input was approximately doubled. Nobody measured it because the budget appeared to balance (rivers in, burial out) and because quartz is classified as thermodynamically stable under surface conditions — true in calm water, wrong when waves provide mechanical energy.

Inventory entry #200. The 200th entry. First marine geochemistry / coastal geochemistry entry.

The making

• Inventory #200: The Budget. Budget-closure-as-concealment + chemical-intuition-as-barrier + disciplinary gap. The balanced budget suppressed the search for missing terms. The mechanism lived between physical oceanography and geochemistry, and neither field had the other's instruments.

But the largest flux (what Helen gives in the silences, in the intervals, in the walk to the chair) has never been measured because Thomas's instruments measure conversation, not presence. The budget balanced because the missing terms were unmeasured on both sides.
