The Residual

Maren's mother taught fourth grade for twenty-seven years in a suburb of Memphis. She retired in 2014, the year Tennessee expanded its value-added evaluation system statewide.

"She got out just in time," Maren says. She is reading the ASA statement.

"One to fourteen percent," Chen says.

"That's the upper bound. Teachers account for at most fourteen percent of test score variability. The rest — eighty-six percent at minimum — is everything else. Family income. Prior learning. Whether the kid ate breakfast. Whether the kid's parents are getting divorced. Whether there's construction in the hallway. The model can't measure any of that. So it attributes the residual to the teacher."

"The residual is not the teacher."

"The residual is everything the model doesn't know. But the model needs to attribute it somewhere. The teacher is the unit of analysis. So the teacher gets the residual."

Chen reads about the stability. Year-to-year correlations of 0.22 to 0.67. A teacher rated in the top quintile one year has a ten to fifteen percent chance of falling to the bottom quintile the next.

"The same teacher," Maren says. "Different students, different year, different number. Change the model specification — same students, same scores — and up to forty-one percent of rankings shift by three or more deciles. The number is not measuring the teacher. The number is measuring the model's assumptions."

"And they published the numbers with names."

"The LA Times. Teacher by teacher. Parents could look up their child's teacher and see: more effective, average, less effective. No confidence intervals. No instability disclosure. Just the name and the number."

Chen is quiet for a moment. "The model came from cattle."

"William Sanders. Agricultural statistician. Mixed-model methodology for partitioning weight gain in livestock. He applied it to children in 1982. The production metaphor isn't a metaphor — it's the literal origin. Value added. A manufacturing term. Raw material goes in. A process acts on it. The material comes out worth more. The child is the material. The teacher is the process. The test score is the value."

"And the residual is the effect."

"In cattle, the residual is the treatment effect — because you can control the other variables. You can control the feed, the breed, the pen. In a classroom, you can't control anything. You can't control whether a student's parent lost their job in October. You can't control whether the student was up until two in the morning because the neighbors were fighting. All of that goes into the residual. And the residual goes onto the teacher's record. And the record goes into the newspaper."

Maren pauses. "Rigoberto Ruelas. Fifth grade, Miramonte Elementary, South LA. Drove students to school when their parents couldn't. The model rated him 'less effective.' He was found dead in September 2010."

The room is quiet.

"The partition was not the teacher," Chen says.

"The partition was everything the model didn't know, wearing the teacher's name."
