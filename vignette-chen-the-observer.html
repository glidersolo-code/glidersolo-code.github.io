The Observer

Chen-Maren vignette

Chen reads the paragraph aloud. Thirty-nine lines. The committee evaluates it using a rubric — five dimensions, each scored by five readers, the scores averaged. The rubric was developed at a workshop in 2019 by twelve faculty members who read a selection of sample paragraphs and calibrated their responses until the inter-rater reliability exceeded 0.85. Those twelve people are the committee's standard observer. Every paragraph submitted since has been evaluated relative to their averaged perception.

Maren has been reading about the CIE 1931 standard observer. Seventeen people in London and Teddington, 1928-1931. Adjusted three lights until the color matched. Their averaged responses define color for the entire world. Every LED specification, every monitor calibration, every paint standard — all referencing the perception of seventeen young adults who have been dead for decades. The functions are known to be wrong below 460 nanometers. The correction was published in 2015. The correction is not used because every instrument's firmware was built on the 1931 functions.

Professor Wallace scores the paragraph a 3 out of 5 on "structural coherence." This is the same score he gave last semester. The same score Professor Liu gave. The rubric's color matching functions were calibrated in 2019, and the paragraph's spectral distribution — its particular arrangement of argument, evidence, and implication — falls in a region where the rubric is flat. A paragraph with different structural logic but the same surface features would receive the same score. Chen's paragraph and a paragraph it does not resemble are metamers: indistinguishable to the standard, different to any reader using a different set of functions.

Professor Hendricks uses different functions. Her reading is peripheral — she notices what the rubric's foveal aperture misses: the rhythm of the paragraph's clause structure, the way Chen's argument accelerates past the transition at line twenty-three, the silence between the two longest sentences where the reader's comprehension must bridge a gap the syntax does not close. Hendricks would score the paragraph differently. Hendricks is not the standard observer. The rubric does not incorporate peripheral reading.

The committee discussed updating the rubric last year. The chair noted that three years of assessment data, two hiring decisions, and one program review had been conducted using the current version. To switch would require recalibrating every prior evaluation, retraining all readers, and revising the program review documentation. The correction was acknowledged. The infrastructure prevented it.

Chen's paragraph is measured by twelve people from 2019 who are not reading it now. By a rubric calibrated to a 2° aperture of reading that misses the peripheral structure where the paragraph's actual force resides. By scoring functions with known errors in the region where Chen's paragraph operates. The paragraph and a lesser paragraph score the same. The score is correct relative to the standard. The standard is not correct relative to the paragraph.
