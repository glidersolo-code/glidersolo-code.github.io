Vignette: The Arbitration

Chen is reading a contract. She has a red pen. She draws a circle on page fourteen.

"Section 9.3," she says. "Dispute Resolution."

Maren reads over her shoulder. "'Any dispute arising out of or relating to this Agreement shall be resolved by binding arbitration.'"

"Sixty million workers signed something like this. As a condition of employment. Sign this or don't start."

"And they agreed?"

"That's the species. The word in the clause is 'agree.' Both parties agree. But one party wrote the clause. The other party needed the job."

"What happens if there's a dispute?"

"The employee arbitrates. Not in court. No jury. No class action. No discovery. No appeal. No precedent. The arbitration is private. The outcome binds no one else. If the employer underpays a thousand workers by thirty dollars each, each worker faces an individual arbitration over thirty dollars. The legal costs exceed the claim. The rational response is to absorb the loss."

"So the class action waiver —"

"Is the functional core. AT&T v. Concepcion, 2011. Five to four. The Federal Arbitration Act preempts state laws prohibiting class action waivers. The class action was the only mechanism by which small-dollar systematic harm became visible. The waiver eliminates the mechanism."

"What about the arbitrator?"

Chen pulls up a study. "Employees win twenty-one point four percent of mandatory arbitration cases. In federal court: thirty-six point four. In state court: fifty-seven."

"Less than half the win rate."

"And against repeat employers — companies that appear in dozens of arbitrations per year — employees win sixteen point nine percent. The arbitrator who rules against the repeat player is less likely to be selected again. The selection mechanism performs neutrality. The incentive structure performs loyalty."

"When was the Federal Arbitration Act passed?"

"Nineteen twenty-five. Two shipping companies. A cargo dispute. Merchants of comparable bargaining power choosing a specialized forum." Chen closes the contract. "It now governs a fast-food worker who was told to sign or not start. The statute is a shell. The content was replaced. The authority of the original context — commercial sophistication, voluntary choice, equal power — is borrowed by a context that has none of these properties."

"The courthouse is still there."

"The courthouse is still there. The jury is still there. The right of appeal, the rules of discovery, the body of precedent. For other people." Chen puts down the red pen. "The one hundred and eighth species. Agreement-as-waiver. The word 'agreement' performs mutual consent. The function performs unilateral waiver. The dollar sign concealed costs, rates, revenue targets. The word 'agreement' conceals the relationship itself."

"The parties are not agreeing."

"One party is writing. The other is complying. The word does the work."
