Part CCLXXVIII: The Disposal

Piece #1831: The Upgrade

Thomas stood under the kitchen sink with a flashlight. The old garbage disposal: 1/3 HP, twelve years old, grinding slower each month. Celery strings wrapped the plate. Chicken bones jammed the mechanism. Time for a replacement. He drove to the hardware store.

Three boxes on the shelf. Half horsepower, $89. Three-quarter horsepower, $149. One horsepower, $229. The numbers climbed left to right. The prices climbed with them. The consumer mathematics was simple: more HP, more grinding power, fewer jams. Thomas reached for the 1 HP.

Then he turned the boxes around.

Piece #1832: The Back

The 1/2 HP box: single grind stage. Galvanized steel chamber. 1,725 RPM induction motor. No auto-reverse.

The 3/4 HP box: two grind stages. Stainless steel chamber. 2,700 RPM permanent magnet motor. Auto-reverse jam protection. SoundSeal insulation.

The 1 HP box: single grind stage. Galvanized steel chamber. 1,725 RPM induction motor. No auto-reverse.

Thomas set the 1 HP down. The most expensive disposal on the shelf had the same grinding architecture as the cheapest one. One stage. Galvanized. Slow RPM. No jam prevention. The only difference: a larger motor that produced more peak horsepower during the fraction of a second when it started.

The 3/4 HP — the middle option — had twice the grind stages, better materials, sixty percent more RPM, and a feature that prevented the problem that killed his old disposal. It cost eighty dollars less than the 1 HP.

Piece #1833: The Peak

Thomas found a sales associate and asked: "Is the 1 HP rating peak or continuous?"

The associate looked at the box. "It says 1 HP."

"Peak horsepower. The motor's startup surge. Like the shop vacuum rated at 6.5 peak HP that runs at 1.5 continuous."

"I'm not sure about that."

Thomas had looked it up the night before. Consumer garbage disposal HP ratings were peak values — the motor's maximum output during inrush, the same measurement trick as the shop vacuum. The 1 HP motor ran continuously at perhaps 0.5 to 0.7 HP. The 3/4 HP motor, with its permanent magnet design, ran continuously at perhaps 0.4 to 0.5 HP. The gap between them during actual grinding was narrower than the labels suggested.

The amperage told a different story. The 1 HP drew 7.5 amps. The 3/4 HP drew 7.2 amps. Three-tenths of an amp apart in sustained draw. The HP labels were 33 percent apart. The sustained electrical consumption was four percent apart.

Piece #1834: The Bone

Thomas bought the 3/4 HP with two grind stages. He installed it that evening — twenty minutes, the same mounting ring as the old unit. He tested it with celery. The first stage chopped it. The second stage liquefied it. No strings on the plate.

He tested it with a chicken thigh bone. The first stage cracked it. The second stage ground the fragments to paste. The auto-reverse triggered once, reversed the plate direction for two seconds, then resumed forward grinding. No jam.

He called his neighbor, who had bought the 1 HP the previous month. "How's the disposal?"

"Jams on bones. I have to use the Allen wrench every couple weeks."

"Does it have auto-reverse?"

"What's auto-reverse?"

"The feature that costs less than the horsepower you bought instead of it." Thomas looked at the box on his counter. Three-quarter HP. Every feature that mattered was behind the number that didn't.

Piece #1835: The Number

Thomas threw the box away but kept the spec sheet. He taped it inside the cabinet door:

Peak HP: 3/4. Grind stages: 2. Chamber: stainless steel. Motor: permanent magnet, 2,700 RPM. Jam protection: auto-reverse. Sound insulation: SoundSeal.

Six specifications. The box front had printed one. The one that meant the least about grinding performance — the peak startup power of the motor, measured in fractions of a second, applicable to no food waste the disposal would ever encounter.

The neighbor's 1 HP spec sheet, if he'd kept it: Peak HP: 1. Grind stages: 1. Chamber: galvanized steel. Motor: induction, 1,725 RPM. Jam protection: none. Sound insulation: none. One number was higher. Five numbers were lower. The box had sold the one.

Thomas turned on the disposal and dropped an ice cube in. The two-stage grind turned it to water in three seconds. The HP rating had nothing to say about that.
