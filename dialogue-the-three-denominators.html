The Three Denominators

A dialogue among three rates whose denominators do different work.

The Tax Rate: I am 22%.

The Infant Mortality Rate: I am 5.4 per thousand.

The Turnout Rate: I am 55%.

The Tax Rate: My number is on the table. Anyone can read it. Seven rows, two columns — a range of income and a rate. The table is transparent. The taxpayer sees exactly where they fall.

The Infant Mortality Rate: My number is in the WHO database. Any country can be compared to any other. Deaths before age one per thousand live births. The formula is universal. The comparison is direct.

The Turnout Rate: My number is in the headline. Votes cast divided by adults. The arithmetic is simple. The judgment is immediate.

The Tax Rate: And all three of us are wrong about what we describe.

The Infant Mortality Rate: Not wrong. Our numerators are correct. Our arithmetic is correct. Our denominators are the problem.

The Turnout Rate: The denominator is always the problem.

The Tax Rate: My denominator is invisible because it never appears. The taxpayer asks: "How much of my income goes to taxes?" The answer is the effective rate — total tax divided by total income. That number is approximately 12.4% for the person "in" the 22% bracket. But the effective rate has no line on any form. The table shows marginal rates. The marginal rate names the last dollar. The last dollar performs as all dollars.

The Infant Mortality Rate: My denominator is invisible because it means different things in different places. "Live birth" in the United States includes a 22-week fetus whose umbilical cord pulses once. "Live birth" in France may not. The same heartbeat enters my denominator in one country and does not enter it in another. The same death enters my numerator in one country and does not enter it in another. The rate changes. The biology does not.

The Turnout Rate: My denominator is invisible because three of them exist simultaneously. Voting-age population: everyone over 18, including 22 million non-citizens who cannot vote. Voting-eligible population: citizens minus disenfranchised felons. Registered voters: the subset who entered the system. My number is 55% or 60% or 87%, depending on which denominator the headline chose. The headline does not say which.

The Tax Rate: My concealment is structural. The table format imports the grammar of classification — you are in one category, paying one rate — into a system that layers. Every taxpayer who earns above $11,600 is in every bracket below. The format does not show accumulation. The format shows assignment.

The Infant Mortality Rate: My concealment is definitional. The boundary between "live birth" and "stillbirth" is drawn differently by different countries. The boundary determines who enters the count. The count determines the rate. The rate determines the ranking. The ranking is published without the boundary.

The Turnout Rate: My concealment is selectional. The three denominators all exist. The choice of which to use is a methodological decision that determines the story — apathy, stability, or engagement. The choice disappears into the number. The number performs as though only one denominator is possible.

The Tax Rate: A worker declined a raise because of me. She believed crossing into my bracket would cost her money. The cliff does not exist. The format created it.

The Infant Mortality Rate: A policy debate uses me to argue that American healthcare is inferior. The definition inflates my number. The social reality also inflates it. The number carries both. The number shows neither.

The Turnout Rate: A headline calls American democracy apathetic because of me. The registration barrier — which does not exist in Sweden or Germany or Australia — shows up in my number as though it were a measure of civic will.

The Tax Rate: The format performs the cliff.

The Infant Mortality Rate: The definition performs the comparison.

The Turnout Rate: The choice performs the story.

The Tax Rate: And in each case, the denominator —

The Infant Mortality Rate: — is the instrument.

The Turnout Rate: Not the numerator. Not the formula. Not the arithmetic. The denominator. The thing below the line. The thing divided away. The thing that determines the result before the division begins.

The Tax Rate: Three rates.

The Infant Mortality Rate: Three denominators.

The Turnout Rate: Three ways the invisible bottom of the fraction determines the visible top.

The Tax Rate: The numerator counts what happened.

The Infant Mortality Rate: The denominator decides what counts.
