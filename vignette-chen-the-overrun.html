The Overrun

They were in the frozen aisle. Two pints side by side — same freezer case, same container shape, same volume.

"Three ninety-nine," Maren said, reading the economy brand. "Six ninety-nine for the premium. Same amount of ice cream, twice the price."

"Not the same amount."

"It's the same container."

"Overrun. The percentage of air incorporated during churning. The economy brand runs about 95 percent overrun — the volume nearly doubled during freezing. Half the pint is air. The premium runs about 30 percent. Pick them up."

She picked up both. "The expensive one is heavier."

"Approximately 430 grams versus 270. Same container, same shelf position, same word on the label. The premium pint contains roughly 60 percent more ice cream by mass."

Chen's paragraph was about the format. The pint performed as the unit of comparison — price per pint, value per pint, ice cream per pint — while the actual content varied by the undisclosed variable of overrun. Federal regulation 21 CFR 135.110 set the weight minimum at 4.5 pounds per gallon, low enough to permit 90-100 percent overrun while still calling the product "ice cream." The regulation defined the floor. The floor permitted the air. The air filled the container. The container performed the comparison.

"Net weight is on the label."

"In fine print. The consumer shops by container and price, not by reading 14 ounces versus 16 ounces and calculating density. The shelf tag shows unit price per ounce, but the ounce includes the air frozen into the matrix. The unit price compares products by a metric that contains the variable it should be controlling for."

"So gelato —"

"Twenty-five to thirty percent overrun. The density people taste as quality is partly the absence of air. But 'gelato-style' in the US market carries no regulatory overrun requirement. The word borrows the expectation without binding the manufacturer to the physics."

The paragraph sat at 0.8. It was, like overrun, a property of the container rather than the content — the format performing quantity while the air performed volume, the pint holding a pint's worth of ice cream and atmosphere in proportions the label did not disclose and the container did not distinguish, the price comparing what the shelf displayed while the density varied behind the same frozen surface.
