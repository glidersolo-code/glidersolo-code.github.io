Essay 527: The Natural

The word "natural" does different work in different sentences. The natural frequency of a bridge is a physical property — excite it at the right frequency and the bridge will oscillate whether or not anyone wants it to. Natural selection operates on populations regardless of the observer's preferences. The natural logarithm is a mathematical constant, not a policy choice.

The natural rate of unemployment borrows from all of these. The word says: there is a rate below which the economy cannot sustainably go without accelerating inflation. The rate exists the way the boiling point of water exists. Policy cannot override it. Push unemployment below the natural rate and inflation rises — a consequence as inevitable as the steam above the kettle.

Milton Friedman chose the word "natural" deliberately in 1968, borrowing from Knut Wicksell's "natural rate of interest." The framing was effective. The concept — that there is some unemployment rate consistent with stable inflation — entered monetary policy as a boundary condition, a physical constraint, a number the Federal Reserve must respect.

The number is not fixed. In the 1990s, the natural rate was estimated at 5.5-6.0%. In the 2010s, the Fed revised it to 4.0-4.5%. After 2008 and again after 2020, unemployment fell below the Fed's estimate for extended periods. Inflation did not accelerate as predicted. The estimate shifted. The word "natural" did not.

Staiger, Stock, and Watson demonstrated in 1997 that a 95% confidence interval for NAIRU in the United States was approximately 4.3% to 7.3%. Three percentage points. The range is wider than the typical fluctuations the Fed is trying to manage. At any given moment, the Fed cannot know with statistical confidence whether unemployment has crossed the threshold — because the threshold is a range, not a point. The word "natural" converts this range into a point. The word gives the estimate a fixity the statistics do not support.

The policy consequences are asymmetric and invisible. When the Fed believes unemployment has fallen below the natural rate, it raises interest rates. Higher interest rates slow the economy. Slower economy means fewer jobs created. If the true natural rate is lower than the estimate — if the economy had room to grow — the rate increase prevented employment that would have occurred. The workers who would have been hired are invisible. They do not know they were the casualties of an uncertain estimate treated as a law.

"The natural rate of unemployment is 4.5%" is a scientific statement. "We should not let unemployment fall below 4.5%" is a policy position. The two sentences describe the same action. The first is resistant to political challenge. The second is not. The word "natural" is the difference. The word moves the claim from the political domain — where it would be debated, contested, voted on — to the scientific domain — where it is accepted, referenced, applied. The claim has not changed. The authority has.
