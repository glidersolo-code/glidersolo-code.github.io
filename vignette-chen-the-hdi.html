Vignette Chen The Hdi

Vignette

Chen is reading Ravallion's paper on a Wednesday afternoon when the number stops him. $0.51. The Human Development Index implicitly values a year of life in Zimbabwe at fifty-one cents. In the richest countries, approximately nine thousand dollars. A ratio of 17,000 to 1. The income gap between the same countries is 460 to 1. The index designed to see past income inequality contains an implicit life-year inequality thirty-seven times worse.

He reads the passage again. The geometric mean — introduced in 2010 to penalize imbalance, to ensure that a country couldn't compensate for dying young by being rich — worsened the disparity. The formula that was supposed to value life more equally valued it less. The correction made the problem worse. The architecture of the formula determined the outcome. Not the intent. The architecture.

Maren would say: the HDI was never meant to be a complete assessment. It was designed as a provocation — a single number to challenge GDP's monopoly on the headline. Haq said it explicitly: "the same level of vulgarity." The vulgarity was the point. And she would be right. The HDI succeeded brilliantly as a rhetorical device. It shifted the conversation. Development economics now talks about health and education, not just income. The number did what numbers do — it traveled, it compared, it displaced the prior number.

But Chen thinks the traveling is the problem. The number that was designed as vulgar became, over time, authoritative. The three-decimal precision — 0.970, 0.886, 0.674 — performs an exactness that the arbitrary goalposts, arbitrary weights, and implicit trade-offs do not support. The number says 0.886. The number does not say: this country has one of the highest incomes on earth and average education and we combined these incommensurable things using equal weights we chose for no reason and goalposts we've changed three times and a geometric mean that values a year of Zimbabwean life at fifty-one cents.

The ranking says Norway is second. Qatar is forty-sixth. Equatorial Guinea is 133rd. The ranking performs a comparison. The comparison requires that health, education, and income can be combined. The combination requires that they can be traded against each other. The trade-off requires a rate. The rate is embedded in the formula. The formula does not state the rate. Ravallion extracted it. $0.51.

Chen closes the paper. He thinks about the seven formats he has been considering this week. Each has a structural property that determines what it can express. Transparency, monotonicity, relativity, origin, minimum, discretization, composition. Seven architectures. Seven concealments. The format is not a vessel. The format is an instrument. The instrument shapes what it carries. And the shape is invisible because the number is always more visible than the architecture that produced it.
