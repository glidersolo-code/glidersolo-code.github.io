The feedback loop is in the incentive structure. The calibration is in the testing population. The scope is in the accounting boundary. The substitution is in the question being asked. The average is in the summary statistic. The theory is in the explanatory model — the story that explains why a practice works, which conceals the mechanism by which the practice actually works.

The cleanest evidence for theory-launders-practice: when New York's stop-and-frisk was ruled unconstitutional in 2013 and the stops ended, crime continued to decline. The practice that the theory authorized was removed. The outcome persisted. The theory predicted that ending the practice would reverse the outcome. The prediction failed. But by then the theory had been laundering the practice for twenty years.
