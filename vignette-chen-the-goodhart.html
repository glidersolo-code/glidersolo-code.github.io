Vignette

The Target

Chen placed two graphs on the table. Same school district. Same years. Same students. Same subject: reading.

The state assessment showed a climbing line. The independent assessment showed a flat line.

"Same students," Maren said.

"Same students."

"So one of the lines is wrong."

"Both lines are right." Chen tapped the climbing line. "This test is the target. The school's funding depends on it. Teachers' evaluations depend on it. The principal's career depends on it. So the school has reorganized itself around this test. Science reduced to one period a week. History dropped from third grade. Art gone. Music gone. Recess shortened. An additional reading block added — consisting entirely of practice tests formatted identically to the state assessment."

"So the scores went up because—"

"Because the students got better at this test. Not at reading. At this test. The independent assessment, which no one's career depends on, measures reading. It shows no change."

Maren stared at the two lines. "So when the metric becomes the target—"

"It ceases to be a good measure. Charles Goodhart, 1975. Originally about monetary policy. The principle is about everything."

"But the graph looks like improvement."

"The graph is improvement. Of the metric. The metric is no longer measuring what it was designed to measure. It's measuring the system's response to being measured. But the format hasn't changed. Same label. Same units. Same axis. Same upward arrow in the superintendent's PowerPoint. The format performs continuity. The referent has been substituted."

Maren picked up the flat line. "This one is invisible."

"This one is invisible because no one's career depends on it. The target absorbs all the attention. The non-target tells the truth. And the truth, having no incentive structure behind it, cannot compete with the target for attention."

Chen drew a loop on a napkin. System → metric → target → incentive → behavior → system. "The loop closes. The first observation was innocent — it described the world as it found it. Every subsequent observation is contaminated. It describes a world that has reorganized itself around the observation. The description is accurate. The description is accurate about the wrong thing."

"Cobras," Maren said quietly.

Chen looked up.

"I read about the cobra bounty. British India. Pay per dead cobra. Residents breed cobras. Cancel the bounty. Release the cobras. More cobras than before."

"The incentive produced the opposite of its intention."

"Not through failure."

"Through success. The system responded rationally to the incentive. The incentive was the problem."

Maren set both graphs down, side by side. The climbing line and the flat line. The target and the truth. The same students producing two different stories depending on whether anyone's career was attached to the number.

"Every metric that matters becomes a target," she said. "And every target ceases to be a good measure. So the metrics that matter are the ones most likely to stop measuring what matters."

Chen nodded. "The architecture guarantees the divergence. The more important the number, the less trustworthy the number. The format holds. The referent moves. And the reader sees the format."
