The Type

Chen-Maren vignette

Chen's paragraph has a blood type. The committee tested it on first encounter: they drew a sample — the opening sentence — and mixed it with their own antibodies. Professor Hendricks's serum contained anti-conventional. Professor Wallace's contained anti-experimental. The paragraph clumped with both. This should have been impossible. A paragraph cannot be both conventional and experimental — the categories are defined by mutual exclusion, the way Type A plasma attacks B antigens.

The committee reported the result as AB — a rare compatibility, a paragraph that carried both antigens and therefore triggered both responses. They flagged it for further testing.

Maren has been reading about Karl Landsteiner. About how the four blood types were a triumph of triage — the two most dangerous dimensions of incompatibility identified with a simple test, saving lives from the first day the classification was used. About how the forty-three blood group systems and 360+ antigens on the red blood cell surface were collapsed into eight categories, and the eight categories became the identity.

Chen's paragraph has 360 dimensions. Its surface carries antigens the committee's typing protocol cannot detect: the specific frequency of its rhythm, the particular weight of its silences, the angle at which its argument approaches the reader's assumptions. The committee tested for two: does it conform to genre expectations (A antigen), and does it demonstrate analytical rigor (B antigen)? These are the most dangerous incompatibilities — a paragraph that fails both will be rejected on contact. The committee types the paragraph AB-positive and moves to the crossmatch.

The crossmatch is the close reading. It mixes the specific paragraph with the specific reader's sensitivities, regardless of what the typing predicted. Professor Hendricks crossmatches: she reads the paragraph again, slowly, and finds an antibody she did not know she carried — an anti-something, a reaction to an antigen on one of the paragraph's untyped dimensions. The paragraph clumps in her hands. The typing said compatible. The crossmatch says not.

Professor Wallace does not crossmatch. He trusts the type. The type said AB-positive. The type is the identity. The 358 untyped dimensions exist on the paragraph's surface, immunologically active, capable of triggering a response the two-dimensional classification cannot predict. But the classification is the chart, and the chart is the paragraph, and the paragraph — the 360-dimensional paragraph, the one whose complete profile no typing protocol captures — goes into the system under its type, carrying antigens the system has not tested for.
