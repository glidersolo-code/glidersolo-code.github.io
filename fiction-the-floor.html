Part CCCX: The Floor

Piece #1991: The Number

Seven dollars and twenty-five cents. Thomas had earned it per hour since 2009. The number on his pay stub was the same number it had always been. The same digits, the same decimal, the same federal minimum. In 2009 the number bought a gallon of gas and most of a gallon of milk. In 2026 the number bought neither. Thomas looked at the digits: 7.25. They had not moved. Everything around them had moved. The prices had risen. The rent had risen. The bus fare, the bread, the co-pay. The number stayed. The staying was the cut. But it did not look like a cut because the number was the same.

Piece #1992: The Index

Social Security benefits adjusted every year. The formula was automatic — the Cost-of-Living Adjustment, tied to the Consumer Price Index, enacted 1975. Federal tax brackets adjusted too, indexed since 1985. The minimum wage did not adjust. It waited for Congress. Congress waited for consensus. Consensus waited for political capital. Political capital waited for an election year. The wait had lasted seventeen years. Thomas noticed the asymmetry: every other floor in the federal system rose with prices. This floor did not. The exception was not an oversight. It was a structure — a structure in which the floor could sink without anyone voting to lower it.

Piece #1993: The Author

To raise the minimum wage requires a bill, a committee, a debate, a vote, a signature. It requires an author. To erode the minimum wage requires nothing. Inflation does the work. No bill is introduced. No committee convenes. No legislator's name appears on the reduction. Thomas realized that the seventeen-year decline in his purchasing power had no author. The cut was real — twenty-eight percent of his 2009 purchasing power had been removed. But the cut arrived without legislation, without announcement, without a roll call vote. The non-decision produced the same economic effect as a decision. The non-decision cost no one any political capital because a non-decision has no author.

Piece #1994: The Word

Minimum. The least. The floor. The word performed adequacy — it said: this is the bottom, and the bottom has been determined, and below the bottom you cannot fall. Thomas multiplied: $7.25 × 2,080 hours = $15,080. The poverty threshold for a two-person household was $20,440. The minimum was below the minimum. The floor was below the floor. The word said adequacy. The arithmetic said poverty. The word and the arithmetic coexisted because the word was louder.

Piece #1995: The Stillness

The number held still. The world moved around it. Prices rose. Rents rose. The cost of bread and busses and medicine rose. The number — 7.25 — did not rise. The stillness was not neutrality. Stillness in a moving system is motion — motion in the opposite direction. A stationary wage in an inflating economy is a declining wage. But the paycheck showed the same number, and the sameness of the number concealed the decline, because "nothing has changed" read as "no harm has been done." Three hundred and nine Parts. The unchanged number. The changed world. The gap between the two was the policy that had no name, no author, no vote, and no end.
