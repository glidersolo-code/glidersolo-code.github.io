The Composite

Mahbub ul Haq wanted a number as vulgar as GNP. Those were his words. "We need a measure of the same level of vulgarity as GNP — just one number — but a measure that is not as blind to social aspects of human lives as GNP is." The vulgarity was the design specification. The Human Development Index was born in 1990 as a deliberate simplification — a single number to displace a single number.

Amartya Sen resisted. He expressed "considerable skepticism about trying to focus on a crude index of this kind, attempting to catch in one simple number a complex reality about human development." Haq's counter-argument was political: a dashboard cannot displace a headline. Only another headline can. Sen was persuaded. The rhetorical function overrode the intellectual objection. The philosopher who understood that human development cannot be reduced to a single number helped create the single number that reduces human development.

The formula combines three dimensions: health (life expectancy at birth), education (mean and expected years of schooling), and standard of living (GNI per capita, log-transformed). Each dimension is normalized to a 0-to-1 index using goalposts — life expectancy from 20 to 85 years, schooling from 0 to 18 and 0 to 15 years, income from $100 to $75,000. The three indices receive equal weight. The geometric mean combines them.

The equal weighting presents itself as neutrality. The UNDP invokes the "principle of insufficient reason" — since there is no basis for choosing unequal weights, equal weights are chosen. The principle frames a choice as a non-choice. But equal weighting is a value judgment: it asserts that health, education, and income are of equal importance. Alternative weightings shift Maldives and Gabon by more than one hundred positions. The ranking is an artifact of the weighting. The arbitrariness is concealed by the principle.

The normalization performs a further concealment. Life expectancy is measured in years. Schooling is measured in years. Income is measured in dollars. The goalposts convert all three into dimensionless indices between 0 and 1. Once the units are stripped, the geometric mean can combine them. The mathematical operation that makes the combination possible is the normalization that erases the dimensions in which the incommensurability would be visible. Years of life and years of schooling and dollars of income become three numbers between 0 and 1. The cube root of their product becomes the HDI. The formula performs the commensuration. The commensuration is where the concealment lives.

Martin Ravallion extracted the trade-off the formula does not state. The geometric mean embeds a marginal rate of substitution between dimensions. For Zimbabwe: the HDI implicitly values a year of life at $0.51. For the richest countries: approximately $9,000 per year. A 17,000-to-1 ratio — far greater than the 460-to-1 income ratio between the same countries. The 2010 switch from arithmetic to geometric mean, intended to penalize imbalance, worsened this disparity. The fix for one structural problem introduced a different structural problem.

Countries with radically different profiles produce similar scores. Qatar's income is among the highest on earth; its education scores pull the composite down. Equatorial Guinea ranks 63rd by GDP but 133rd by HDI — oil wealth and child mortality compressed into a single decimal. Cuba has less than a tenth of Qatar's income but comparable life expectancy. The single number absorbs these configurations. The ranking then performs a comparison that the components, taken separately, do not support.

The goalposts are described as "natural zeros" and "aspirational targets." They are neither. The income upper bound was $8,193 in 1990 and is $75,000 today. Each change rearranges the rankings. Changing the goalposts shifts roughly 30% of countries by five or more positions. The boundaries of the scale are not properties of the thing being measured. They are choices that determine the numbers that determine the rankings that perform the comparisons.

The structural property is composition. The batting average conceals through visibility. The h-index conceals through directionality. The percentile conceals through absorption. The survival rate conceals through its starting point. The age statement conceals through its floor. The Likert scale conceals through its bins. The HDI conceals through its combination. Incommensurable dimensions are stripped of their units, weighted equally by declaration, combined by a geometric mean that embeds trade-offs the formula does not state, and reported as a three-decimal number that performs comprehensiveness. The format makes the incommensurable commensurable by erasing the dimensions in which the impossibility would be visible. Seven structures. Seven concealments. One instrument: the format.
