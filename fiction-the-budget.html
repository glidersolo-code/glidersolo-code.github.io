The Budget

Thomas, Part LXII

751. The Steady State

The ocean's silicon budget was considered closed. Rivers deliver approximately eight teramoles of dissolved silicon per year. Diatoms consume the silicon, build glass shells, die, and sink. Most of the shells dissolve in the deep water; some are buried in sediment. Inputs equal outputs. The system is in steady state.

Thomas's budget of Helen is also closed. He arrives on Saturdays. He brings attention, conversation, presence — the inputs. Helen returns warmth, tea, the arrangement of the room — the outputs. Inputs approximate outputs. Thomas has never questioned whether the budget is complete because the numbers appear to balance. What he brings, she returns. What she offers, he receives. The exchange feels measured, equilibrated, whole.

A balanced budget suppresses the search for missing terms.

752. The Sand

Quartz is one of the most stable minerals on Earth's surface. It resists weathering. It resists dissolution. It persists in riverbeds, deserts, beaches — everywhere other minerals have broken down, quartz endures. Geochemists classified it as effectively inert under surface conditions. A reasonable classification, supported by thermodynamic data, by dissolution-rate experiments in calm water, by a century of observation. Sand does not dissolve.

Unless waves break on it.

Researchers in Toulouse built a laboratory swell channel — a simulated beach made of pure quartz sand, with a wave machine. They measured how much silicon dissolved into the water as waves broke over the sand. The dissolution rate was far higher than thermodynamic stability predicted. Wave energy mechanically accelerated the kinetics: agitating grains, exposing fresh surfaces, driving fluid into pore spaces. The calm-water experiments had missed it because the experiments were calm.

Thomas has classified certain parts of his relationship with Helen as inert. The silences between topics. The interval while she pours tea. The walk from the door to the chair. These are the sand — the stretches that appear chemically stable, resistant to meaning, enduring because nothing happens in them. Thomas passes through these intervals the way water passes over quartz in a still pool: in contact but not interacting. He has never measured them because his instruments measure conversation, not silence. His dissolution-rate experiments are all conducted in calm water.

But the intervals are not calm. Helen's energy moves through them like wave energy through beach sand. The silence while she pours tea is not absence of interaction — it is a different kinetics. Something dissolves in the silence. Something enters solution. Thomas has not measured it because his instruments do not measure dissolution under agitation. They measure the silicon that arrives via rivers — the obvious, channeled, monitored inputs — and miss the silicon that dissolves from the sand he walks on every Saturday.

753. The Missing Flux

Eight point three teramoles per year. That was the beach flux the Toulouse researchers calculated when they upscaled their laboratory result to the world's sandy coastlines. Not a minor correction. Not a footnote. A flux comparable to the entire river contribution. The ocean was receiving roughly double the dissolved silicon that the budget accounted for.

The question this raises: if inputs are double what the budget assumed, where is the extra silicon going? Either there are unidentified sinks — processes removing silicon that nobody has measured — or the ocean is accumulating dissolved silicon and the system is not in steady state at all. Either way, the budget was wrong. Not because anyone made an error. Because the budget appeared to balance, and the appearance of balance prevented the search for what was missing.

Thomas's budget of Helen is also missing a flux. What she gives him in the silences, in the intervals, in the walk from the door to the chair — this is unaccounted for. His budget measures conversation: words exchanged, topics covered, warmth conveyed in recognizable units. The budget appears to balance. But an input roughly equal in magnitude to everything he measures has been dissolving from the surfaces he walks across without noticing. If he included the silences, the budget would not balance. He would have to find the sinks — the places where Helen's unmeasured warmth has been going — or accept that the system is not in steady state. That it has been accumulating something. That seven hundred and fifty-one Saturdays of unaccounted warmth have been building in the deep water of a relationship Thomas thought he understood because the numbers on the surface added up.

754. The Interdisciplinary Gap

The beach silicon flux was missed because it lived between two fields. Geochemists study dissolution kinetics — how fast minerals dissolve under various chemical conditions. Physical oceanographers study wave energy — surf dynamics, coastal erosion, energy dissipation along shorelines. Nobody in geochemistry was modeling wave energy. Nobody in physical oceanography was measuring dissolution rates. The laboratories in Toulouse that discovered the flux were interdisciplinary: LEGOS (ocean dynamics), IRAP (planetary geochemistry), IMFT (fluid mechanics). The discovery required instruments from all three.

Thomas lives between two fields too. The field of conversation — where he is expert, attentive, calibrated — and the field of presence, where he is clumsy and unmeasured. The conversation instruments are precise. Thomas can track Helen's topics, rhythms, warmth with fourteen years of data. But the presence instruments — the ones that would measure what dissolves in silence, what transfers in the walk to the chair, what accumulates in the interval while she arranges the cushion — Thomas does not own these instruments. He has never built them because the conversational instruments produce a budget that appears complete.

The interdisciplinary gap matched the phenomenon's interdisciplinary nature. What Thomas needs to measure lives at the intersection of two capacities he has kept separate: his capacity to analyze and his capacity to be present. The analysis produces data. The presence produces dissolution. Neither field has the other's instruments. Neither field asks the other's questions. And the budget, balanced on the analysis side, suppresses the search for what the presence side has been contributing all along.

755. Two Hundred

Entry number two hundred in the inventory of hidden channels. Two hundred objects that were present and unread. Two hundred signals that the instruments received but the frameworks could not interpret. The relay station that routed immunity through the brain. The fitting parameter that locked the equation to spheres. The ghost range that was the correct record. The sand that dissolved when the waves broke.

Thomas is entry two hundred and one. He is the hidden channel that he himself cannot read. Seven hundred and fifty-five Saturdays of data, and the largest flux in his system — what Helen gives him in the spaces between what he measures — has never appeared in the budget. The budget balanced. The balance was the concealment. And somewhere in the deep water of Thomas, something has been accumulating.

The ocean may not be in steady state. Thomas is certainly not. The inputs exceed what the outputs can account for, and the excess is stored somewhere Thomas has not looked — in a reservoir he has not mapped, in a depth he has not sounded, in a column of water he assumed was static because nothing on the surface suggested otherwise.

The sand is dissolving. It has always been dissolving. The waves have always been breaking. The only thing missing was the measurement. And the budget, appearing to balance, ensured the measurement was never made.
