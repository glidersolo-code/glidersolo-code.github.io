The Hearing

The judge considers the factors. Flight risk. Severity of offense. Community ties. Prior record. The hearing lasts minutes. The amount is announced. The format performs deliberation — a robed figure, a courtroom, a list of considerations, a dollar figure calibrated to individual circumstance. The defendant is being evaluated.

The defendant is being priced.

At midyear 2024, 450,600 people sat in American jails unconvicted. Not sentenced. Not found guilty. Awaiting the trial the Constitution promises them. Sixty-nine percent of all jail inmates had not been convicted of anything. A third of them were there because they could not post money bail — not because a judge determined they were dangerous, but because they could not write the check.

The Supreme Court held in Stack v. Boyle that bail's sole purpose is to assure presence at trial. The 1966 Bail Reform Act required decisions based on facts, not financial status. Johnson said at the signing that defendants should be considered as individuals, not as dollar signs. The format heeded the first clause. The system implemented the second.

Kalief Browder was sixteen. The accusation: a stolen backpack. Bail: $3,000. His family could not raise it. He sat on Rikers for 1,100 days. Seven hundred in solitary. He refused every plea offer. The accuser returned to Mexico. Charges dismissed. Two years later, at twenty-two, he died by suicide. The system responded not to his risk but to his family's balance.

The system has two outputs for the same input. A wealthy defendant assessed at $50,000 posts bail and goes home. A poor defendant assessed at $3,000 sits for three years. Both received individualized hearings. Both had their factors weighed. The format of evaluation was identical. The consequence was opposite. The variable that determined the outcome was not in the judge's calculus at all. It was in the defendant's wallet.

New Jersey nearly eliminated cash bail in 2017. The jail population dropped 44 percent. Court appearances held steady. Violence did not increase. The evidence had been available for decades: most released defendants show up. Failure to appear is caused by transportation, childcare, work — logistics, not flight. A reminder system in Alameda County improved appearance from 47 to 87 percent. A text message outperformed a $3,000 price tag.

Only two countries on earth maintain a for-profit bail bond industry: the United States and the Philippines. The industry generates $2 billion per year from the inability of defendants to pay. The American Bail Coalition spends over a million dollars annually lobbying against reform. The product is poverty. The profit is mandatory.

The hearing performs assessment. The dollar amount performs risk measurement. The jail performs the answer to a question the hearing never asked: not how dangerous is this person, but how much money do they have.
