The Statement

The law requires you to know you are causing harm. The law does not require you to stop.

The National Environmental Policy Act, signed January 1, 1970, requires federal agencies to prepare an Environmental Impact Statement for major actions significantly affecting the environment. The statement documents the harm. The documentation is the compliance.

The Supreme Court made this explicit. Robertson v. Methow Valley Citizens Council, 1989: NEPA "simply prescribes the necessary process for preventing uninformed — rather than unwise — agency action." The word choice is surgical. Uninformed, not unwise. The law prevents the agency from not knowing. The law does not prevent the agency from not caring.

Strycker's Bay, 1980: once the agency "has considered the environmental consequences," the court cannot interject itself within the area of the executive's discretion. The consideration IS the legal act. What follows — the decision — lies beyond the law's reach.

The average Environmental Impact Statement takes 4.5 years to complete. It runs 661 pages. It costs between $250,000 and $6.6 million. It identifies habitat fragmentation, watershed contamination, species displacement. It evaluates alternatives, including no action. It is published. The public comments. The agency issues a Record of Decision. The agency may build. The forest may be fragmented. The document remains on file.

The document demonstrates that the agency considered the environment. The consideration was the document. The highway was the decision. The document did not change the decision. The document changed the legality of the decision.

Critics from both sides describe the same structural feature. Environmentalists: the law is toothless — it requires process, not protection. Industry: the law causes delay without producing better outcomes. Both describe a document that performs deliberation without the deliberation affecting the decision. Both are correct. The document is the compliance. The compliance is the document. The 661 pages perform the consideration. The consideration need not change the outcome. The knowing is the law. The stopping is the discretion.
