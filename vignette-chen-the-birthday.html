Vignette

The Birthday

Chen is nine years old. Chen is in fourth grade. Chen steals a candy bar from the corner store. The store owner sees. The store owner calls Chen's mother. Chen's mother comes to the store. Chen's mother apologizes. Chen's mother pays for the candy bar. Chen goes home. Chen is grounded for a week.

Chen is nine. The law cannot touch Chen. Chen is incapable of forming criminal intent. Chen is incapable because Chen is nine.

One year later. Chen is ten. Chen is in fifth grade. Chen steals a candy bar from the same store. The same store owner sees. This time the store owner calls the police. This time the police come. This time the police file a report. This time the report goes to the juvenile court. This time Chen sits in a courtroom.

Chen is ten. The law can touch Chen. Chen is capable of forming criminal intent. Chen is capable because Chen is ten.

Nothing changed except the calendar. The brain is the same brain, one year further along a trajectory that will not complete for another fifteen years. The impulse is the same impulse. The candy bar is the same candy bar. The store is the same store. The only thing different is the number, and the number is the capacity, and the capacity is the punishment.

Maren tells this story to adult Chen on the fire escape. Not because it happened — it didn't. Because it illustrates the fiction.

"The line between nine and ten is a political choice dressed as developmental fact," Maren says. "England chose ten. Belgium chose eighteen. North Carolina chose six. If the line reflected a biological reality, it would be the same everywhere. It's not the same anywhere."

Chen looks at the city below. "Six," she says. "Six years old."

"Six."

"A first-grader."

"In North Carolina, a first-grader can be criminally prosecuted."

"And in Belgium, a seventeen-year-old cannot."

"The same species. The same brain architecture. The same prefrontal cortex developing at the same pace."

Chen is quiet.

"The line isn't about the brain," she says.

"No."

"The line is about what the society is willing to do."

"Yes."

"And the line performs the capacity that justifies what the society is willing to do."

"Yes."

Chen holds her coffee cup. Thinks about the table — the range from 6 to 18 — and what it means that the same child is simultaneously capable and incapable depending on which flag flies above the courthouse. Thinks about how the birthday performs the capacity. How the capacity justifies the trial. How the trial ratifies the birthday. How the circle closes before the child understands what a circle is.

"The brain doesn't know what country it's in," Chen says.

"No. The brain doesn't know what time it is either."

"But the law does."

"The law keeps excellent time. The law knows exactly when midnight falls on a tenth birthday. The law is very precise about the birthday. The law is very imprecise about the brain. The precision is the concealment."
