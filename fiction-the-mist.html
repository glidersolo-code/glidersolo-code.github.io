Part CCXLIX: The Mist

Piece #1686: The Gallon

The humidifier box says "output: 4 gallons per day." Thomas bought it for the winter. His sinuses cracked in December. The furnace ran constantly and the air inside dropped to 18 percent relative humidity. He set the humidifier on the dresser and filled the 4-gallon tank. He expected 4 gallons of moisture per day. The output is tested at 20 percent relative humidity — AHAM Standard 611, a condition of maximum dryness, where the vapor pressure deficit between the water surface and the room air is at its largest. Evaporation rate is proportional to that deficit. As the room's humidity rises toward his target — 40 percent — the deficit narrows and the evaporation rate drops. At 35 percent RH, the machine delivers approximately 2.5 gallons per day. At 40 percent, less. The headline number describes the machine's output at the moment of maximum need, not the output at the condition the consumer is trying to reach.

Piece #1687: The Room

The box says "covers rooms up to 500 square feet." Thomas's bedroom is 200 square feet. He assumed ample capacity. The room-size rating is calculated from the output capacity at 20 percent RH, using assumed values: 8-foot ceiling height, a specific air exchange rate, a specific starting humidity. His bedroom has 9-foot ceilings — 12 percent more air volume. His house was built in 1952 — the natural air change rate is approximately 3 changes per hour. A new-construction home might be 0.5 changes per hour. Every cubic foot of humid air that leaks through the walls and windows is replaced by dry outdoor air that the humidifier must re-humidify. His 200-square-foot bedroom with 1952 construction and 3 ACH requires approximately the same output as a 500-square-foot bedroom in a 2020 home at 0.5 ACH. The box measured one variable — square footage. The room provided four: square footage, ceiling height, construction tightness, and outdoor conditions.

Piece #1688: The Tank

The tank holds 4 gallons. This is the reservoir's volume — how often the consumer must refill. It is not how much moisture reaches the air. An evaporative humidifier — which draws air through a wet wick — releases moisture at a rate governed by the humidity differential. The tank empties as the differential demands. An ultrasonic humidifier — which vibrates water into microscopic droplets ejected into the air — aerosolizes water at a rate set by the machine's output knob, regardless of the room's current humidity. It can over-humidify a tight room to 60 percent while the consumer believes "more is better." The tank empties at the same rate whether the room needs the moisture or not. Two technologies, same tank capacity label. One self-regulates. One does not. The label does not distinguish.

Piece #1689: The Dust

Hard water enters the tank. The consumer fills from the tap. Municipal water in Thomas's city contains dissolved calcium and magnesium — 180 ppm, classified "hard" by the USGS. An ultrasonic humidifier aerosolizes the water and the minerals with it. The minerals land on furniture, electronics, and lungs as fine white dust. An evaporative humidifier leaves the minerals in the wick — the water evaporates, the minerals stay. The consumer's choice between these two technologies determines whether 180 ppm of dissolved minerals becomes airborne particulate. The box says "4 gallons per day." It does not say "4 gallons of mineral-laden aerosol per day" or "4 gallons of pure water vapor per day." The output is the same number. The output is not the same substance.

Piece #1690: The Winter

Two hundred and forty-nine Parts. Thomas ran the humidifier through January. The tank needed filling twice a day, not once — the machine delivered less than 4 gallons per day at his target humidity, and his 1952 house leaked the humidity faster than the machine replaced it. He bought a second unit. Two humidifiers in a 200-square-foot room, each rated for 500 square feet. The white mineral dust accumulated on the nightstand because both were ultrasonic and his water was hard. He achieved 38 percent relative humidity. The target was 40. The box had said 4 gallons. The room had said otherwise. The winter, having dried the air, did not consult the label before demanding more than the label described.
