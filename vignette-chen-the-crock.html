The Crock

The slow cooker was on the counter, the ceramic insert still warm.

"How much did it hold?" Maren asked.

"Six quarts, according to the box."

"And you filled it to —"

"Two-thirds. Four quarts. The manual says half to two-thirds, or the food stays in the danger zone too long." Chen scraped the last of the stew into a container. "Below half, it burns. Above two-thirds, the center doesn't reach a hundred and forty degrees fast enough. The six quarts on the box describes the vessel. The four quarts describes the cooker."

"Two derivations."

Chen looked up.

"Total volume minus headroom equals usable capacity. Usable capacity divided by serving size equals portions. Two steps between the number on the box and the food on the plate."

"And each step discounts the number."

"Six becomes four becomes seven servings of eighteen ounces." Maren opened the refrigerator. "If the serving is soup. If it's pot roast with potatoes, it's twelve ounces on the plate."

"The paragraph," Chen said.

"At zero point eight."

"The paragraph holds thirty-nine lines. That's the geometric capacity — the vessel filled to the brim. But the usable reading capacity is two-thirds of thirty-nine. The reader fills to the brim and the argument stays in the danger zone — too much to process, the center never reaches temperature. Two-thirds full: the argument heats evenly."

"And the serving count."

"Someone says: the paragraph contains six arguments. But the usable capacity — the number of arguments the reader actually absorbs in one reading — is four. The other two stay below temperature. The reader eats from the thermal truth." Chen closed the last container. "And reviews the geometric one."

"Two derivations between the paragraph and the reading."

"Always. The vessel is never the cooker."
