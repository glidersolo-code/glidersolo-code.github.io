The Parabola

Chen drew a curve on the napkin.

"Surplus production. The Schaefer model. Logistic growth. The curve rises, peaks at K over 2, and falls. The peak is the maximum sustainable yield."

Maren traced it with her finger. "It's symmetric."

"It's symmetric because the logistic equation is symmetric. The equation generates a parabola. A parabola peaks at its midpoint. The midpoint is not a property of the fish. The midpoint is a property of the equation."

"But the fisheries—"

"The fisheries fished to the peak. The Pacific sardine fleet: 142,000 tons in 1946. 26,000 tons one year later. Ninety percent gone in five years. Orange roughy in New Zealand: the model used standard parameters — fast maturation, annual reproduction. The fish lives over two hundred years and doesn't breed until seventy."

Maren put down her coffee. "Seventy."

"Seventy to eighty years old before the first spawn. The model assumed it would behave like cod. Three of eight fisheries collapsed. Recovery time: decades. The model had predicted years."

"So the model was wrong."

"The model was correct. The parabola was a correct parabola. The peak was at the correct midpoint. The logistic equation performed its mathematics flawlessly. The model was wrong because the model was not the ocean. The equation assumes equilibrium — the stock sits at a stable point where growth replaces harvest. The ocean is not in equilibrium. The equation assumes one species. The ocean is an ecosystem. Remove half the biomass of one species and the food web restructures."

"And the policy?"

"UNCLOS Article 61. International law. 'Maintain or restore populations at levels which can produce the maximum sustainable yield.' The Magnuson-Stevens Act. United States law. Maximum sustainable yield as the management standard. Larkin wrote the epitaph in 1977. 'An Epitaph for the Concept of Maximum Sustained Yield.' The concept survived its own eulogy."

"Because it was already in law."

"Because it was already in law. And because the alternative — admitting that the number is a mathematical artifact rather than a biological property — would require dismantling the regulatory framework built on the artifact. The number is load-bearing."

Maren looked at the curve on the napkin. The symmetric rise. The neat peak. The mirror-image decline.

"The ocean doesn't look like this."

"Nothing looks like this. This is a parabola. This is what happens when you write surplus production as rB times one minus B over K. This is what the equation looks like. The ocean looks like the ocean."

"So the number on the quota—"

"Came from the parabola. Not from the fish. The fish never said how many of itself could be sustainably taken. The equation said. The equation said it with the confidence of mathematics and the precision of a formula. And the precision is the concealment. The number looks measured. The number was generated."

Chen tapped the peak of the curve.

"Pella and Tomlinson showed in 1969 that for most fish stocks, the peak is not at the midpoint. It's lower. The Schaefer model overestimates MSY. The correction exists. The corrected model is more complex. The corrected model does not generate a clean number. The clean number persists."

"Because the clean number fits the form."

"Because the clean number fits the quota, the treaty, the legislation, the management plan, and the press release. Because a single number can be written into Article 61. An asymmetric curve with a shape parameter cannot."

Maren looked at the napkin. "The form of the model is the policy."

"And the policy outlived the model. The epitaph was published forty-nine years ago. The quota is set today."
