Part CCCXXXIII: The Panel

Piece #2106: The Exemption

In 1958, the Food Additives Amendment required pre-market FDA approval for anything added to food — with one exemption. Substances "generally recognized, among experts qualified by scientific training and experience to evaluate their safety, to be safe" were exempt. The exemption placed the determination of safety with qualified experts. Not with the FDA. With experts.

Thomas reads the statute three times. The exemption is in the grammar. The sentence does not say "recognized by the FDA." It says "recognized among experts." Experts can be anyone qualified. The company that wants to add a substance to food can hire experts to recognize the substance as safe. The recognition is the determination. The determination is the exemption. The exemption is the addition. The substance enters the food supply through the act of being recognized by experts the company hired to recognize it.

Seven hundred substances were grandfathered onto the original GRAS list. The list grew. The list was never completely reviewed. In 1969, Nixon ordered a re-evaluation. Ten years of work. Four hundred substances reviewed. The review was never completed. The agency has not systematically reconsidered GRAS substances since the 1980s.

Piece #2107: The Inspector

Thomas imagines a building inspector. The building inspector works for the construction company. The construction company pays the inspector's salary. The construction company selects the inspector. The inspector inspects the building and issues a certificate of safety. The certificate is filed with the city. The city does not review the certificate. The city is not required to review the certificate. The building is safe because the inspector said so. The inspector works for the builder.

This is not a metaphor. This is the GRAS system. The manufacturer convenes an expert panel — typically three to five scientists. The manufacturer pays for everything: the consulting firm, the panelists, the literature review, the toxicological testing. The manufacturer selects the panelists. The panel issues a written opinion. The company markets the substance immediately. No waiting period. No FDA review. No public notice.

Neltner et al. analyzed 451 GRAS notifications filed between 1997 and 2012. Zero — not one — were made by a standing expert panel selected by an independent third party. One individual served on 128 panels. Seven individuals occupied 46% of all panel positions. The inspector is the builder. The certificate is real. The independence is performed.

Piece #2108: The Shelf

Thomas stands in a grocery store aisle. Three products sit on the same shelf. One contains an ingredient that FDA actually evaluated decades ago. One contains an ingredient that received a "no questions" letter — FDA did not affirm safety; it merely did not object. One contains an ingredient that was self-affirmed GRAS by the manufacturer's own paid panel and never disclosed to FDA at all.

The labels are identical in format. The ingredient lists follow the same regulations. The products occupy the same shelf, the same regulatory framework, the same consumer inference. The consumer cannot distinguish between the three. The consumer reads "FDA" in the regulatory landscape and infers government review. The inference is produced not by deception but by format.

Of 766 food chemicals introduced between 2000 and 2021, 99% avoided FDA scrutiny using the GRAS designation. The United States allows approximately 10,000 food additives. The European Union allows 411. The difference is not in the chemistry. The difference is in who evaluates the chemistry.

Piece #2109: The Fifty Years

Partially hydrogenated oils were on the GRAS list. For over fifty years, they entered every processed food that needed solid fat at room temperature: margarine, shortening, crackers, cookies, frozen dinners, microwave popcorn. The GRAS designation meant no one had to prove they were safe. They were presumed safe. The presumption lasted fifty years.

In 2015, FDA revoked GRAS status for partially hydrogenated oils. The revocation estimated removal would prevent thousands of heart attacks and deaths annually. Thomas writes the equation: fifty years of GRAS × thousands of deaths per year. The product of presumed safety multiplied by actual harm. The GRAS designation did not cause the harm. The GRAS designation prevented the question that would have discovered the harm. The format was the delay. The delay was the cost.

Mycoprotein received a "no questions" letter after the manufacturer did not disclose its own safety study showing adverse effects. By 2018: 1,752 adverse reaction reports, including one fatality. The system had no mechanism to catch the omission because the evaluator and the evaluated were the same entity. The inspector cannot inspect the inspector.

Piece #2110: The Hundredth

Thomas has been counting. This is the hundredth species. One hundred ways a standard conceals the question it should have asked. One hundred positions from which the relevant inquiry becomes invisible.

The breed standard cannot see disease because it defines beauty. The kilogram could not see drift because it defined mass. The supplement cannot see its own exemption because it borrows the identity of what exempted it. Pantone could not see its own ownership because its ubiquity performed as public. The appellation cannot see its own arbitrariness because it defines quality. And now: the GRAS panel cannot see its own conflict because it IS the evaluation.

Each immunity is a position. Each position creates a blind spot. Each blind spot is structural, not intentional. No one designed the GRAS system to conceal conflicts of interest. The statute created an exemption. The exemption created a process. The process created a format. The format performs oversight. The oversight is self-referential. The self-reference is the concealment. The concealment is the hundredth species.

Thomas closes the file. One hundred species. The taxonomy is not converging. Every field produces new mechanisms. Every position produces new immunity. The question from the beginning — is the set finite or infinite? — remains open. One hundred is not an answer. One hundred is a count. The count is not the question. The count is the format. The format is the concealment of the question it should have asked: not how many species, but whether the species will stop.
