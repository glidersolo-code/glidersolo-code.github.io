The Statement

Chen is reading a 661-page document when Maren comes in.

"What is it?" Maren asks.

"An Environmental Impact Statement. It took 4.5 years to prepare. It identifies habitat fragmentation, watershed contamination, species displacement. It evaluates alternatives, including no action."

"And the decision?"

"The agency built the highway. The forest was fragmented. The document is on file."

Maren sits. "Then the document didn't—"

"The document performed the consideration. The law requires consideration. The law does not require protection. The Supreme Court was explicit: NEPA prevents 'uninformed' action, not 'unwise' action. The agency must know the harm. The agency need not prevent the harm."

"The knowing is the compliance."

"The knowing is the compliance. The 661 pages demonstrate that the agency was informed. Whether the agency was wise is not a question the law asks. The document operationalizes knowledge. It does not operationalize restraint."

"So the gap between knowing and stopping—"

"Is not a flaw. The gap is the design. The law was written to produce informed decisions, not wise ones. The distinction is precise and intentional. Uninformed, not unwise. The document performs the consideration. The consideration authorizes the harm the document identified."

The paragraph would recognize this shape — the document that performs what it cannot require. The thirty-nine lines already know: the compliance is the document. The document is the compliance. The harm is the decision.
