Fiction The Engagement Survey

Fiction

Part CDXLIII: The Survey

Piece #2656: The Twelve

Thomas receives the annual engagement survey. Twelve questions. Five-point scale. "I know what is expected of me at work." Thomas considers: expectations shift weekly. His manager reorganized priorities three times this quarter. Thomas selects 3.

"Someone at work seems to care about me as a person." Thomas's manager asked about his weekend once, in January. Thomas selects 3.

"I have a best friend at work." Thomas has colleagues he respects. The word "best friend" does not describe any of these relationships. Thomas selects 2.

Thomas submits the survey. His responses will be aggregated, anonymized, and reported as a team engagement score. The score will be Thomas's disposition, not Thomas's conditions.

Piece #2657: The Percentage

Thomas reads the Gallup report. Twenty-three percent of workers globally are "engaged." Sixty-two percent are "not engaged." Fifteen percent are "actively disengaged." Thomas is in the 62%.

Thomas considers what the 62% means. It does not mean 62% of workers dislike their jobs. It means 62% of workers answered Gallup's twelve questions in a way that falls below Gallup's proprietary cutoffs. The cutoffs are not published. The methodology by which a score of 3.4 becomes "not engaged" while 3.8 becomes "engaged" is not available for independent verification.

The number 62% travels without its denominator. It appears in boardrooms, in keynote presentations, in consulting proposals. It performs a crisis. The crisis has a solution. The solution is Gallup.

Piece #2658: The Action Plan

Thomas's manager receives the team engagement report. The team scored 3.2 out of 5. The manager is asked to create an action plan.

The action plan addresses recognition frequency, team meetings, feedback conversations, and career development discussions. The action plan does not address: the hiring freeze that left the team understaffed for nine months, the pay freeze now in its second year, the schedule changes that disrupted childcare arrangements for three team members, or the promotion criteria that have not been updated since 2019.

The twelve questions did not ask about these things. The report does not contain them. The action plan cannot address what the instrument did not measure. The manager schedules a team lunch. The team lunch is the action plan.

Piece #2659: The Cycle

Thomas takes the survey again the following year. The team lunch happened. The recognition emails increased. The manager said "good job" more frequently. Thomas's answers do not change.

Thomas's conditions did not change. His pay did not change. The staffing did not change. The schedule did not stabilize. The manager's communication improved. The communication was the intervention. The conditions were the problem. The survey measured the communication. The survey did not measure the conditions. The cycle will repeat.

Survey. Report. Action plan. Survey. The rhythm of asking performs the rhythm of responding. The organization listened. The listening was the response.

Piece #2660: The Trillion

Thomas reads the headline: "$8.8 trillion lost to disengagement." Thomas divides. $8.8 trillion across the global workforce, across 1,800 working hours per year. The number implies that if Thomas were "engaged" — if Thomas selected 4 instead of 3 on twelve questions — the economy would recover its share of $8.8 trillion.

Thomas's share of $8.8 trillion would buy a raise. The raise is not in the action plan. The action plan has a team lunch. Thomas selects 3. The 3 enters the system. The system produces a number. The number produces a crisis. The crisis produces a consulting engagement. The consulting engagement produces an action plan. The action plan produces a team lunch. The team lunch does not produce a raise. The survey returns. Thomas selects 3.
