Vignette Chen The Biodegradable

Vignette

"It says biodegradable," Maren said, holding up the bag.

"Under what conditions?" Chen asked.

"It doesn't say."

"That's the mechanism. The word describes a relationship — between a material and an environment — but it's printed as a property of the material alone. Like printing 'flammable' on a match and expecting it to ignite underwater."

Maren turned the bag over. No conditions listed. No asterisk. No fine print. Just the word, in green, above a leaf.

"What conditions does it need?" Maren asked.

"Industrial composting. Sixty degrees Celsius, sustained. Controlled moisture. Aerobic oxygen. Active microbial communities. Ninety days minimum."

"And how many people have access to that?"

"About twelve percent of American communities. For the other eighty-eight percent, the bag enters a landfill. Landfills are anaerobic — no oxygen, no moisture, no microbial activity. They preserve rather than decompose. An archaeologist found readable newspapers from 1987 in a landfill. Identifiable lettuce from the same era."

"So the bag just sits there."

"Three years later, a study found the bags could still carry groceries. In soil. In seawater. Intact."

Maren set the bag down. "Then what's the difference between this and a regular plastic bag?"

"In a landfill? Almost nothing. In an industrial composting facility? Everything. The word is true in one place and false in seven others. The label doesn't tell you which place you need."

"There's also the fragments," Chen continued. "Oxo-degradable plastics — a variant — contain metal salts that accelerate fragmentation. The material breaks into pieces. It doesn't break down. The pieces are microplastics. Too small to see. Too dispersed to collect. The EU banned them in 2019."

"The word 'degradable' was true."

"The material degraded. Into smaller pieces of the same material. Degradation performed as decomposition. Fragmentation performed as dissolution."

Maren picked up the bag again. "So the word is a conditional statement printed without its conditions."

"Exactly. 'This bag will biodegrade IF disposed of in an industrial composting facility at sixty degrees with controlled moisture and aerobic conditions.' The label compresses that sentence into one word. The compression removes the conditions. The removal transforms a relationship into a trait. Every conditional property, printed without its conditions, becomes an unconditional promise."

"And the consumer buys the promise."

"The consumer buys the word. The word says what the material IS. The conditions determine what the material DOES. The gap between being and doing is an entire infrastructure — the facility, the temperature, the moisture, the turning, the monitoring — that most consumers will never have access to, and that the label will never mention."
