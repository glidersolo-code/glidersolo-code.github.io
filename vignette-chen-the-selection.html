The Selection

Chen gave Maren three numbers. "2, 4, 6. They fit a rule. Propose triples and I'll tell you whether they fit."

"10, 12, 14?"

"Fits."

"30, 32, 34?"

"Fits."

"The rule is ascending by two."

Chen shook his head. "Wrong. The rule is any ascending sequence. 1, 5, 900 fits. 3, 7, 25 fits."

"But every triple I tested confirmed—"

"That's the point. Every triple you tested confirmed because you only tested triples consistent with your hypothesis. You never tested a disconfirming triple. You never asked: what if it's just ascending? Peter Wason, 1960. Only 20% of participants found the rule. The other 80% announced a rule that was too specific — because they tested only what they expected."

"I searched for confirming evidence."

"You searched for confirming evidence because the hypothesis generated the search. The hypothesis 'ascending by two' generates test triples like 8, 10, 12. It does not generate 1, 5, 900. The hypothesis shapes the inquiry. The inquiry produces confirmations. The confirmations strengthen the hypothesis. The loop closes."

Maren set down her pen. "So this is the second reader architecture."

"Anchoring captures the magnitude — the first number determines the estimate. Confirmation bias captures the direction — the prior belief determines which evidence is sought, weighted, remembered. Together they describe a reader who starts at the wrong number and then selects evidence confirming it."

"And the reader doesn't know."

"The reader doesn't know. Lord, Ross, and Lepper showed this in 1979. Two groups — one supporting capital punishment, one opposing — read the same mixed evidence. Both groups concluded the evidence supported their prior position. Both scrutinized the methodology when the results contradicted them, accepted the methodology when the results confirmed. Same data. Same study. Different evaluations. Not because they differed in intelligence. Because they differed in prior belief."

"The evidence polarized them."

"The evidence was supposed to moderate. It entrenched. Each group found in the same data what they had already believed. The evidence became a mirror. The reader saw the reader."

Chen drew the five levels. "Inside the number. Between numbers. Across the dataset. In the feedback loop. In the reader. Each level has its own class of concealment. Each is invisible from the level below. And at every level: the format holds. The number arrives, the reader reads, and what is hidden stays hidden — because the concealment is in the architecture, not in the content."

Maren stared at the five levels. "And the reader, at the top—"

"The reader is the final architecture. The reader who is anchored and confirming and calling it judgment. The reader who cannot see the reader."
