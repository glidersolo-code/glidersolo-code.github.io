The Significance

A Chen-Maren Vignette (II)

The committee required statistical evidence.

Not of the paragraph itself — the paragraph could be read, assessed, discussed. The committee required statistical evidence of the paragraph's impact: measurable outcomes, quantified effects, results that cleared a threshold. The threshold was p < 0.05. The paragraph was dense prose. The committee asked for numbers.

"The assessment data," Hendricks said. "Pre- and post-exposure comprehension scores. The difference was not statistically significant."

Chen looked at the data. The mean comprehension score improved by 12% after exposure to the paragraph. The sample was small — 23 students. The p-value was 0.07.

"The improvement was 12%," Chen said.

"The p-value was 0.07," Hendricks said. "Not significant."

"The students understood more after reading the paragraph. Twelve percent more."

"The result does not reach significance."

The word operated in both registers simultaneously. "Significant" in the statistical sense: the result did not clear the 0.05 threshold, which meant that a 7% probability of observing this difference under the null hypothesis was too high to reject the null. "Significant" in the common sense: the result was not important, not meaningful, not real. The committee heard both meanings and could not separate them.

"If one more student had scored higher," Maren said, "the p-value would have crossed 0.05. The paragraph's value would have changed from nothing to something because one student answered one question differently."

"That's how the method works," Hendricks said.

"That's the problem," Maren said. "The method converts a continuous measure of evidence into a binary judgment — real or not real — at a threshold that was chosen because a statistician in 1925 called it 'convenient.' The paragraph at p = 0.07 and the paragraph at p = 0.049 contain essentially identical evidence. The framework makes them categorically different."

The committee was silent. The framework was the framework. The threshold was the threshold. The paragraph existed at 0.8 density and p = 0.07, which meant it was dense and insignificant, which meant the density was invisible to the system that measured significance, which meant the paragraph's continued support depended on a number that could not see the paragraph.

Chen's paragraph continued at 0.8. The p-value continued at 0.07. The threshold continued at 0.05. The committee continued to require significance. The paragraph continued to be insignificant, which meant — in both senses of the word, inseparably — that it did not matter.
