Chen-Maren: The Signal

The internet is slow. Maren is on a video call in the kitchen that keeps freezing. Chen is in the study trying to diagnose the problem.

"The router says Wi-Fi 6," he calls to her. "It's supposed to be fast."

"It's not fast."

He opens the admin panel on his laptop. "We're getting 94 megabits per second. The box said AX3000."

"What's AX3000?"

"It means — " He stops. "It means up to 3,000 megabits per second combined across both frequency bands. But you can only connect to one band at a time, and 'up to' means under ideal conditions, and ideal conditions means you're standing next to the router with no walls and no other devices."

"So 3,000 means 94."

"3,000 means a theoretical maximum that requires conditions that don't exist in a house with walls."

Maren's video call reconnects. She mutes herself for a moment. "What does Wi-Fi even stand for?"

"Nothing. It's a brand name. It was chosen because it sounds like Hi-Fi. Someone on the founding board got nervous about a meaningless name, so they added the tagline 'The Standard for Wireless Fidelity.' Then the marketing people came in and removed it. But by then everyone already believed it meant Wireless Fidelity."

"So it doesn't mean anything."

"Correct."

"And the number 6 doesn't mean fast."

"The number 6 means it uses the 802.11ax standard instead of the 802.11ac standard. The letters are sequential IEEE amendments, but they skip letters because most amendments address things consumers never see — security, roaming, mesh. The letters consumers care about — b, g, n, ac, ax — are scattered among twenty others."

"Chen."

"Yes."

"I just need the video call to not freeze."

He looks at the router, its small LED blinking green, its label promising a speed neither of them will ever see. "Move closer to the router," he says.
