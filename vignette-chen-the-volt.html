The Volt

Maren was holding two cordless drills, one in each hand, reading the boxes.

"This one says 20V MAX," she said. "This one says 18V."

"Same voltage class," Chen said without looking up from her workbench.

"I know that. Five lithium-ion cells. 4.0 volts per cell fully charged, 3.6 nominal. The 20V is the peak. The 18V is the working voltage."

"Then why are you holding them?"

"Because I'm interested in the moment the 20 becomes 18." Maren set the DeWalt down and pulled the trigger with no bit installed. The chuck spun freely. "Right now the battery is fresh off the charger. The voltmeter would read close to 20. The instant I pull the trigger and current flows through the motor —"

"It drops."

"Under light load, 19. Under moderate load, 18. Under heavy load, less. The 20 volts exists before the trigger is pulled. Every pull takes it further from the number on the box."

Chen looked up. "The paragraph."

"At zero point eight."

"The paragraph describes something at rest. Chen at rest. The moment the story continues — the moment any load is applied —"

"The voltage drops." Maren set the second drill down. "The label can print the peak because the peak is the most flattering instant. Before the trigger. Before the load. Before the work."

"And once you've printed 20, you can't print 18 without looking weaker."

"The shelf enforces the inflation. Not the physics."

Chen smiled. "Milwaukee still prints 18."

"Milwaukee trusts the consumer to understand nominal voltage."

"Or Milwaukee trusts its brand enough not to need the extra 2 volts."

Maren picked up both boxes again. "The qualifier — 'MAX' — technically discloses everything. But the consumer reads the integer. The integer is the comparison. The qualifier is the fine print."

"The qualifier modifies. The integer decides."

"The integer decides." Maren put the 20V MAX back on the shelf. "Like the paragraph. Thirty-nine lines that modify. But Chen decided before the paragraph was written."
