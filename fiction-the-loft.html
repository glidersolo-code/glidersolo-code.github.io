Part CCXXXIV: The Loft

Piece #1611: The Power

Thomas shops for a down jacket. The tag says 800 fill power. The competitor says 650. He buys the 800 because higher fill power means warmer jacket. But fill power measures loft — the volume of space one ounce of down occupies in a standardized cylinder test. Eight hundred cubic inches per ounce. The down is fluffier, traps more air per gram, insulates more efficiently per unit weight. Fill power describes the quality of the down. It does not describe the quantity. A jacket with 800-fill down and 3 ounces of fill is less warm than a jacket with 650-fill down and 6 ounces. The consumer compared the fill power. The warmth depends on fill power multiplied by fill weight. The multiplication was never performed because one factor was on the tag and the other was in the specification sheet.

Piece #1612: The Test

IATA (International Down and Feather Testing Laboratory) and IDFL test fill power using a standardized cylinder: a known weight of down is placed in a graduated cylinder, a standardized weight is placed on top, and the volume the down occupies is measured. The test is sensitive to moisture, humidity, and the down's conditioning before measurement. The same sample tested at different laboratories may yield different numbers. The test measures loft under compression — not insulation in a garment, where baffles, shell fabric, and body heat distribution affect thermal performance. The number describes the down in a cylinder. The consumer wears the down in a jacket.

Piece #1613: The Weight

Fill weight — the total ounces of down in the garment — is the other half of the warmth equation. An ultralight jacket designed for packability may use 800-fill down at 2.5 ounces: warm for its weight, but not warm enough for severe cold. A mountaineering parka may use 650-fill down at 10 ounces: heavier, bulkier, far warmer. The ultralight tag says 800 fill power. The mountaineering tag says 650. The consumer reads the tags and reaches the wrong conclusion about which jacket is warmer, because the number that is present (fill power) describes efficiency, and the number that is absent (fill weight) determines total insulation.

Piece #1614: The Blend

The tag says "Down Fill: 80/20." Eighty percent down clusters, 20 percent feathers. Down clusters trap air; feathers do not insulate as effectively. An 800-fill rating applies to the down portion only, not the blend. The effective insulation of the fill includes the feathers, which dilute the loft. Higher down percentages (90/10, 95/5) mean more of the fill actually insulates at the rated efficiency. The consumer sees 800 fill power and 80/20 as two separate specifications. They are interdependent: the 800 describes the 80 percent, not the 100 percent.

Piece #1615: The Wet

Down's insulating property depends on loft, and loft collapses when wet. Water displaces the air trapped between down filaments. An 800-fill jacket soaked with rain or sweat insulates approximately as well as no jacket at all. Hydrophobic treatments (DownTek, Nikwax Hydrophobic Down) restore some wet-loft but not all. Synthetic insulations (PrimaLoft, Climashield) retain warmth when wet — a critical property the fill-power number cannot encode because fill power assumes dry down. The number describes performance under the condition the consumer hopes to maintain, not the condition the consumer will inevitably encounter.
