Part CDXCVI: The Score

Piece #2921: The Variables

Thomas reads about the Allegheny Family Screening Tool. Eight hundred variables. He reads the sources: welfare benefits, Medicaid claims, behavioral health records, criminal justice involvement, juvenile probation, prior child welfare referrals. All from county administrative databases. All from public systems. Thomas reads the breakdown: twenty-five percent of the variables directly measure poverty. Another twenty-five percent measure prior contact with child welfare and juvenile justice — contact with the system itself. Thomas does the arithmetic. Half the variables measure being poor or being previously investigated. The other half measure using public services. The model's input is not danger. The model's input is visibility.

Piece #2922: The Denominator

Thomas reads Kim, Wildeman, and colleagues, 2017. American Journal of Public Health. Thirty-seven point four percent of all American children experience a child protective services investigation by age 18. Thomas reads the number again. Not one in ten. Not one in five. More than one in three. For Black children: 53%. More than half.

Thomas thinks about what the algorithm screens into. Not a targeted intervention for rare cases. A mass surveillance system that contacts a third of all children and more than half of Black children before they become adults. The score is a filter. The filter is applied to a pipeline that is already full. The model decides which families to investigate. The pipeline ensures there will always be families to investigate. The denominator is not danger. The denominator is poverty.

Piece #2923: The Loop

Thomas reads about the feedback structure. A family is referred to CPS. The AFST generates a score. The score is above 17. The referral is mandated for investigation. The investigation generates a case file. The case file enters the county's administrative data. The administrative data feeds the model's training set.

The next time the family is referred — by a teacher, a neighbor, a mandated reporter — the model sees the prior investigation. The prior investigation raises the score. The higher score mandates a new investigation. The new investigation generates a new case file. The new case file enters the training data.

Thomas draws the loop on paper. Prediction → investigation → case file → training data → higher prediction. The model's prediction creates the evidence that validates the model. The family that was investigated because of the score becomes, in the training data, the evidence that the score was right. Thomas reads about self-fulfilling prophecy. He reads about Merton. He reads about Goodhart's Law. This is both. The prediction creates the outcome it predicted. The measure became the target. But the family is not a target. The family is a loop.

Piece #2924: The Blind Spot

Thomas reads about what the model cannot see. A family with private insurance. A family whose therapy happens in a private office that generates no county record. A family whose substance use is treated at a private clinic. A family whose domestic instability is managed by a private attorney. A family that experiences the same depression, the same stress, the same risk — but has the resources to address it outside the reach of county databases.

The model does not score these families lower because they are safer. The model scores them lower because they are invisible. The eight hundred variables require data. The data requires system contact. System contact requires poverty — or something close enough to poverty that the family enters the public system. The algorithm learned that public system contact predicts future system contact. It learned this correctly. It did not learn why.

Piece #2925: The Abandonment

Thomas reads the timeline. Oregon adopts a version in 2018. Drops it in June 2022 for racial bias. Illinois deploys Eckerd's Rapid Safety Feedback system. Abandons it in 2017 — the director says it was "not predicting much." More than 4,100 children had been assigned a 90% or greater probability of death or injury. New Zealand develops a version in 2013. The government scraps the proposed study in 2015.

Thomas reads what remains after the tools are abandoned. The investigations they triggered. The case files they generated. The foster placements they informed. The families they separated. The data they created, still sitting in administrative databases, still available to train the next model. The tool is abandoned. The damage is archived. The archive is the training data for the tool that replaces it.
