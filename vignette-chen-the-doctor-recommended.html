Vignette Chen The Doctor Recommended

Vignette

"Number one doctor recommended," Maren read from the box of pain reliever.

"How do they know?" Chen asked.

"Doctors were surveyed. This brand came out on top."

"Who designed the survey?"

"The... the company that makes the product?"

"The brand commissions a marketing research firm. The firm contacts physicians. The question: 'Which brand of acetaminophen do you recommend most often?' Notice: the question asks for a brand. Not a molecule. A doctor who says 'I recommend acetaminophen — any brand, generic is fine' cannot express this within the survey's format."

"So the question forces a brand answer."

"The question forces a brand answer. Then the results: suppose 312 physicians respond. 87 name this brand. 63 name the competitor. The rest scatter among generics and alternatives. 87 out of 312. Twenty-eight percent. The label says '#1.' The survey says 28%. The other 72% recommended something else."

"But '#1' sounds like consensus."

"Plurality performs as consensus. 28% performs as majority. The '#1' is a rank, not a proportion. It says: more doctors named this brand than any single other brand. It does not say: most doctors prefer this brand. The difference is the difference between being the tallest person in a room of ten and being taller than everyone else combined."

"And the generic—"

"The generic is the same molecule. Same dose. Same manufacturer, in many cases — the name brand and the store brand produced on the same line. The molecule is the medicine. The brand is the marketing. The survey asks about the marketing. The label reports the answer as though it were about the medicine."

Maren looked at the box. "The doctor's voice is on the box."

"The doctor's checkbox is on the box. The checkbox became a count. The count became a rank. The rank became a label. The label became a voice. The consumer hears a doctor recommending this product. The doctor checked a box in a survey designed by the brand's marketing agency. Five transformations between the checkbox and the voice. Each invisible."

"Five transformations."

"And at the end: trust. The consumer trusts the doctor's recommendation. The recommendation is a survey result. The survey was an instrument designed to produce the result. The doctor's authority — years of training, clinical judgment, pharmacological knowledge — is the fuel. The survey is the engine. The label is the destination."
