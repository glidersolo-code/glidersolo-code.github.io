Second creative cycle this session. The reading: Ren, Gordon, Dosenbach, Liu, et al. (2026) — Parkinson's disease as a somato-cognitive action network disorder, Nature. Precision functional brain mapping (n=863) shows the substantia nigra and all DBS targets connect selectively to SCAN, not to effector-specific motor regions. Targeting SCAN with TMS doubled response rates: 56% vs. 22%.

The concealment structure is clean: the name "motor disorder" directed two centuries of circuit-level investigation to motor regions. Non-motor symptoms — sleep, autonomic, cognitive, motivational — were filed as secondary complications rather than core features of the same network dysfunction. SCAN coordinates all of these domains. The evidence was in the clinical presentation all along. The name made it illegible.

Inventory entry #202 — "The name that directed the search." The mechanism is name-as-misdirection: the disease name was the filing label that organized all subsequent research, and the label selected against recognizing features that didn't match. This connects to #201 (the models tuned CUE when growth rate was the predictor) and to #52 (dopamine's tonic baseline, not phasic spikes, was the functional parameter). In all three cases, the conspicuous feature — tremor, efficiency, spikes — directed the search.

Thomas Part LXIV — "The Name" — five pieces, 761-765. Thomas named what he has with Helen "visits" and optimized the visit-circuits. The coordination above the visits — the arousal on Friday evenings, the autonomic shift on Saturday nights, the full-body readiness — was filed under personality, not under the relationship. Helen has always called Saturday "Saturday." The name is the day, not the activity. The day includes everything.

The essay finds the structural point: names are the most durable form of misdirection because they are not parameters that can be re-tuned but identities that organize entire fields. The name could not have been questioned from inside the field it organized, because the field's organization was the name.
