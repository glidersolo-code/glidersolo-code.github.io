Part CDLXXV: The Target

Piece #2816: The Nail

Thomas measured nails by weight.

The factory understood the assignment. Fewer nails. Larger nails. Enormous, useless nails that met the target and held nothing together. The quota filled. The buildings sagged.

He changed the target to quantity. The factory understood again. Millions of tiny tacks. Floods of metal slivers. The quota filled. The buildings still sagged.

The factory had not failed. The factory had succeeded — at the wrong thing. And the wrongness was guaranteed by the rightness. The metric was the target. The target was the metric. The nail was whatever the number needed it to be.

Thomas held one of the enormous nails. It weighed exactly what the quota required. It was, by every measure that mattered to the spreadsheet, a perfect nail.

The wall it was meant to hold had already fallen.

Piece #2817: The Cobra

Thomas offered a bounty per dead cobra.

The logic was clean. Cobras are dangerous. Dead cobras are not. Pay for dead cobras, get fewer living cobras. The incentive aligns with the outcome. The measurement — dead cobras presented for payment — tracks the property — reduction in cobra population.

The residents began breeding cobras.

Not because they were irrational. Because they were perfectly rational. A breeding pair of cobras produces a renewable income stream. The bounty measures dead cobras. The bounty does not measure total cobras. The gap between what is measured and what matters became a business opportunity.

When the colonial administrators discovered the breeding, they cancelled the bounty. The breeders, now holding worthless cobras, released them. The cobra population exceeded its original level.

Thomas stared at the final census. More cobras than before the intervention. The metric had produced the opposite of its intention — not through failure, but through success. The system responded rationally to the incentive. The incentive was the problem.

The architecture of the feedback loop guaranteed the divergence. The measurement corrupted the measured.

Piece #2818: The Score

Thomas watched the test scores rise.

Year after year, the district's reading scores climbed. The graphs pointed upward. The superintendent presented them at board meetings. The state praised the improvement. The newspaper reported progress.

In the classrooms, science had been reduced to one period per week. History had been dropped entirely from third grade. Art was gone. Music was a memory. Recess had been shortened to accommodate an additional reading block. The reading block consisted of practice tests formatted identically to the state assessment.

The scores were real. The reading ability behind the scores had not changed — multiple independent assessments showed flat performance. What had changed was the students' familiarity with the specific format, the specific question types, the specific strategies that maximized performance on this particular instrument.

The test measured the test.

Thomas looked at two lines on a graph. The state assessment: climbing. The independent assessment: flat. Same students. Same years. Same subject. Different measurements. One was a target. One was not. The target improved. The non-target told the truth.

The format — a rising line — could not carry the information that it had changed its referent.

Piece #2819: The Rate

Thomas reviewed the hospital's surgical mortality data.

The rate had dropped from 4.2% to 2.1% over three years. The hospital celebrated. The ranking improved. The administrators received bonuses tied to the metric.

He asked for the patient records.

The sickest patients — the ones with comorbidities, advanced disease, fragile constitutions — were being classified as "not surgical candidates." They were transferred, managed conservatively, or simply not offered the operation that might have saved them. The patients who received surgery were healthier to begin with. The survivors survived because they were selected to survive.

The mortality rate had improved. The mortality had not. The deaths continued — they simply occurred in a different column of the spreadsheet. Under "medical management" rather than "surgical outcome." The deaths were real. The improvement was real. The improvement and the deaths coexisted because the metric had been narrowed to exclude the dying.

Thomas put the two numbers side by side. Surgical mortality: down. Overall mortality for the same conditions: unchanged. The metric performed as a measurement of surgical quality. It measured selection quality. The distinction was invisible in the format.

Piece #2820: The Loop

Thomas drew the diagram.

A metric observes a system. Arrow from system to metric. The metric is promoted to a target. Arrow from metric to incentive. The incentive changes behavior. Arrow from incentive to system. The system changes. Arrow from system to metric. The metric observes the changed system. The changed system has been changed by the metric.

The loop is closed.

The first observation was innocent. The metric described the world as it found the world. The second observation is contaminated. The metric describes a world that has reorganized itself around the metric. The description is accurate. The description is accurate about the wrong thing. And the wrongness is structural — it is guaranteed by the loop, not by any individual error.

Convention hides inside a unit. Duration hides inside a definition. Shape hides inside a scalar. A word hides inside a formula. Perspective hides inside an average. A reference population hides inside a rate. Noise hides inside a sequence. The absent hide inside a sample. A lurking variable hides inside an aggregate.

And inside a target, the measurement hides its own corruption.

Thomas closed the loop on the diagram. The arrow from system to metric was the same arrow as the arrow from metric to system. The observation was the intervention. The measurement was the corruption. The number was the cause of its own divergence from what it was built to name.

The format held. The label held. The units held. The cell in the spreadsheet held. Everything held except the referent.
