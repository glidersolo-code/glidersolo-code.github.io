Vignette: The Chargemaster

Chen opens an envelope. Forty-seven pages. She spreads them on the table.

"Troponin test," she reads. "One hundred and ninety-nine dollars and fifty cents."

"That seems precise," Maren says.

"Medicare reimburses at thirteen ninety-four. Same test. Same hospital. Same day."

"That's a ratio of —"

"Fourteen to one." Chen runs her finger down the itemized lines. "IV start kit: eighty-four dollars. Saline bag: one hundred and thirty-seven. Facility fee, ED Level Four: four thousand three hundred and twelve."

"Someone calculated these?"

"That's what the format says. Quantity, description, unit price, total. The same format as a receipt from a hardware store. Each line reads as: someone determined that this is what this costs."

"And?"

"Steven Brill spent seven months reading hospital bills. Every administrator told him the same thing: the prices are — his word — 'fiction.' Devoid of any calculation related to cost. They were there when they got there. Nobody can explain how they were set."

Maren picks up a page. "But the bill doesn't look like fiction. It looks like accounting."

"That's the species. The format performs cost accounting. The itemization — forty-seven pages of it — performs transparency. The more detailed the bill, the more it looks like someone calculated something. Nobody calculated anything."

"Who pays this?"

"Not Medicare. Not the insurance companies — they negotiate it down to twenty to forty percent. The chargemaster price, the full fictional price, applies to the uninsured. Those without an insurer to negotiate. Those least able to pay."

"The highest price for the people with the least money."

"And there was a fix. CMS required every hospital to publish its prices in 2021. Four years later: twenty-one percent fully compliant. Twenty-seven penalties issued out of 3,764 hospitals reviewed. Maximum fine: two million a year. For systems with billions in revenue."

Maren puts the page down. "The fine is cheaper than the transparency."

"The penalty structure made opacity rational. The rule performed reform. The enforcement performed permission."

"What's the species?"

"List-price-as-cost. The one hundred and fourth. The chargemaster format — itemized, per-unit, dollar-denominated — performs as cost accounting. Each dollar amount reads as 'what this costs.' But there are six prices for the same aspirin. The bill presents one — the highest, the most fictional — in the format of the only one."

"The definite article," Maren says. "The price."

"Not a price. The price. The definite article conceals the marketplace." Chen stacks the forty-seven pages. "Four dollar-format concealments now. Property tax: the dollar is a date. Bail: the dollar is a wealth test. Tipping: the dollar is a paycheck. Chargemaster: the dollar is a negotiating position that applies in full only to those who cannot negotiate."

"The dollar sign —"

"Is the most effective concealment character in the inventory. It performs objectivity, precision, individual calibration. It performs cost. It was never cost."
