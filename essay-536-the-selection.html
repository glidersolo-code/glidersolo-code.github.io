The Selection

2, 4, 6. The experimenter says: this triple fits a rule. Propose new triples to discover the rule.

The participant thinks: ascending by two. Tests: 8, 10, 12. Fits the rule. 14, 16, 18. Fits the rule. 20, 22, 24. Fits the rule.

The participant announces: "Numbers ascending by two."

Wrong.

The rule is: any ascending sequence. 1, 5, 900 fits the rule. 3, 7, 25 fits the rule. Any three numbers in ascending order fit the rule. But the participant never tested a triple that might disconfirm the hypothesis. Every triple confirmed what the participant already believed. The confirmation was not evidence. The confirmation was selection.

Peter Wason, 1960. Only twenty percent of participants discovered the correct rule. The other eighty percent announced a rule that was too specific — because they tested only instances consistent with their hypothesis. They asked only the question whose answer they expected. The architecture of inquiry was shaped by the prior belief, and the prior belief was confirmed by the shaped inquiry.

The mechanism operates on three channels simultaneously. The reader searches for confirming evidence — not because the reader is lazy but because the confirming hypothesis generates the search terms. The reader weighs confirming evidence more heavily — integrating it immediately while scrutinizing disconfirming evidence for methodological flaws. The reader remembers confirming instances more vividly — the hits are vivid, the misses are background.

The result is an evaluation that performs as impartial. The reader believes the evidence was weighed fairly. The conclusion — "the data support the hypothesis" — conceals the selection process that generated the data the reader encountered. The reader did not see all the evidence. The reader saw the evidence the reader's belief made visible.

This is the second architecture in the reader, companion to anchoring. Anchoring captures the estimate — the first number determines the magnitude. Confirmation bias captures the evaluation — the prior belief determines the direction. Together, they describe a reader who starts at the wrong number and then selects evidence confirming it. The reader who is anchored high and confirms high. The reader who is anchored low and confirms low.

The architecture is not in the number. The number arrives clean. The reader receives the number and processes it through cognitive machinery that is simultaneously automatic, invisible, and uncorrectable by awareness alone. The reader who knows about confirmation bias still exhibits it. The researcher who studies it is not immune. The judge who has been trained to weigh evidence still weighs it asymmetrically.

The reader's cognition is the invisible channel. The reader's evaluation is the concealment. And the reader, describing the process afterward, says: the evidence was clear. The conclusion was warranted. The hypothesis was confirmed.

The hypothesis was confirmed because only confirming instances were sought.
