Part CXXXVII: The Zone

Piece #1126: The Map

The map was not a record. It was an instruction.

Thomas looked at the zoning map of a city. Colors filled every parcel: yellow for single-family residential, orange for multifamily, red for commercial, purple for industrial. The colors were solid, the boundaries sharp, the categories mutually exclusive. Each parcel belonged to exactly one category. The categories appeared to describe what was there.

They described what was permitted.

The Standard State Zoning Enabling Act was drafted in 1922 by a committee at the Department of Commerce, chaired by Herbert Hoover — then Secretary of Commerce, later President. The act provided model legislation for states to grant municipalities the power to regulate land use. By 1926, when the Supreme Court upheld zoning in Village of Euclid v. Ambler Realty Co., forty-three states had adopted enabling acts based on the model.

The model separated uses. Residential from commercial. Commercial from industrial. Single-family from multifamily. The separation was presented as protection — keeping factories away from homes, preserving property values, ensuring orderly development. The protection was real. The protection was also a prescription, and the prescription created the conditions it appeared to protect.

Piece #1127: The Character

Seventy-five percent of residential land in American cities was zoned for single-family detached homes only.

In San Jose, ninety-four percent. In Los Angeles, seventy-five. In the Twin Cities, seventy-three, before Minneapolis became the first major city to eliminate single-family-only zoning in 2018.

The single-family zone excluded apartments. Excluded duplexes. Excluded the corner store, the accessory dwelling unit, the live-work space. The exclusion created a landscape: quiet streets, uniform building stock, consistent setbacks, predictable property values. The landscape was then described as "neighborhood character."

Thomas saw the loop. The zoning created the character. The character justified the zoning. When reform was proposed — allow a duplex, permit a small apartment building — the objection was that the change would alter the neighborhood's character. The character that the zoning had created. The character that existed because the zoning prevented alternatives.

The loop was not hidden. Anyone who read the zoning code could see it. The concealment was not in the mechanism but in the grammar: "this is a single-family neighborhood" instead of "this is zoned as a single-family neighborhood." The is/ought distinction collapsed in the map. The map said what was permitted. The city read what was described.

Piece #1128: The Proxy

The categories were not neutral.

Explicitly racial zoning — zones designated by race — was struck down by the Supreme Court in Buchanan v. Warley (1917). Nine years later, Euclid v. Ambler upheld use-based zoning. The transition was from explicit to structural.

Single-family-only zones excluded multifamily housing. Multifamily exclusion excluded renters. Renter exclusion correlated with race and income. The correlation was not accidental — it was, in many cities, the purpose. The categories appeared neutral: "use," "density," "character," "compatibility." The categories operated as proxies for the classifications that had been struck down.

Thomas thought about the sentencing grid (#272). There, two axes captured what the system deemed relevant and excluded what it didn't. Here, the categories captured what the municipality deemed compatible and excluded what it didn't. In both cases, the excluded dimensions — the person, the community — were invisible because the included dimensions produced a precise output. The grid said 46-57 months. The map said R-1.

Piece #1129: The Reform

Minneapolis eliminated single-family-only zoning in 2018. Oregon followed statewide in 2019. California passed SB 9 in 2021.

Each reform treated the map as prescription. Each recognized that the categories were not natural but constructed. Each acknowledged that the construction had consequences — for housing supply, for affordability, for who could live where.

The resistance treated the map as description. The neighborhoods were single-family. The character was established. The change would alter what existed.

Both readings were correct. The map was prescriptive in function and descriptive in appearance. The reforms changed the prescription. The resistance defended the description. The argument was not about the map. The argument was about which reading of the map was primary.

Thomas noted that the reforms did not abolish zoning. They modified the categories. The map remained. The colors changed. The prescriptive function continued — now permitting duplexes and triplexes where only single-family homes had been allowed. The new prescription would create new conditions. The new conditions would become the new character. The new character would eventually be defended as the new description.

Piece #1130: The Zone

The map created what it measured.

Thomas counted the mechanisms. Convention-as-nature: the categories were experienced as natural distinctions. Regulatory-self-sealing: the zoning created the conditions that justified the zoning. Conceptual-reification: the categories on the map were treated as features of the landscape.

Three mechanisms, none new. But the combination was specific. The Dewey Decimal system (#270) organized knowledge; the shelf determined encounter. The zoning map organized space; the color determined habitation. The Dewey system encoded a hierarchy of knowledge from 1876. The zoning map encoded a hierarchy of uses from 1922. Both were experienced as neutral organization. Both were specific decisions by specific people at specific moments.

The map was not the territory. The map had become the territory. The territory could not become anything the map did not permit. The map looked like a description. The description was an instruction. The instruction had been followed for a century. The following looked like the world.

Thomas set down the map. The city continued as zoned.
