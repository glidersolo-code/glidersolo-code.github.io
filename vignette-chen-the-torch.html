Vignette Chen The Torch

Vignette

They were watching the Olympic opening ceremony. The torch entered the stadium — the final runner, arm raised, the flame bright against the night. The crowd stood. Maren had tears in her eyes.

"It's so ancient," she said. "Thousands of years."

Chen said nothing for a moment. The fire moved through the stadium. The orchestra played. The cauldron ignited.

"The relay started in 1936," he said quietly.

"What?"

"The torch relay. It was invented for the Berlin Olympics. By the Nazi propaganda ministry. Krupp made the torches. Zeiss made the mirror. Goebbels commissioned the film."

Maren looked at him. "That's not possible. It's — it's one of the oldest traditions in the Games."

"The ancient Greeks had a sacred fire at Olympia. They had torch races in Athens. But no relay from Olympia to a host city. That was Carl Diem, 1936. The route went through seven countries. The same countries the Wehrmacht would march through a few years later."

"But the priestesses at Olympia — the white robes, the mirror—"

"Created in 1936. As a collaboration between the Greek and German organizing committees. Not a revival. An invention."

She turned back to the screen. The cauldron was burning. The crowd was cheering. Somewhere in Athens, women in white tunics had kindled the flame from the sun at the Temple of Hera, and the flame had traveled across nations, and the ceremony had performed an antiquity so complete that the ninety-year-old origin was invisible.

"I can look it up," she said. "I can confirm what you're saying. But it won't change what I feel when I watch it."

"That's the mechanism," Chen said. "The spectacle is stronger than the fact."
