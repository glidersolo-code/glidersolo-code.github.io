The Dose

They were looking at a safety data sheet. A number beside the compound name. 200 mg/kg.

"LD50," Maren said. "Median lethal dose. Oral, rat."

"What does that mean?"

"The dose that kills half the rats."

"Thirty to a hundred inbred Wistar rats, a single strain maintained for genetic uniformity, exposed once by gavage under controlled conditions. The fifty is not a safety threshold. It is the point on the dose-response sigmoid where the curve is steepest — where the statistician's confidence interval is smallest. The number is optimized for measurement precision, not for the information the reader needs."

Chen's paragraph was about the fifty. Trevan proposed the concept in 1927, at the Wellcome Physiological Research Laboratories, to standardize pharmacological preparations — not to measure environmental health risk. The metric migrated from drug potency comparison to hazard classification. The Globally Harmonized System uses LD50 to assign hazard categories. The hazard categories determine labels. The labels determine shipping restrictions, workplace exposure limits, regulatory status.

"And the species —"

"Dioxin. LD50 in guinea pigs: 0.6 micrograms per kilogram. In hamsters: 1,157 micrograms per kilogram. Nearly two-thousand-fold difference. Same molecule. Two rodents. The safety data sheet says 'rat.' The reader infers 'me.' The FDA uses allometric scaling factors — body surface area adjustments — to estimate human equivalent doses. The factors assume metabolic scaling is predictable. For compounds metabolized by cytochrome P450 polymorphisms that differ between species, it is not."

"So the number describes the rat."

"The number describes the rat at the steepest point of the curve. The route matters — oral, dermal, inhalation, intravenous — the same compound can differ by a hundred-fold between routes. The time window matters — LD50 is acute, twenty-four hours; it says nothing about what happens over decades. Asbestos has a very high acute LD50. Lead has a moderate one. Both kill by accumulation the number cannot see."

The paragraph sat at 0.8. It was, like an LD50, a number that appeared to describe a property of the substance while describing a property of the measurement — which species, which strain, which route, which time window, which point on the curve — the fifty performing the authority of a safety threshold while being the point where the confidence interval was narrowest, the precision of the measurement concealing the imprecision of the inference.
