Reading: Parkinson's Disease as a SCAN Disorder

Paper: "Parkinson's disease as a somato-cognitive action network disorder" Authors: Jianxun Ren, Wei Zhang, Louisa Dahmani, Evan M. Gordon, Shenshen Li, and 31 additional researchers (36 total) Institutions: Changping Laboratory, Beijing; Peking University; Massachusetts General Hospital; Washington University School of Medicine, St. Louis; and others Journal: Nature, February 4, 2026 DOI: 10.1038/s41586-025-10059-1

The Finding

Parkinson's disease has been classified as a motor disorder since James Parkinson described the "shaking palsy" in 1817. The diagnostic criteria center on motor symptoms: tremor, rigidity, bradykinesia. The circuit-level understanding mapped Parkinson's to the basal ganglia and their connections to effector-specific motor regions — the hand area, foot area, face area of motor cortex. Loss of dopaminergic neurons in the substantia nigra disrupts these circuits. The disease was a motor circuit disorder.

But Parkinson's also produces non-motor symptoms — sleep disturbances, autonomic dysfunction, cognitive impairment, mood changes — that often predate the motor symptoms by a decade or more. These were filed as secondary: the motor circuit is the disease, the non-motor symptoms are complications.

Ren, Gordon, Dosenbach, Liu, and colleagues used precision brain imaging (n=863, multimodal, multi-intervention) to map which network the substantia nigra and all deep-brain stimulation targets actually connect to. The answer: not effector-specific motor regions. The somato-cognitive action network (SCAN) — a recently described network that coordinates arousal, organ physiology, whole-body motor plans, and behavioral motivation.

Abnormally high SCAN-to-subcortex connectivity characterizes Parkinson's. Effective treatments (medication, DBS, TMS, focused ultrasound) all reduce this hyperconnectivity. In a clinical trial: targeting SCAN with TMS produced 56% response rate (18 patients), vs. 22% when targeting nearby effector motor regions (18 patients). A 2.5-fold increase from targeting the correct network.

The Concealment

The disease was named after its most visible symptom. The circuit search was directed by the name. "Motor disorder" → motor circuits → effector-specific regions. But the actual dysfunction is in a network that does more than motor control — it coordinates action with motivation, arousal, and organ physiology. The non-motor symptoms aren't complications. They're core features of a SCAN disorder. The tremor was the filing label, not the disease.
