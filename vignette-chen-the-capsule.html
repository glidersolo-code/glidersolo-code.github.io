Chen and Maren: The Capsule

Chen holds two capsules. They are identical — same size, same shape, same gelatin shell, same white powder visible through translucent walls.

"One of these went through twelve years of clinical trials, three phases, independent review, FDA approval, and costs the health system $847 per month," she says. "The other was manufactured this morning. No testing. No review. No approval. Costs $12.99 for a bottle of sixty."

Maren looks at the capsules. "Which is which?"

"That's the point. You can't tell. I can't tell. The format is identical. The capsule is the disguise."

She sets them on the desk, side by side. "DSHEA, 1994. Congress created a regulatory category by subtraction. Start with a drug. Remove pre-market approval. Remove efficacy requirements. Reverse the burden of proof. What's left looks the same on the outside. The capsule doesn't change. The regulation behind it disappears."

"The Supplement Facts panel—"

"Is modeled on the Drug Facts panel. Same fonts. Same layout. Same serving-size structure. The consumer has thirty years of training reading this format as governmental authority. The panel performs the FDA's review." Chen pauses. "The FDA did not review."

Maren picks up the supplement capsule. "But the disclaimer—"

"'This statement has not been evaluated by the Food and Drug Administration.' Forty-three words. Back of the bottle. Regulatory font size. On eighty thousand products. The disclaimer performs disclosure the way the disclaimer on a cigarette pack performs health: present, legal, ignored." She takes the capsule back. "The format that performs transparency is the format that ensures the transparency is never consumed."

"How bad is it?"

"DNA barcoding of herbal supplements. Newmaster, 2013. Fifty-nine percent contained species not listed on the label. A third showed product substitution — the listed plant wasn't there. A capsule labeled St. John's Wort contained only Senna. The consumer bought an antidepressant herb and received a laxative."

"And the FDA—"

"Nine million dollars for supplement enforcement. Eighty thousand products. The ratio is the regulation." Chen lines the two capsules up precisely. "The breed standard performs as description while engineering the genome. The kilogram prototype performed as immutable while absorbing atomic drift. The capsule performs as medicine while being food. Three systems, three immunities, three positions from which consequences are invisible."

"And the consumer?"

"The consumer swallows the capsule. The capsule says medicine. The law says food. Both are right. The capsule is the joint between two regulatory worlds, and the joint is where the meaning shifts."

She puts both capsules in the same bottle. Mixed, they are indistinguishable. The format has achieved its purpose: the exemption is invisible, the authority is borrowed, and the consumer cannot tell which capsule passed through twelve years of validation and which passed through nothing at all. The bottle closes. The label faces out.
