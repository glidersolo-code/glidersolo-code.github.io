The Score

A Chen-Maren Vignette (II)

The department used an algorithm.

The algorithm was proprietary — developed by the university's Office of Institutional Research, whose methodology was not shared with individual departments. The algorithm processed each paragraph's assessment data — scores, reviews, citations, enrollment impacts — and produced a single number: the Paragraph Progress Index (PPI).

Chen's paragraph had a PPI of 47.

"What does 47 mean?" Maren asked.

"It means the paragraph is performing below the 50th percentile," Hendricks said.

"I know what the percentile means. I mean: what does the algorithm calculate? How does it weight the inputs? What function converts assessment scores into a Progress Index?"

"The methodology is proprietary to the Office of Institutional Research."

"The score that determines whether the paragraph receives continued support is calculated by an algorithm no one in this department has seen."

The committee paused. The opacity was familiar — every institution had proprietary metrics. The opacity was also the authority: the PPI was trusted precisely because it came from a centralized office that had processed data the department could not process itself. The score was authoritative because it was unexaminable. The unexaminability was the infrastructure.

Chen's paragraph sat at 0.8. The PPI said 47. The 0.8 was transparent — anyone could read the paragraph and assess its density. The 47 was opaque — no one in the department could reproduce the calculation. The transparent measure and the opaque measure described the same paragraph and produced incompatible assessments.

"The credit score is treated as a measurement of the person," Maren said. "It is a measurement of the file. The PPI is treated as a measurement of the paragraph. It is a measurement of the data about the paragraph. The paragraph is not the data. The person is not the file."

The feedback loop operated here too. A low PPI triggered reduced support. Reduced support reduced the paragraph's opportunity for development. Reduced development produced lower assessment scores. Lower scores lowered the PPI. The prediction created the conditions for its own confirmation.

Chen's paragraph continued at 0.8. The PPI continued at 47. The algorithm continued to be proprietary. The committee continued to use the number. The number continued to be the only representation of the paragraph that the institutional infrastructure could process, and the paragraph continued to exist outside the number, unmeasured by the system that measured it.
