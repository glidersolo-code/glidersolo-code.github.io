The Survey

Twelve questions. Five-point scale. Annual administration. "I know what is expected of me at work." "Someone at work seems to care about me as a person." "I have a best friend at work." The Gallup Q12 measures employee engagement. The organization administers the survey. The organization listens.

The listening is the product.

Gallup's 2023 State of the Global Workplace report found 23% of workers globally "engaged," 62% "not engaged," and 15% "actively disengaged." The cost of this disengagement: $8.8 trillion, or 9% of global GDP. The number is derived from proprietary cutoffs applied to proprietary data, scaled by a proprietary productivity discount, and reported with the precision of a macroeconomic indicator. No external auditor has verified the calculation. The precision performs the methodology.

The twelve questions measure how an employee feels about their manager and workplace relationships. They do not measure pay. They do not measure staffing levels. They do not measure schedule predictability. They do not measure workload. The things that drive disengagement — the structural conditions of employment — are not captured by the instrument that diagnoses disengagement.

This is the reframe. The survey asks about feelings. The results are reported as properties of the workforce: this employee is "engaged," that employee is "not engaged." The vocabulary assigns a disposition to the worker. The disposition was generated by conditions the survey did not measure. The action plan that follows the survey addresses the manager's communication and recognition practices. The staffing level does not change. The compensation does not change. The schedule does not stabilize.

Two-thirds of employees do not believe their organization acts on survey feedback. The cycle continues: survey, report, action plan, survey. The rhythm of asking performs the rhythm of responding. The administration of the instrument became the intervention the instrument was designed to trigger. The question was the answer.

Thompson named this disconnected capitalism: employers demand increasing engagement while financialization simultaneously forces cuts to the conditions that produce engagement. The Q12 cannot see this because its questions measure the interpersonal surface of the workplace, not the structural depth. The surface is warm or cold. The depth is never measured. The warmth of the surface is reported as the temperature of the whole.
